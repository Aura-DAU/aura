### COURSE PLACEMENT

This course is an Open Elective open to BTech 3rd and 4th year students and PG students in the areas
of HSS and Design

### PRE-REQUISITES

This course is for students who take a more than superficial interest in their society and culture.
This is ideally for students who have developed an interest in the themes and problems introduced in
the HSS  core courses.   This is also for students who enjoy reading, writing and expressing their
thoughts on issues that affect their lives not only as students of this Institute but as citizens of
this country and the world at large.

### COURSE OBJECTIVES

This course will examine the concept of “culture” as it bears upon the articulation of both group
and individual identities. We will try to understand what we mean by ‘identity’ – is it is a thing
to be possessed, an essence in the character of a community or people, or something more complex,
fluid and changing, or whether it exists at all. Finally how does the question of identity bear upon
political questions like justice, equality and freedom?  Through the course, these questions will be
posed with reference to specific historical examples.

### COURSE DESCRIPTION

Starting from a broad understanding of the meanings of culture, politics and identity, this course
intends to introduce students to larger social and historical contexts within which debates on
‘cultural identity’ took shape in the modern West and India.   From basic concepts and definitions,
students will be introduced to contemporary debates on the problem of identity from modernist and
post-modernist positions.

Are individual and collective identities primordial or are they historically constituted? Are
identities fixed or mutable? How do individuals define themselves and their identities through
active negotiations with political structures and cultural codes and values ? This preliminary
discussion will be followed by more specific case studies that would try to show how our individual
and collective identities are born out of collective struggles for freedom, equality, recognition
and justice on the one hand and out of specific projects of power on the other.

### COURSE STRUCTURE

Classes are organized in a lecture plus seminar format where both Instructor and students will be
engaged in discussion and debate based on readings and other course material given to them in
advance of each class. Apart from introducing students to such basic identity categories and
discourses such as nation, ethnicity, gender, race and caste the course will also explore specific
contexts within which these categories and discourses took shape and acquired resonance.

### LECTURE THEMES

This understanding will be generated through lectures based a selection of readings of seminal texts
by authors, activists, political thinkers from both the West and India who pioneered thinking around
nation, gender, race and caste identities.   Students begin with a selection of readings from an
anthology that provides a series of theoretical sources of contemporary thinking about identity,
including explorations of race, class, gender, and nationality.

Students are introduced to  the long and rich tradition of philosophical analysis and debate by
leading theorists such as W.E.B DuBois, Alain Locke, Simone de Beauvoir, Frantz Fanon, Stuart Hall,
Ranajit Guha, Eric Hobsbawm, Judith Butler and Edward Said. They  then go on to read selections from
the writings of Gandhi, Nehru, Ambedkar, Savarkar, Golwalkar and Deendayal Upadhayay among others.
This is meant to allow for a comparative study of identities through multiple theoretical
frameworks.

Students are also encouraged to explore these themes through class discussions around questions of
identity as they have encountered these through literature, cinema, popular culture and from their
own lived experience. The key focus is to historicise and contextualise both definitions and
experiences cultural identity and ask when and why categories and the experience of these shifted
over time and political context.

### EVALUATION

A structure of continuous evaluation will be followed with an end sem examination  worth 60% of your
grade) a group project worth 20% of your grade and 20% for in -class group and individual
presentations, discussions, pop-quiz etc.

### ATTENDANCE POLICY

Will follow the institute’s rule of insisting on 80% attendance. Habitual non-attendance and a score
below 80% (without any specific urgent medical reason) will be penalized.

### COURSE OUTCOMES

By the end of the course, students will be expected to have gained a broad understanding and
knowledge of the social, intellectual and political struggles in the contemporary world and develop
a critical response to these. They should  be able read and respond to academic writing in the
Humanities and Social Sciences. Finally, they would develop an open mindedness about their received
views and have challenged many of their assumptions about culture, identity and politics and to be
able to think more critically about the present.
