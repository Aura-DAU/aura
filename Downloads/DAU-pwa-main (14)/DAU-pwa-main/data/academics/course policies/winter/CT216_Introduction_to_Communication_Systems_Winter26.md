## Course Description

The course CT216 introduces students to the various engineering techniques that empower modern communication systems. The course demonstrates the application of elegant mathematical principles to the practical engineering design of communication systems. Students gain hands-on skills on these topics by performing software laboratory experiments throughout the semester and by completing one project in the semester. The specific topics covered are information theoretic limits and power versus bandwidth trade-off in a communication system, introduction to signals and systems, digital and analog modulation schemes, design of receivers and demodulators, channel coding schemes, the seven-layer OSI model, etc.

---

## Course Outcomes (COs)

This course introduces students to the engineering knowledge (P1) of the mathematics and engineering fundamentals of communication systems. Students engage in the analysis (P2) and design (P3) of the basic modules of a communication system. As part of the Lab and the Project, students conduct investigations of complex design and analysis problems (P4) and make use of modern tools (P5) such as MATLAB and Python. Through the Lab and Project works, which are team-based and require a formal presentation by students, they acquire team-work (P9) and technical communication (P10) skills.
