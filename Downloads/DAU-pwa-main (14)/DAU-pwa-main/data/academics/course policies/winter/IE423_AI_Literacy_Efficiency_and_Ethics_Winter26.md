## Course Description

This course bridges the gap between technical capability and academic utility. The goal is Metacognition — using AI to "Learn how to Learn," conduct rigorous research, and prepare for an AI-driven job market.

## Course Structure

| Module | Goal | Weeks |
|---|---|---|
| Module I: Literacy & Inclusion (The Foundation) | Moving from "Prompting" to "Algorithmic Thinking." | Weeks 1–4 |
| Module II: Research & Data Fluency (The Workflow) | Accelerating discovery without compromising rigor. | Weeks 5–8 |
| Module III: Pedagogy & Career (The Divergence) | Preparing for the future of work and assessment. | Weeks 9–12 |
| Module IV: Ethics & Policy (The Governance) | Establishing rules and understanding impact. | Weeks 13–14 |

**Weekly Detailed Schedule:**

| Week | Topic | Sessions |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Paradigm Shift | Tue (Joint): Predictive vs. Generative AI; history from LSTMs to Transformers; why "Chatting" is the wrong mental model. Thu: The Ethics of Assistance — defining the "Gray Line" between Co-piloting (allowed) and Plagiarism (banned). |
| 2 | Algorithmic Design Thinking | Tue (Joint): Not just about prompts — Decomposition; breaking complex problems into logic chains. Thu: "Logic Mapping" — mapping out a thesis argument's logic tree before asking AI to draft text. |
| 3 | Inclusive Pedagogy (UDL) | Tue (Joint): AI as the Great Equalizer — how AI supports neurodiversity (Dyslexia, ADHD, language barriers). Thu: "The Reader Bot" — building a custom "Reader Assistant" that simplifies dense academic papers into bullet points/analogies. |
| 4 | The Hallucination Problem | Tue (Joint): Probabilistic Reasoning — why AI "lies"; the concept of "Temperature" in models. Thu: "Spot the Bot" — gamified session where faculty and students compete to identify AI-generated text vs. human text. |
| 5 | The Literature Review | Tue (Joint): Synthesis vs. Search — using Semantic Scholar and Consensus. Thu: "The Research Matrix" — generating a comparative analysis table of 10 papers in 15 minutes to find research gaps. |
| 6 | Data Storytelling | Tue (Joint): Visualizing Complexity — moving beyond static Excel charts to interactive dashboards. Thu: "Thesis Viz" — using AI to suggest 3 alternative visualizations (Heatmap, Scatter, Sankey) for project data. |
| 7 | Code & Simulation (Guest: CS Dept) | Tue (Joint): AI in the Loop — understanding code generation even if you aren't a coder. Thu: "Chat with Data" — using Python libraries (via AI) to clean messy CSV datasets for thesis work. |
| 8 | Mid-Semester Review | Tue (Joint): "Time-Saved" Showcase — lightning talks sharing efficient workflows discovered so far. Thu: Open Q&A and "Prompt Clinic" (troubleshooting specific issues). |
| 9 | Assessment Redesign | Tue (Joint): Bloom's Taxonomy 2.0 — moving from "Remembering" to "Evaluating." Thu: "Exam Prep" — using AI to generate infinite variations of practice problems for upcoming finals. |
| 10 | Personalized Learning | Tue (Joint): The Age of the Personal Tutor — adaptive learning systems. Thu: "The Super-Tutor" — training an AI to teach a complex concept (e.g., Quantum Mechanics) via Socratic questioning. |
| 11 | The Future of Work | Tue (Joint): Human-AI Collaboration Models — the "Centaur" vs. the "Cyborg" approach to work. Thu: "Resume Optimization" — beating the Applicant Tracking System (ATS) by optimizing keywords and formatting. |
| 12 | Discipline Deep Dives | Tue (Joint): The Two Cultures — bridging STEM and Humanities in AI. Thu: "The Shadow Portfolio" — documenting new AI skills (Prompting, Data Viz) for LinkedIn/CV. |
| 13 | Global Impact | Tue (Joint): The Hidden Cost — environmental impact (water/energy consumption of training) and data labor ethics. Thu: "Debate Club" — AI & Intellectual Property: Who owns the output? |
| 14 | Capstone Showcase | Tue (Joint): Final Presentations. Thu (Joint): Final Presentations. |

## Program Structure Context

IE423 AI Literacy, Efficiency, and Ethics is nominally offered for credit as an ICT elective for BTech 6th semester students, and is also open for audit to BTech 6th/8th semester, MTech, and PhD students. Sessions run 2 lectures/week at 90 minutes each, with lab assignments done in take-home mode.

---

## Additional Notes

**Certification Requirements (AI Literacy Certificate):** To earn the completion certificate, students must submit:
1. The Research Matrix — an AI-generated literature review table.
2. The Career Packet — an AI-optimized Resume & Cover Letter.
3. The Study Log — evidence of using AI for deep Socratic learning.

- This is a distinctive, highly contemporary course focused on AI-augmented metacognition, research workflows, and career preparation — a departure from traditional technical/theoretical course structures in this batch.
- This course shares co-instructor V Sunitha with SC471 (Algorithmic Graph Theory) from the Autumn 2025-26 batch.
- The course is explicitly designed to be co-taught jointly (many sessions marked "Joint") and includes a guest lecture from the CS Department (Week 7).
