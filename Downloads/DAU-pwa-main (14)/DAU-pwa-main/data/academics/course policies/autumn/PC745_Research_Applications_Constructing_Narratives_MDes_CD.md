## Course Description

Core course for MDes (CD) Semester 3. This course is part of a two-course sequence to help students identify a communication problem, conduct research, and craft a narrative on the possibilities of a specific design intervention based on field and archival work, literature, and product reviews. This is the **second part** of the course — the first part being **PC740 - Research Project Seminar**, taken by Prof. Madhumita Mazumdar — and principally focuses on crafting an appropriate narrative leading to a communication product, based on the identification and research conducted in PC740.

**Course Themes:** Encourages students to apply theoretical insights drawn from the earlier module on narratives and develop their own narrative form around a particular idea or message they wish to communicate. The main focus is helping students formulate their final project proposals. Students engage in informed discussions about their design problems with faculty, provide a clear rationale for their project selection, explain how they intend to implement skills/aptitudes gained from previous modules, and demonstrate the intellectual capability of integrating the technical component of their design project with questions derived from their understanding of the social and cultural contexts of communication. Students apply research methodologies learnt in the previous module (PC740) and write a concrete proposal per given guidelines.

The primary objective is to enable students to make informed decisions about the nature of the project they wish to undertake, the appropriate communication medium, the range of intellectual skills needed, and the technical know-how to execute the project. This module may involve a group project testing students' capacity to work together and develop a product reflecting coordinated teamwork — e.g., developing a public interest message through paper, voice, and screen, and developing a keen sense of narrative as it takes shape through each medium.

## Course Outcomes (COs)

At the end of the course, students are expected to have a deeper and more robust understanding of the role of Communication Design in society, and to take this understanding to develop concrete and executable proposals which will develop into their final Thesis projects.

## Evaluation / Grading Scheme

**Assessment Method:** Continuous assessment of application of concepts through the duration of the course, along with assessment of group and individual assignments. Assessment covers not only the assignments but professionalism, precision, pride, care, and discipline displayed by students.

Students are evaluated on: overall concept for the final proposal, integration of direct and indirect research into the overall narrative, and creative approach to presenting the narrative and how effectively it communicates the concept to the intended audience.

At each stage, students make a formal presentation supported by script/concept drawings/references, reviewed by both instructor and peers.
