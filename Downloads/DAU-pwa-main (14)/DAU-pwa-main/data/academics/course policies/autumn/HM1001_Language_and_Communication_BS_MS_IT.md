## Course Description

This course is designed to make students both proficient in, and aware of, the social processes of language use and communication, approaching language not merely as grammar and vocabulary, but as discourse: language produced and interpreted in context, shaped by who is speaking to whom, for what purpose, and within what shared frame of knowledge.

While a good part of the course is designed to equip the BS-MS (IT) students with the essential skills for effective communication and comprehension of ideas that will help them in their career as IT professionals, it also includes modules that train the students in reading texts closely, recognising how coherence is built (not just through grammatical cohesion but through inference and shared assumptions), and discussing ideas in them.

The course will include sessions intended to improve the students' writing competence, ability to articulate ideas and think critically, presentation skills, and command over English language. Exercises like group activities, writing tasks, class presentations, and creative assignments will be combined with lectures on basic English grammar, reading skills, and protocols of communication appropriate to a modern work culture — including sensitivity to discourse type (email, report, proposal, conversation) and to audience, register, and purpose.

Apart from three hours of contact hours per week, the course will include occasional screenings of fictional and documentary films related to the theme.

---

## Course Outcomes (COs)

At the end of the course, students will be equipped with essential skills for writing and speaking in English in everyday situations, and for communicating ideas as part of work, at presentations and during interviews. Beyond the mechanics of grammar, students will develop an awareness of language as a social act — adjusting what they say and how they say it based on audience, purpose, and context, and recognising how meaning is built through inference and shared knowledge as much as through explicit statement. Through individual and group activities, students will practise producing and interpreting a range of discourse types — from professional writing and spoken interaction to literature, poetry, and cinema — sharpening both their communicative competence and their ability to read, listen, and respond critically and appropriately in varied real-world situations.
