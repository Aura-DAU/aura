# Academic Requirements for the PhD Program in Humanities, Social Sciences and Design

*(Effective from Autumn 2024-25)*

## Source

- **Institution:** DA-IICT, Gandhinagar
- **Original file:** `Academic/Academic Requirements_Ph D Program_-HSS&Design_wef 2024-25 as of July 2024.pdf`
- **Dated:** July 2024

## Scope

Governs the PhD (Doctor of Philosophy) degree, typically completed in 4–6 years, covering post-admission academic activities only (not admission eligibility/procedures). Dean (Academic Programs)/Registrar may issue implementing instructions; the Director, as Academic Council Chairman, may relax/exempt provisions in exceptional cases (reported to the Academic Council at its next meeting).

## 1. Registration

**1.1 Categories of Registration:** Three categories — **Regular (Full-time)**, **Sponsored (Part-time)**, and **External (Part-time)**. Only resident registration counts toward the degree residence requirement; a foreign student on a student visa cannot register externally.
- **Regular (resident):** Must be residential for a minimum of 3 years from first-semester registration; coursework and research carried out at the Institute. A student may shift from Regular to part-time category after 3 years, on supervisor recommendation with RPC approval (obtainable via email) forwarded to Dean (AP) — only if minimum course credits (Table 1) are already earned.
- **Sponsored/External (part-time):** Register for a duly approved research programme; pay prescribed fees.
- All students must register in the semester their final thesis is submitted for evaluation, paying prescribed fees.

**1.2 Provisional Registration:** New entrants awaiting qualifying-exam results register provisionally, submitting certificates for verification by the Academic Calendar deadline (~8 weeks from registration); originals returned, self-attested copy retained.

**1.3 Late Registration:** ~1 week window with a late fee; beyond that (Dean approval, PGC recommendation), registration is allowed **for thesis credits only**.

**1.4 Academic Advising:**

**(a) Course work:**
- DA-IICT's academics span **7 areas**: Communication and Signal Processing (CSP), Computer Science (CS), Electronics and VLSI (EnV), Physics (Phy), Mathematics (Math), Humanities Arts & Social Science (HSS), and Design.
- Full-time students register for **minimum 3 courses (4xx/5xx/7xx only)** in semester I — 2 must be area core courses (per the PhD coordinator's list), and by semester II's core courses, one of the 4 total core courses across both semesters must be a **Research Methods** course. Regular PhD candidates may count a **maximum of 1 MOOC** toward minimum course credits for the entire program.
- Part-time students register for minimum 3 PG-level courses in semester I and minimum 2 in semester II, including a compulsory Research Methods course (in either semester). Non-Research-Methods courses are decided with the supervisor. Courses can be taken via:
  - **Mode 1:** UGC-recognized platform (e.g., SWAYAM) with a passing certificate; a 2/3-credit course (2-0-0-2/3-0-0-3) must run minimum 8/12 weeks.
  - **Mode 2:** On-campus, in person, if offered to UG/PG students that semester (suits work-from-home students).
  - **Mode 3:** On-campus course taken online (if the instructor agrees), with in-person attendance for all evaluation components.
- Full-time students may also use Mode 1, with supervisor recommendation and PhD coordinator approval — max 1 such course per semester, credits counted once the certificate is submitted.
- Minimum course credits can be earned up until synopsis registration, across all 3 registration categories.

**(b) Supervisor Assignment (Full-Time):** Students identify a prospective supervisor during semesters I–II; the Dean (AP) office formally appoints one after semester II, on joint student + supervisor request. Must be finalized before Comprehensive Part II; once finalized, the RPC is formed by Dean-AP in consultation with the supervisor. To complete Comprehensive Part II by end of semester III, supervisor appointment must occur within the first 2 months of that semester (end of February if semester III is Winter; mid-September if Autumn).

**(c) Supervisor Assignment (Part-Time):** The faculty member whose Letter of Recommendation (LoR) the student submitted at admission is appointed as supervisor at the start of semester I.

**(d)** Students may repeat/substitute courses graded DD, DE, or F, per §2.4.

**1.5 Semester Load:**
- Course-work-only full load: typically 3–4 courses; PGC may permit up to 5 (on PhD coordinator + supervisor recommendation); minimum 3 courses/semester. A semester load is typically 12 credits. Once minimum research credits are met, students are advised to register for only 3 research courses per semester.
- External-registration (research-only) students: max 12 / min 3 credits per regular semester.

**1.6 Adding/Dropping and Withdrawal:** Same framework as M.Tech (ICT)/(EC) — Add/Drop with instructor + Convener PGC endorsement by Academic Calendar deadlines; pre/post-registration withdrawal rules identical (one semester at a time; Dean approval; risk of being struck off rolls if improperly handled); withdrawn/suspended semesters show as "withdrawn" and count toward maximum duration.

**2.1.1** Course work follows §1.4(a) guidelines. Full-time students from semester III, part-time from semester I, are advised by their supervisor on courses matching their research domain.

**2.1.2** Research credits (e.g., PC801-Research-1, 3 credits) become advisable after clearing the comprehensive exam. Max research courses/semester = max course count (min 3, max 4; up to 5 on recommendation). Students must register the **Synopsis as one Research Unit (3 credits)**.

**2.1.3** Course levels: 1xx/2xx/3xx/6xx do **not** count toward minimum course credit requirement; 4xx/5xx/7xx do. An area's 2xx/3xx/6xx course floated as a core course becomes eligible with added academic components and a distinct PhD-registration course identity.

**2.1.4** **Self-study courses:** 3 credits each; max 2 for the entire PhD, max 1 per semester; only from semester III onward; max 1 per instructor. Requires application to Dean (AP) with course content/evaluation/credit structure, expected outcome, and instructor consent (self-study proposal form available from Dean-AP office). Graded by the course instructor/mentor.

**2.1.5** 5+ years of professional research experience (R&D orgs, industry/research labs, universities) may count for **6 research credits** in the relevant domain, on supervisor recommendation and Dean (AP) approval.

**2.1.6** **Research credits before clearing the comprehensive exam:** Permitted if the supervisor is appointed and allows it — max 2 such courses, max 1/semester. Requires PGC/PhD Coordinator approval, supervisor consent, and supervisor + RPC-awarded grades communicated to the PhD coordinator.

**2.1.7** **Transfer of credits:** PG-level courses at reputed outside organizations may be credited/waived based on certificates, course weightage, supervisor recommendation, and Dean (AP) approval — only if not already counted toward another degree/diploma.

**2.2 Audit Courses:** Same rule as other programs — satisfactory audit performance earns 'P'; unsatisfactory leaves no grade/transcript entry.

**2.3 Level 1/2/3/6 (1xx/2xx/3xx/6xx) Courses:** May be taken with area-coordinator (if no supervisor) or supervisor recommendation, plus Dean (Academic Programs) approval. Credits don't count toward minimum course credits but may count toward total minimum credits.

- (a) "I" converts to a regular grade by the Academic Calendar deadline or auto-converts to F.

- (f) Repeated/substituted courses: old grade stays on transcript, excluded from CPI.
- (g) Partial-semester leave or mid-semester thesis submission: PGC may reduce research credits appropriately.

**2.5 Sponsored (Part-Time) / External (Part-Time) Category — Additional Requirements:**
(a) Must be sponsored by their employer.
(b) Must submit an LoR from a DA-IICT faculty member (implying agreed research area/problem statement) at application time.
(c) Must complete coursework and comprehensive exam per standard PhD requirements.
(d) Sponsorship letter in prescribed format at admission, including a Sponsorship Certificate (+ No Objection Certificate if sponsored by a College/University) undertaking the candidate's release for coursework/comprehensive-exam completion.
(e) The Sponsorship Certificate must confirm the candidate's continued employment and return to the parent organization for research work after completing coursework/comprehensive requirements.
(f) Minimum 2 years' job experience at a reputed organization.
(g) **No stipend/scholarship or teaching assistantship** from the Institute.

| Requirement | Sponsored/External PhD |
|---|---|
| Guidance | DA-IICT Guide (mandatory), Co-Guide from sponsoring agency (optional, subject to mutual acceptance) |
| Infrastructure | DA-IICT / sponsoring agency, respectively, for time spent at each |
| Financial Support (TA/RA) | Not applicable unless indicated in the agreement |
| Intellectual Property Rights | Jointly DA-IICT and sponsoring agency, as specified in the agreement |

**2.6 Residency Requirement:** Being on campus for course work/research on all working days of the semester.
- **Regular category:** Must fulfill residency for both course and research credits.
- **Sponsored/External:** Not required to be residential while earning credits, but must be residential a **minimum of 10 working days per semester** (can include the RPS); supervisor maintains and submits attendance sheets to Dean (AP) each semester. Special circumstances may allow earning course credits at a reputed outside organization, on supervisor recommendation and Dean (AP) approval.

**2.7 PhD Synopsis:**
1. Requires supervisor/RPC consent.
2. Registered at the start of a semester.
3. Synopsis Committee constituted by Dean (AP).
4. Presentation must occur by semester end.
5. After passing, thesis must be submitted to Dean (AP) within a **maximum of 6 months**.
6. Registration requires minimum course, research, and total credits already earned.

## 3. Academic Performance Requirement

**3.1 SPI/CPI:** Same weighted-average definitions as other programs, to 2 decimal places. **S and X graded courses excluded from computation.**

**3.2 Minimum CPI for graduation:**

| Program | CPI for Graduation |
|---|---|
| PhD | **7.0** |

**3.3 Academic Probation and Dismissal:** CPI below 7.0 at semester-end, or an X grade in a research course, triggers probation (written intimation) for the next semester. PGC prescribes course load and may set a minimum SPI target; monitors progress; recommends leaving to the Director if performance doesn't improve. **More than 5 unsatisfactory (X) grades → automatic discontinuation.** Termination letter issued by appropriate authority.

## 4. Fellowships / Teaching Assistantships

**4.1** Regular (full-time) students are eligible for TA/RA stipend support, **maximum 5 years**, involving lab courses/tutorials, teaching assistance, research projects, and academic administration. Amount announced in the Institute's Admissions Policy/website; questions go to Dean (AP)/PGC.

**4.2** Stipend: **Rs. 35,000/month** for the first 2 years, **Rs. 40,000/month** from year 3 onward.

**4.3** Stipend reduced by **50%** the following semester if a student gets more than 1 unsatisfactory (X) grade in a semester, or CPI falls below 7.0/10.

## 5. Comprehensive Examination and Proposal Defense

**5.1** All students must pass a comprehensive examination testing overall subject comprehension.

**5.2**
- **(i) Full-time:** Two parts — **Part I (Written/Qualifiers)** tests competency in the broad discipline (based on area core courses), and **Part II (Proposal Defense)** — literature survey + research proposal presented to the RPC within **4 months** of passing Part I. Neither part may occur during semesters I–II.
- **(ii)** Area coordinators organize Part I each semester; supervisor + RPC organize Part II.
- **(iii)** Parts I and II can be conducted in tandem.
- **(iv) Part-time students:** Only Part II required, conducted during semester III — by end of May if semester III is Winter, by end of December if Autumn.

**5.3** Minimum CPI of 7.0/10 required to sit Part I/II, plus registration and passing of the 5 courses per §1.4(a).

**5.4** For full-time students: **Part I** during the first half of semester III (soon after In-semester 1 exam) — February if Winter semester III (Part II by end of May) or August if Autumn semester III (Part II by end of December).

**5.6** **Maximum 2 attempts** per part; only one PhD Comprehensive exam (per part) per semester.

**5.7** A failed first attempt gets a second exam by the same board (unless the Dean decides otherwise); too-short a gap between attempts may bar the second attempt.

**5.8** Failing either part on the second attempt → **discontinuation from the program.**

**5.9** Formal candidacy for the PhD Degree begins after passing both parts (full-time) or Part II (part-time).

## 6. Supervisor Selection and RPC Formation

- **Sponsored/Part-time:** Supervisor = the LoR-writing faculty member; formally assigned by Dean (AP) by mid-semester 1 (a new supervisor is appointed via the normal process if the original LoR faculty becomes unavailable).
- **Regular category:** Student identifies a prospective supervisor as early as possible, preferably by end of semester II; Dean (AP) formally assigns after semester II. Must be finalized before Comprehensive Part II.
- **RPC (Research Progress Committee):** Thesis supervisor + 2 faculty members in the relevant area, formed by Dean (AP) office after supervisor appointment; one of the two additional members may be an external expert, with Dean-AP approval.

## 7. Research Progress Seminar (RPS)

Held twice yearly (typically November for Autumn Semester, April for Winter Semester) for students who've cleared both comprehensive-exam parts.
- RPC assesses research progress.
- RPC may invite an external expert (supervisor needs Dean (AP) approval for this).
- Grades decided by the supervisor in consultation with RPC members.

## 8. Migration Rules (PhD → M.Tech)

**8.1 Eligibility for M.Tech Degree (after failed comprehensive exam):** Direct-entry or migrated PhD students who fail the comprehensive exam within the specified duration may still get an M.Tech degree if they: meet M.Tech eligibility and continuation criteria, and submit an M.Tech thesis within a maximum of 2 semesters from the semester after the failed exam — with **no financial support** during this period.

**8.2 PhD to M.Tech Migration:** Requires application to Dean (Academic Programs), after completing minimum 2 semesters of PhD, and meeting:
- Entered with a B.Tech/BE degree or equivalent.
- An appropriate M.Tech program must be available.
- At least 18 course credits completed.
- Minimum CPI 6.5/10.0.

**8.3 Completion Requirements:** Must complete all M.Tech degree requirements within 2 years (4 semesters) of migration; PhD credits carry over.

## 9. Termination of a Student's PhD Admission

- More than 5 unsatisfactory (X) grades in research courses (entire program) → termination.
- Failure to defend the PhD Synopsis before the maximum allowed duration (Table 1) → termination; the student may seek fresh admission via the Admission Committee, restarting all academic requirements if approved.
- Failing the comprehensive exam on the second attempt → termination.

## 10. Glossary

Same definitions as other postgraduate programs: Academic Probation, CPI, Grade Points, PGC, Semester (Autumn: July–November; Winter: December/January–April), Semester Credits, Semester Grade Points, SPI.

## Appendix — Tentative Core Courses & Course Types

**Tentative core courses:**
- Autumn Semester: (1) PhD Colloquium I, (2) Research Methods
- Winter Semester: (1) Directed Study, (2) PhD Colloquium II: Introduction to Interdisciplinary Studies

**Note on Directed Study, Independent Study, and Reading Courses:** None involve a public student presentation; specific definitions may be adapted to DA-IICT's pedagogical needs.

1. **Directed Study:** Substitutes for a traditional course not offered that semester; covers essentially the same material. Evaluation may include presentation, term paper, exam, etc.
2. **Independent Study:** An extension/"spin-off" of a traditional course, for deeper/more independent pursuit of a topic already studied. Content outline is developed jointly by student and instructor; the student must demonstrate adequate background. Evaluation may include presentation, project work, term paper, exam, etc.
3. **Reading Course:** A specially arranged tutorial-style course not normally in the curriculum, between a student and faculty member; counts as a regular course (may count within a concentration but may not satisfy distribution requirements). Evaluation may include presentation, project work, term paper, exam, etc.
