# Faculty Entrepreneurship Policy

> **Note:** the source PDF's cover page marks this document "Classification: confidential." Treat with corresponding discretion — this summary is provided for internal KB use, not necessarily for open publication to all audiences.

## 1. Preamble

Formulates guidelines for DA-IICT faculty to promote — while maintaining high academic-integrity standards — interdisciplinary research, faculty entrepreneurship, and related policies.

## 2. Vision

"To encourage interdisciplinary research and innovation leading to successful start-ups."

## 3. Mission

Manage IP; evaluate new inventions; collaborate/negotiate research with industrial partners; license institute-generated technology; create and support start-ups.

## 4. Guiding Tenets

Coordinates four pillars: the institute, faculty, students, and collaborators — guided by innovation and faculty competence.

- Start-ups are **strongly encouraged**; each must have a DA-IICT faculty member identified as mentor/director (or equivalent) — **mandatory**.
- Primary focus: **design and innovation over business** — to safeguard academic integrity and research interests.
- Proposal strength isn't judged rigidly — an initial review committee spans academia, industry, and society to assess innovation/business-worthiness.
- The **license-based model** is seen as the best way to quantify academic research commercially.

### 4.1 Pathway to Start-ups

Faculty apply formally to the DA-IICT Director via an **Expression of Interest (EOI)** — a one-page concept note. Unless there's valid reason to start elsewhere, faculty are strongly encouraged to incubate through **DCEI**.

## 4.2 Administrative Composition of Start-ups

Faculty role can be one of four kinds:
a) Promoter (ownership)
b) Mentor (volunteer service)
c) Consultant (paid service)
d) Board member (holding shares, non-promoter)

If faculty is a promoter, the start-up's administrative body can be composed in one of five modes: faculty alone; faculty + students of home institute; faculty of home institute + faculty of other institutes; faculty + students + alumni; or faculty + other entrepreneurs.

## 4.3 Usage of Labs

Governed by the institute's prevailing terms/policies once a company is incubated at DA-IICT.

## 4.4 Support to Start-ups

1. Faculty may guide/coach/mentor student-only start-ups (business plan, capital risk forecasting, financial/technical/social advice).
2. DA-IICT provides hosting offices and lab access (faculty entrepreneur writes to the Director for approval).
3. Seed funding, as a soft loan, follows the DCEI start-up policy.

## 5. Start-Up Internships

Students (BTech/MTech/PhD) can intern under IP experts, entrepreneurs, and design experts on a start-up, for **3-6 months**. Motivates innovation via hands-on experience in early-stage invention, development, and entrepreneurship. Students may refine inventions, prototype, explore applications, evaluate markets, and work with DA-IICT-affiliated companies/researchers. Strong performers may get access to accelerator programs (tech transfer, entrepreneurship, IP, marketing, industrial design). Open to PG students across science, engineering, social sciences, and design.

**Requirements:** must sign an internship agreement with DA-IICT. These internships are **not accepted toward master's thesis requirements**. Award decided by a joint committee appointed by the Director.

## 6. Conflict of Interests

Company-related work overlaps with institute duties, so a comprehensive conflict-of-interest policy applies:

1. Faculty involved in the above activities must sign an agreement that regular duties won't suffer; if there's evidence of negligence due to company/consultancy work, the **Dean-AP** (under confidentiality) can request activity information.
2. Consultancy work: max **1 day/week**.
3. Faculty as a **direct promoter** cannot hold an executive position (CEO/CTO/COO) until the company "graduates" from DA-IICT — can hold an independent-director position instead.
4. Once the company graduates and the faculty wants to act as CEO etc., they must take a sabbatical or leave without pay per DA-IICT norms (case-specific).
5. Faculty as **non-promoter** holding stock options for an independent-director role: a sharing model for the stock options must be discussed/agreed with DA-IICT; dilution terms are case-by-case, and DA-IICT has **first right to buy** if faculty wants to sell.
6. Faculty-driven start-ups selected by an outside accelerator/incubator (India or abroad): faculty can spend up to a maximum of a semester at the incubation center, per a Director-appointed committee's recommendation.
7. If a faculty member consults for a company owned by another faculty member, the standard Consultancy Policy terms apply.

## Exemption

**Prize money for research-recognition awards** is excluded from this policy's regulatory framework — it isn't treated as consulting, industrial funding, or a start-up program.
