## Biography

Mukesh Tiwari is currently working as an Associate Professor at DA-IICT. He joined the institute in 2009. Before this, he completed his M.Sc. in Physics from the Indian Institute of Technology, Delhi, and PhD from the University of New Mexico, working with Nitant Kenkre. His primary research interest is in complex networks, nonequilibrium statistical mechanics, and nonlinear dynamics. His current research directions focus on interdisciplinary sciences. He uses models and techniques originating in physics to understand phenomena and processes in social systems and complex time series arising in finance and physiology. His research also involves studying the properties of highly nonlinear waves in granular systems and transport phenomena in quantum and classical systems.

## In-Depth Profile / Student Guide

### Research Identity
His work is centered around Complex Networks + Statistical Physics + Network Science + Dynamical Systems + Computational Modeling. This is mathematical modeling of real-world systems. He is part of DAU's interdisciplinary math-physics-networks group, not traditional CS-only faculty.

### Core Research Areas
1. **Complex Networks (Core Area):** Studies systems like Social networks, Communication networks, Biological networks, Internet structure. Key questions: Why do networks evolve the way they do? How does information spread? What makes a network robust or fragile?
2. **Statistical Physics Approach:** His signature style. Applies physics concepts to compute emergent behavior in systems, phase transitions in networks, collective behavior of nodes. (Example: How small changes create large system-wide effects).
3. **Network Science:** Graph modeling, Network topology analysis, Connectivity patterns, Community detection.
4. **Dynamical Systems:** Time-evolving systems, Stability analysis, System dynamics modeling.
5. **Data-driven Complex Systems:** Using data + ML ideas to study networks, Hybrid modeling of real-world systems.

### Research Style
His approach: Real-world system -> Graph representation -> Statistical physics modeling -> Emergent behavior analysis. This is deep mathematical modeling of systems, not application engineering.
