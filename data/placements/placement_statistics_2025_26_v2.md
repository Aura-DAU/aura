## Internships

* [cite_start]**Research Internship:** The research internship helps in training students to develop independent research skills, something which the university prides itself on. [cite: 1558] [cite_start]Faculty on campus act as mentors to the students and thus, develop close interaction between them resulting in excellent research. [cite: 1559] [cite_start]Some of the areas of research are in various fields of Computer Science, Electronics and Communications, Natural Language Processing, Speech Technology, Digital Cash Protocols, Distance bounding Protocols, Guessing Attacks, Hash Chains, Numbering Problems in Trees, Search Algorithms, Information Visualization using Height Mapping, Mobile Applications, BandPass Sampling, FPGA Implementation, CMOS Amplifier/Comparator, Design Study, Current Streaming DACS, Image Compression, Rayleigh Fading Channels, Modes in Optical Fiber, V-SAT Satellite, Information Retrieval, and Human Computer Interaction. [cite: 1560]
* **Summer Internship:** The pre-final year students have the liberty to pursue their summer internship in research or as interns in leading tech companies. [cite: 1562] Summer internship lasts for a period of 6 to 8 Weeks. [cite: 1563]
* [cite_start]**Summer Industrial Internship:** Students get an opportunity to work at tech giants. [cite: 1566] [cite_start]It provides them with different sets of skills that can be applied at the industrial level. [cite: 1566] [cite_start]They gain experience by working on live industry projects alongside a team of professionals. [cite: 1567] [cite_start]It provides them exposure to the commercial world. [cite: 1568]
* **Summer Research Internship:** Students work on research projects under the mentors of their field of interest. [cite: 1570] It trains them on how research projects are carried out. [cite: 1571] The interaction between the mentor and the mentee results in good research work. [cite: 1572]
* [cite_start]**Industrial Internship:** Final year students are taken as interns for 6 months of duration in various leading companies, where they are exposed to various industrial practices, which helps them to gain hands-on experience of the industry projects, and apply their knowledge to the industry as well as understand the functioning of the company. [cite: 1574] [cite_start]Companies also gain from the fresh perspective and inputs of the students, which in turn, helps in improving their role in the student community. [cite: 1575]
* **Rural Internship:** The B.Tech. Curriculum mandates all students to undertake a 4 Weeks Rural Internship with an objective to expose and sensitize the students to the social and economic realities of rural lives and help them appreciate the constraints and opportunities for development. [cite: 1577] Rural Internship entails placing students in villages across India to work in NGOs, engaging in various projects associated with socio-economic development, such as education, environment, agriculture, and rural governance. [cite: 1578]

## Placement Policy 2025-26

### Eligibility to Participate

* [cite_start]All students of final and pre-final year are eligible for placement process, provided: [cite: 1797]
  * [cite_start]The placement cell has confirmed their registration [cite: 1798]
  * They meet the eligibility criteria specified by both the company and the placement policy [cite: 1799]

### Types of Offers

* [cite_start]Internship + Job (I+J) [cite: 1801]
* [cite_start]Job (J) only [cite: 1802]
* Internship (I) only [cite: 1803]
* [cite_start]Summer Internship (SI) [cite: 1804]
* [cite_start]For I+J and J offers, the company's declared CTC is used for offer categorization. [cite: 1805]
* For I only, the post internship CTC (if converted to a job) is used. [cite: 1806]

### Company Categories

* [cite_start]**Category I:** 10 LPA and Above [cite: 1808]
* [cite_start]**Category II:** Below 10 LPA [cite: 1808]

### Offer and Switching Rules

* If a student receives a Job (J) or Internship + Job (I+J) offer from a Category I company, this is the final offer. [cite: 1810] The student is out of the placement process and cannot switch to another company [cite: 1811]
* [cite_start]If a student receives a Internship (1) only offer from a Category II company, they have one chance to switch provided: [cite: 1812]
  * [cite_start]The next company must offer at least 1.5 times the CTC of the initial Category II offer [cite: 1813]
  * Example: If the first offer is 5 LPA, the next eligible offer must be at least 7.5 LPA [cite: 1814]
* [cite_start]If the new offer is a Job (J) that allows internships elsewhere, the student may keep both [cite: 1815]

### Dream Category Companies

* [cite_start]At the placement cell's discretion, some companies are classified as "Dream" companies [cite: 1820]
* All students, even those who have used their switch, can apply to Dream companies [cite: 1821]
* [cite_start]If a student receives a Dream company offer, all previous offers are automatically rejected, and the student exits the placement process [cite: 1822]

### Offer Rejection Policy

* [cite_start]An offer is considered rejected if: [cite: 1824]
  * The student informs the placement office in writing only [cite: 1825]
  * [cite_start]The student does not accepts offer within the declared time period [cite: 1826]
* [cite_start]If a student rejects a Job (J) only or Internship + Job (I + J) offer from a Category I or Category II Company, then they would be considered as not interested in obtaining an offer from the Placement Drive, and will be out of it [cite: 1827]
* A student can only reject one offer at maximum, while exercising the switch option or due to a parallel process, if they reject the second offer then they will be out of the placement drive [cite: 1828]
* [cite_start]On upgrading to a higher category company, the previous offer stands rejected automatically [cite: 1829]

### Process Conduct

* [cite_start]Students must not leave the placement process of a company midway; doing so is considered a rejection and may lead to Placement and Internship Affairs and Action Committee (PIAAC) [cite: 1831, 1832]
* All communication regarding offers and rejections must go through the placement office and within specified timelines [cite: 1833]
* [cite_start]Any complaint/allegation from the company which affects the reputation of the university will lead the student to Placement and Internship Affairs and Action Committee (PIAAC) [cite: 1834]

## Placement Process

1. [cite_start]The Placement Office designates a Point of Contact for each company and sends an invitation along with the Job Announcement Form (JAF). [cite: 1838]
2. [cite_start]Companies respond by submitting the completed Job Announcement Form. [cite: 1840]
3. [cite_start]Upon verification, the job opportunity is published and made accessible to students according to the schedule set by the Placement Office. [cite: 1842]
4. [cite_start]Interested and eligible students register for the opportunity, and the list of applicants is shared with the company. [cite: 1844]
5. [cite_start]The company conducts the recruitment process after finalizing the schedule in coordination with the Placement Office. [cite: 1846]
6. [cite_start]The company provides the final list of selected candidates. [cite: 1848]
7. [cite_start]The Placement Office confirms acceptance from the selected candidates and notifies the company accordingly. [cite: 1850]
8. [cite_start]Offer letters are sent to the Placement Office, which are then distributed to respective candidates [cite: 1852]
