## Graduation Requirements

**Base B.Tech (ICT) degree:**
1. Total credits: **156** (including 6 co-curricular credits)
2. Course credits: **129**
3. Internships + BTP/ITP credits: **19**
4. Minimum grade points: **5.0**

**Additional requirements for B.Tech (Hons.) ICT with a Minor in RAS:**
1. Honours courses: **5** (3 core + 2 electives)
2. Honours course credits: **minimum 15**
3. Minimum grade points: **6.5**

The Minor in RAS degree is conferred once a student meets **all** base B.Tech (ICT) requirements **plus** these additional requirements.

## Policies, Procedures, and Guidelines for the Minor in RAS

1. Students choose the minor at the **end of semester 3**.
2. Minimum CPI of **6.5** at end of semester 3 required to apply.
3. **Maximum 30 students** may enroll; if oversubscribed, selection is by descending CPI.
4. Students may drop the minor in any semester — but **cannot re-enroll** once dropped.
5. **No lateral entry** — enrollment option is available only in semester 4. However, Minor (RAS) core and elective courses remain open to all students under the **TE (Technical Elective) category**, without formal minor enrollment.
6. Students must pass all 5 designated Honours courses (3 core + 2 electives) to receive the degree.
7. Enrolled students choose RAS electives starting from **semester 5**.
8. MMP requires approval from the Minor's (RAS) coordinator, in consultation with the UG convener, and must be strictly in Robotics, Autonomous Systems, Cyber-Physical Systems, Robotic Vision, or Machine Vision.
9. **No restriction on BTP topic area** for Minor (RAS) students — may choose a RAS-related topic or any other ICT-domain area.

## Outline of the Minor (RAS) Core Courses

**Introduction to Robotics:** Course overview, history of robotics, robot arm, kinematic structure of the human arm and humanoid robot; coordinate frames, rotation matrix, translations, Euler angles, quaternions; homogeneous transform, compound transformations, Jacobians, Denavit-Hartenberg (D-H) parameters; inverse kinematics (position/orientation/velocities, redundancy, singularities); equations of motion, Euler-Lagrange formulation, Newton-Euler formulation; sensors, actuators, control overview, joint space control, trajectory generation.

**Introduction to Autonomous Systems:** Dynamics of robot manipulators, mobile robots, and drones (quadrotors); controls for motion planning, collision avoidance, trajectory optimization, grasping and manipulating objects; simulation and experiments in dynamics, trajectory generation, motion planning, and control of autonomous systems (robots, manipulators, drones).

**Robot Programming:** Introduces the Robot Operating System (ROS) and commonly-used robotics tools. Combines theory with hands-on application/implementation — creating software and simulations, interfacing with sensors/actuators, integrating control algorithms — working through exercises with autonomous ground robots usable in future robotics research.
