# The Sexual Harassment Complaint Process

## Source

- **Institution:** Dhirubhai Ambani Institute of Information and Communication Technology (DA-IICT), Gandhinagar
- **Original file:** `ICC/TO FILE A COMPLAINT_ PROCEDURE/2_DAIICT_THE SEXUAL HARRASMENT COMPLAINT PROCESS.pdf` (originally a Word document)
- **Issued by:** Gender Cell Committee, July 2018

## Overview

The Complaints Committee needs information on six stages (including fifteen steps), detailed below, for addressing a complaint of workplace sexual harassment. The complaint must have the confidentiality form signed by both the complainant and the respondent (provided in the same folder).

## Stage 1

- **Step 1 — Receive and acknowledge receipt of the complaint.** A parent, close kin, or trusted individual of the complainant may be permitted to register a complaint on their behalf (in case of physical incapability to register one themselves). In either case, the complainant's signature is mandated as proof of the complaint's validity.
- **Step 2 — Meet and talk to the complainant to explore options for formal and informal resolution.** The informal complaint must be filed in writing regardless of the resolution sought. If the complainant decides to file a written complaint, guidance is provided if required. The respondent will necessarily be informed of the complaint and specifically instructed not to communicate with the complainant at any time whatsoever (confidentiality clause applicable).
- **Step 3 — Informal mechanism.**
- **Step 4 — Formal mechanism.** The respondent and the complainant are not permitted to meet during the formal process.
- **Step 5 — Respondent and response.**

## Stage 2: Planning Carefully

- **Step 6 — Prepare the file.** The complaint should be read and analyzed carefully to understand the parties involved. The committee should be adequately prepared and may call on relevant internal/external/specialist input if the need arises.
- **Step 7 — Consideration.**

## Stage 3: Interviews

- **Step 8 — Prepare an interview plan for the hearing:** Complainant and Respondent.
- **Step 9 — Assess the completeness of the information collected.**

## Stage 4: Reasoning

- **Step 10 — Analyze and assess the information gathered during the inquiry.**
- **Step 11 — Create a timeline** to help establish the sequence of events related to the complaint.
- **Step 12 — Compare similarities and differences** within each of the statements made by the interviewees. No external member may be invited by the complainant or respondent to the Gender Cell proceedings.

## Stage 5: Findings and Recommendations

- **Step 13 — Findings.**
- **Step 14 — Recommendations** (warning, counseling, punitive action, legal action).

## Stage 6: Report

- **Step 15 — Writing the report.**

If necessary, based on the committee's findings, it will advise and recommend necessary action to the Director of the Institute.
