# Academic Integrity Policy

## Background

DAIICT was established in 2001 and conferred university status in 2003. It's on the UGC's list of universities and is a member of the Association of Indian Universities (AIU). NAAC accredited DAIICT with an 'A' Grade in 2017.

As a research-led university, faculty, students, and research staff make heavy use of online/offline information in their research and publications — raising the risk of unacknowledged use of others' intellectual work (plagiarism), which this policy exists to curb. Framed in pursuance of the **UGC (Promotion of Academic Integrity and Prevention of Plagiarism in Higher Educational Institutions) Regulations, 2018**.

## Policy Owner & Responsible Office

Owned by DAIICT. The **Dean (Academic Program)** has overall responsibility for creating, implementing, and updating the policy. **Approved by the Board of Governors of DAIICT.**

## Scope

Applies to all DAIICT faculty (full-time, adjunct, contractual), students, and research staff involved in research and publishing — including external collaborators — collectively "Authors." Also applies to all students/research staff submitting project work, theses, and dissertations as part of degree requirements.

## Key Definitions

- **Academic Integrity:** intellectual honesty in proposing, performing, and reporting activity leading to intellectual property creation.
- **Author:** a student, faculty, researcher, or staff of DAIICT claiming to be the creator of the work under consideration.
- **Institutional Academic Integrity Panel:** the body constituted at the institutional level to consider departmental panel recommendations and decide penalties for plagiarism allegations; in exceptional cases it investigates directly.
- **Plagiarism:** taking someone else's work or idea and passing it off as one's own.
- **Script:** a research paper, thesis, dissertation, book chapter, or similar work submitted for a master's/research degree or publication — **excludes assignments, term papers, project reports, coursework, and answer scripts.**

## Policy Objectives

1. Create awareness of academic integrity in research/thesis/dissertation submission.
2. Prevent academic misconduct including plagiarism, via timely information, technical support, and guidance.
3. Strengthen institutional mechanisms for responsible conduct of research.
4. Develop systems to check and deter plagiarism in compliance with UGC regulation.

## Plagiarism Prevention

- DAIICT uses **TURNITIN** to ensure theses, dissertations, publications, and similar documents are checked before submission; all faculty have access and make it available to students as needed.
- Extends to M.Tech, M.Des, and PhD students, and research staff, per AIC direction.
- Every student submitting a thesis/dissertation must submit an undertaking that the work is original and plagiarism-free.
- Documents must be uploaded to TURNITIN and get a similarity report.
- Each supervisor maintains similarity-report records for their students.
- The Resource Centre accepts the final report once it complies with AIC guidelines.

## Plagiarism Awareness

AIC recommends regular awareness sessions: the Resource Centre mentions the plagiarism software during post-admission orientation; course coordinators train students on the software; supervisors reinforce detection/prevention finer points at thesis/dissertation submission.

## Similarity Checks — What's Excluded

Per UGC notification, similarity checks exclude:
i. All quoted work reproduced with proper permission/attribution.
ii. References, bibliography, table of contents, preface, and acknowledgments.
iii. Generic terms, laws, standard symbols, and standard equations.

## Levels of Plagiarism (UGC scale)

| Level | Similarity | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Level 0 | Up to 10% | Minor similarities, **no penalty** |
| Level 1 | Above 10% to 40% | Low level |
| Level 2 | Above 40% to 60% | Mid-level |
| Level 3 | Above 60% | High level |

Similarity parameters are decided by the AIC.

## Redress Procedure

**Pre-submission phase:** the supervisor examines the similarity report; if the plagiarism level is above Level 1, the student is counselled to re-write/re-phrase similar content, repeated until the report clears.

**Post-submission phase:** if a plagiarism allegation is raised by examiners, publishers, or another party, the supervisor refers it to the AIC. The AIC follows principles of natural justice, submits recommendations after a hearing (accounting for the similarity-check exclusions above), and handles the redress/penalty on a case-by-case basis, hearing both sides as needed.

## Penalties

Imposed on Masters/Research-program students and on faculty/research staff **only after academic misconduct is established without doubt**, all appeal avenues exhausted, and the individual given fair, transparent opportunity to defend themselves before the AIC. Specific penalty levels for thesis/dissertation plagiarism follow the UGC notification's guidelines.
