# B.Tech Project (BTP) / Industrial Training Project (ITP) Guidelines

## Source

- **Institution:** DA-IICT, Gandhinagar
- **Original file:** `Academic/Academic Rules-Guidelines/Project-Thesis Guidelines/BTech Project Guidelines.pdf`
- **Dated:** March 1, 2023

## Preamble

1. Every student must complete at least a semester-long B.Tech Project (BTP), demonstrating their ability to learn current research and/or industrial areas of interest.
2. There is a distinction between the **on-campus project (BTP)** and the **off-campus project — Industrial Training Project (ITP)**.
3. A student may split BTP work between semesters 7 and 8: **3 credits (0-1-6) BTP Part 1** in semester 7, **9 credits (0-2-12) BTP Part 2** in semester 8 — or complete the entire BTP in semester 8.
4. Instead of BTP, a student may do an **ITP** in semester 8 only, off-campus.
5. ITP is done at an approved industry site with a designated industry mentor, on a well-specified project — suited to students wanting early industry experience, including those preparing for industry roles or heading to higher studies.
6. In BTP, the student addresses a problem of interest, identifies the issues, and develops techniques to address them — theoretical, experimental, or developmental work, individually or in groups under faculty supervision. BTP work may continue from a previous internship.

## Guidelines

1. BTP/ITP generally begins the first week of January and runs through the last week of April — **minimum 13 continuous weeks** before a student can present.
2. Group size for a common BTP problem: **maximum 2 students** under one faculty mentor.
3. A faculty member is recommended to supervise **no more than 6 BTPs**, totaling no more than **10 students**.
4. ITP placement can come via DA-IICT's placement office internship drive.
5. Self-arranged ITPs (via a student's own contacts) are also allowed, but **max 10 students** per company/organization at a time, unless the company formally partners with the placement office. Self-arranged ITPs are encouraged for R&D establishments.

## ITP Evaluation

1. **Part 1:** The on-campus mentor communicates with the onsite mentor mid-Winter-semester and receives a grade (after possible discussion), uploaded to USPMES.
2. **Part 2:** Student submits a report to the on-campus mentor, checked via Turnitin plagiarism software (threshold ~25% cumulative, 10% single source). The on-campus mentor again receives a grade from the onsite mentor, uploaded to USPMES.
3. An evaluation committee (formed by the BTP/ITP Coordinator at semester end) hears presentations from returning off-campus students and grades based on presentation + report, by consensus or individually.
4. Committee scores are collected by the BTP coordinator as hard copy; the on-campus mentor also participates in grading.
5. **Final Part 2 grade: 50% onsite mentor grade + 50% committee grade.**
6. Part 1 grade is uploaded from USPMES to e-Campus by the BTP coordinator; Part 2 grade is uploaded per the calculation above.

## BTP Evaluation

1. **Part 1:** Mentor grades based on interactions/assessment, uploaded to USPMES — end of Autumn semester (if Part 1 done in Autumn) or mid-Winter-semester (otherwise).
2. **Part 2:** Mentor requires a written report, Turnitin-checked, plus mentor approval; mentor uploads a grade to e-Campus carrying **70% weightage** for the final Part 2 grade.
3. An evaluation committee (including the mentor) grades independently — the mentor's own committee-context grading doesn't factor into this component. This committee grade, submitted as hard copy to the BTP coordinator, carries **30% weightage** for Part 2.
4. Part 1 grade uploaded to e-Campus as it appears on USPMES; Part 2 grade uploaded per the 70/30 calculation.

## Acceptable BTPs/ITPs

May be a novel/new idea, extension of previous research or a previous BTP, an abstract problem, or a proof-of-concept problem. Possible areas:
- Software Development
- System Design and Simulation
- Hardware Development/Implementation
- Embedded System (Software & Hardware combined) Development/Implementation
- Theoretical Modeling
- Technical Study
- Technical Survey and Modeling
- Modules of a larger R&D project jointly guided by faculty teams, focused on synthesizing classroom learning to solve real-world problems

## Deliverables of a BTP/ITP

Expected deliverables generally include:
- Problem statement
- Methodology adopted
- Background work
- Comparative study (products/methods/designs)
- Design documentation
- Simulation study results
- Analysis results
- Test methods and results
- Conclusive contribution of the project

For projects that don't clearly fit the above categories, the supervisor may specify project-specific deliverables.
