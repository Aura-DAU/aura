# Course (PC748)

Course PC 748
INTRODUCTION TO DESIGN HISTORY
Instructor - Prof. Madhumita Mazumdar
COURSE INSTRUCTOR- Prof. Madhumita Mazumdar
Course Objective : This course will introduce students to the emergent field of
Design History or Design Studies as a part of a broader attempt to instill in
students a critical engagement with their discipline. It will map the evolution of
interest in ‘historical’ questions related to Design both as practice and as
discipline. But before that we will try to understand the many meanings of
Design in use and ask which one of these helps us understand the true nature of
our practice.
Course Structure:
The course will introduce students to the range of approaches to the study of the
History of Design- particularly conventional, narrative approaches to Design
History and conceptual and critical approaches to the same.
Students would be able to make the distinction between practice and discipline
even if both are interlinked in order to understand how historians have
approached the subject of their study. The module will also include an important
connecting thread in the History of Design- that of the larger economic, social,
political and cultural contexts within which Design took shape both as practice

and as pedagogy. So there will be two entangled strands of historical inquiry we
will follow- one following the development of Design and the other looking at the
global and national contexts that shaped it.
These global contexts are indicated by such historical periodizations such as the
Industrial Revolution, the first Great War and the rise of Communism in Russia,
the inter-war years, the rise of Fascism the Cold War era, the era of Mass
Consumption, the Counter-Culture and the contemporary era of Globalization.
The final segment of the course will turn to India and look at colonial context in
which India found itself during the period of the Industrial Revolution. We
will see how the first consciousness of an Indian-designed and made products
was emerging in the context of the Swadeshi movement in 1905-1911, the
impact of the emergence of Gandhi in sensitizing us to the power of Design in
nationalist politics and finally we will look into the ways in which both modern
western Design practice and pedagogy as well as our own craft traditions and
material culture served to shape the context within which India’s first modern
Design school was established in 1961.
Course Outcome : Toward the end of the course, we will try to understand how
Design history helps us engage with questions of form, function and aesthetics in
completely different ways and generate new ways of looking at the products and
services they consume as part of our everyday lives. They will seek to
understand how does Design History prompt us to question the larger
implications of what we buy or use in terms of the impacts these have on human
health and wellbeing, on the built environment and the planet at large.
There will be continuous evaluation of students through class participation,
individual presentations and a final research paper, seminar or in-class
examination.
