# SCxxx Application of Statistics to Complex Systems Winter24 - Pratim Roy

## Overview
This document presents the course syllabus, policy guidelines, and lecture breakdown for **SCxxx Application of Statistics to Complex Systems** (Winter 24) taught by Pratim Roy. The course is an elective for students pursuing a B.Tech (Honours) degree in ICT and an elective for MNC students.

## Course Format
- **Lectures:** 3 hours per week.  
- **Credits:** 3.0 (3‑0‑0‑3).  

The course introduces probabilistic approaches to studying systems with a large number of degrees of freedom, ranging from ideal gases to stock‑market dynamics using concepts such as entropy and ensembles.

Grades are based on one mid‑semester examination, the end‑semester exam, and assignments. The weightage is:

## Course Outcomes
Students will learn to formulate a physical problem and solve it using analytical and computational methods. After completion, students should be able to:

- Understand and analyse the physical world using a basic set of principles.  
- Design, model, and investigate complex engineering problems.  

## Lecture Schedule
### 7.1 Concepts of Probability (10 Lectures)
- **Theory (5 lectures):** General definitions and statistical terminology, random variables, probability distributions, probability distributions for large *n*, information and entropy, random walk.  
- **Applications (5 lectures):** Using random variables as a tool to study physical systems, optimization, sampling from probability distributions, Monte Carlo integration, and applications in simple systems.

### 7.2 Statistical Formulation of a Physical Problem (6 Lectures)
Brief overview of necessary formalism from classical mechanics, Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formalism, how to specify the state of a system, statistical ensembles, density of states.

### 7.3 Concepts of Thermodynamics (7 Lectures)
Zeroth, First, and Second Laws of thermodynamics, entropy, concept of equilibrium and thermodynamic potentials, uses of these concepts in areas such as finance and the stock market.

### 7.4 Classical Statistical Mechanics (13 Lectures)
- **Theory (10 lectures):** Microcanonical, canonical, and grand canonical ensembles; partition function; Ising model and its uses in physics and other areas such as the stock market; exact solution of the Ising model.  
- **Applications (3 lectures):** Metropolis‑Hastings algorithm for Monte Carlo simulation of the Ising model and calculation of relevant physical quantities.

### 7.5 Kinetic Theory (4 Lectures)
Liouville’s theorem, Boltzmann equation, conservation laws.  

*Note: The number of lectures assigned to each topic is tentative and may change depending on student progress.*

## Objectives and Learning Outcomes
The objective of this course is to teach students how to develop a mathematical formulation of a physical problem and select appropriate methods to solve it. Laboratory sessions will supplement the lectures, giving students an opportunity to put theory into practice.
