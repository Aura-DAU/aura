# SCxxx Application of Statistics to Complex Systems Winter24 - Pratim Roy

## Overview

This document presents the course syllabus, policy guidelines, and lecture breakdown for SCxxx Application of Statistics to Complex Systems Winter24 - Pratim Roy.

## Course Details

### Placement
Application of Statistics to Complex Systems can be a science elective course for students taking a B.Tech (Honours) degree in ICT and an elective for MnC students.

### Format
The course will consist of 3 hours of lectures each week.

### Content
Concepts of statistics have proved to be highly useful in probing the behavior of different physical systems, typically consisting of a large number of particles. These were traditionally first developed in the context of thermal physics but have subsequently been used in other diverse areas. This course is intended to introduce probabilistic approaches to studying systems with a large number of degrees of freedom. These systems can range from ideal gases (large numbers of particles) to modeling the dynamics of the stock market using unifying concepts such as entropy and ensembles.

The course will introduce both the formalism needed for systematically studying such systems and many concrete applications wherein the techniques are applied.

## Course Outcomes

Students will learn to formulate a physical problem and solve it using analytical and computational methods. Problem-solving will be the main focus of this course. After completion, students should be able to:
- Understand and analyze the physical world using a basic set of principles  
- Design, model, and investigate complex engineering problems  

## Course Material and Tentative Lecture Schedule

### Concepts of Probability (10 Lectures)
**Theory (5 lectures):**  
- General definitions and statistical terminology  
- Random variables, Probability Distributions  
- Probability distributions for large n  
- Information and Entropy  
- Random Walk  

**Applications (5 lectures):**  
- Using random variables as a tool to study physical systems  
- Optimization, Sampling from Probability Distributions  
- Monte Carlo Integration and applications in simple systems  

### Statistical Formulation of a Physical Problem (6 Lectures)
- Brief overview of necessary formalism from classical mechanics  
- Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formalism  
- How to specify state of a system  
- Statistical Ensemble, Density of states  

### Concepts of Thermodynamics (7 Lectures)
- Zeroth Law, First Law, and Second Law of Thermodynamics  
- Entropy, Concept of equilibrium and thermodynamic potentials  
- Uses of these concepts in areas such as finance and the stock market  

### Classical Statistical Mechanics (13 Lectures)
**Theory (10 lectures):**  
- Microcanonical, Canonical, and Grand Canonical Ensembles  
- Partition Function  
- Ising model and its uses in physics and other areas (e.g., stock market)  
- Exact solution of the Ising model  

**Applications (3 lectures):**  
- Metropolis-Hastings algorithm for Monte Carlo simulation of the Ising model  
- Calculating different physical quantities in this context  

### Kinetic Theory (4 Lectures)
- Liouville's Theorem  
- Boltzmann equation  
- Conservation Laws  

*Note:* The number of lectures assigned for each topic is tentative and may change depending on student progress. The list of topics is indicative and may be adjusted.

## Objectives and Learning Outcomes

The objective of this course is to teach students how to formulate a mathematical formulation of a physical problem and select appropriate methods to solve it. Laboratory sessions will supplement lectures, providing opportunities to apply theoretical concepts.
