Flexible and Printed Electronics Lab

Lab Experiments

1. Analysis of surface properties in flexible substrates for printed electronics.

Objective: To evaluate surface wettability for printed ink adhesion on flexible substrate using contact
angle measurement.

Outcome: Understand surface energy relevance in ink spreading and adhesion.

2. Development of conductive ink for flexible electronics and printing techniques.

Objective: To prepare a basic carbon or silver-based ink and test its properties.

Outcome: Learn principles of ink optimization for printing.

3. Printing resistive and/or capacitive tracks using conductive ink on flexible substrate to evaluate
their electrical behavior.
Objective: To design and print resistive and/or capacitive structures on flexible substrates using
conductive ink, and evaluate their electrical behavior.
Outcome: Understand material resistive/capacitive behavior along with tracking geometry effects.
4. Characterization of printed resistor using temperature-dependent resistance measurement to
demonstrates TCR (temperature coefficient of resistance) using 2-wire method.
Objective: To measure resistance of printed track as a function of temperature.
Outcome: Demonstrates TCR (temperature coefficient of resistance) using 2-wire method.
5. Electro-physical analysis of moisture-induced conductance in printed tracks.
Objective: To investigate how moisture exposure affects the resistance of printed conductive tracks.
Outcome: Hands-on experience with resistive sensor characterization along with understanding of
environmental effects on printed electronics.
6. Development of flexible electronics circuitry using conductive ink.
Objective: To fabricate a simple flexible electronics circuit using conductive ink on a flexible substrate,
and then assemble and test a basic electronic circuit.
Outcome: Develop an understanding of how flexible electronics can be used in real-world applications.
7. Bending and reliability testing of printed conductive tracks.
Objective: To study how repeated mechanical bending affects the electrical resistance of printed
conductive tracks on flexible substrates.
Outcome: Understanding mechanical reliability of flexible and printed electronics.
8. Fabrication and characterization of a flexible strain sensor using conductive ink.
Objective: To fabricate a flexible strain sensor using conductive ink (e.g., carbon, graphene etc.) on a
flexible substrate and study its response under applied strain.
Outcome: Students will be able to measure and analyze the change in electrical properties with strain,
demonstrating the sensor’s sensitivity and flexibility.

IT486: Blockchains and Cryptocurrencies (3-0-0-3)
Autumn 2025-26

Course Placement
Open to students in the final year of the B.Tech and M.Tech programs.

Pre-requisites: You should know the basics of cryptography.

Instructor: Anish Mathuria

Objectives: This course explores the fundamentals of blockchains and cryptocurrencies.

Course topics
Topics covered include blind signatures, anonymous electronic cash, decentralized
cryptocurrencies, proof of work, Bitcoin blockchain, bitcoin transactions, Script language,
micropayments, lotteries, cryptocurrency exchange, proof of solvency, atomic currency swap,
mining, mining pools, chain splits, ECDSA/Schnorr signatures, signature aggregation, Pedersen
commitment, confidential transactions, simplified payment verification, hierarchical key
generation, unlinkable transactions, coin mixing, ring signatures, transaction malleability, smart
contracts, and Ethereum.

Course materials
We will use selected papers and references available online. The lecture slides will be made
available in the Lecture folder as the course progresses.

The following references are useful.
 Bitcoin and Cryptocurrency Technologies: A Comprehensive Introduction, Narayanan,
et. al. Princeton, 2016. ISBN: 978-0691171692.
 Programming Bitcoin, Jimmy Song, O’Reilly, 2019. ISBN: 978-93-5213-808-1.

Evaluation (tentative)
 20% pop quizzes (in-lecture)
 40% two in-sem exams
 20% assignments (written + programming)
 20% term paper

Course Outcomes
Upon completion of the course students should be able:
 to understand properties of blockchains and their applications
 to understand the fundamental principles and algorithms underlying bitcoin
 to understand the limitations of cryptocurrencies such as bitcoin
