# PhD Comprehensive Examination Formats – Engineering PhD vs HSS PhD

## Overview

The comprehensive examination format differs significantly between the Engineering/Science PhD program and the Humanities & Social Sciences (HSS)/Design PhD program at DAU. This document explicitly describes both formats and their differences.

## Engineering / ICT / Science PhD – Comprehensive Examination Format

### Format (2017-18 rules, still foundational)

The Engineering PhD comprehensive examination consists of **two parts**:

| Part | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Part 1: Written Qualifier (Qualifier Exam)** | A written examination common to all Engineering PhD candidates in the broad discipline of ICT, related areas, Mathematics, and Natural/Computational Sciences. The syllabus is broadly based on the foundation areas of ICT, or the compulsory courses for B.Tech/M.Sc entry candidates. |
| **Part 2: Proposal Defense** | A research proposal presented by the student to an expert committee. Must be completed no later than **6 months** after the candidate clears the qualifier exam. The proposal defense is customized per student. |

### Key Rules (Engineering PhD)

- **Exam board:** Constituted by Dean (AP) in consultation with PGC; consists of **3 to 5 faculty members**.
- **Eligibility:** Minimum CPI of **7.0/10** required to sit for the exam.
- **Timing:**
  - B.Tech/M.Sc entry: May appear earliest after Semester 2; must pass before start of Semester 5
  - M.Tech/M.Phil entry: May appear earliest after Semester 1; must pass before start of Semester 4
- **Maximum attempts:** **2 attempts**; failure in both leads to discontinuation from the program.
- **Oral component:** The qualifier is written; the proposal defense is oral/presentation-based.
- **PGC responsibility:** The PGC organises the comprehensive examination for all eligible students each semester.

## HSS / Design PhD – Comprehensive Examination Format

### Format (2024-25 rules)

The HSS and Design PhD comprehensive examination consists of **two parts**, but with a different structure:

| Part | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Part 1: Written Examination** | A written examination; the syllabus is **set per student** by the PhD comprehensive committee (not a common syllabus across all HSS students, unlike Engineering). |
| **Part 2: Oral / Presentation** | An oral examination or presentation; also personalised per student. |

### Key Rules (HSS PhD)

- **Syllabus:** The comprehensive examination syllabus is **set individually** for each HSS PhD student by their respective comprehensive examination committee — there is no uniform common syllabus.
- **Exam board:** The respective comprehensive examination committee is responsible for organising the examination for each PhD student.
- **Eligibility:** Minimum CPI of **7.0/10** required (same as Engineering).
- **Maximum attempts:** **2 attempts** (same as Engineering); failure in both leads to discontinuation.
- **Timing:** Similar to Engineering (appearance window depends on entry qualification).

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| Feature | Engineering / ICT / Science PhD | HSS / Design PhD |
|---|---|---|
| **Part 1 Format** | Written Qualifier (common syllabus) | Written Examination (student-specific syllabus) |
| **Part 2 Format** | Research Proposal Defense (oral, within 6 months of clearing Part 1) | Oral / Presentation |
| **Syllabus** | **Common** to all Engineering PhD students in ICT/related domains | **Individualised** per student, set by their committee |
| **Organizing Body** | PGC (organises for all eligible students each semester) | Respective comprehensive examination committee per student |
| **Exam Board Size** | 3 to 5 faculty members | Set by committee (per student) |
| **Min CPI Required** | 7.0/10 | 7.0/10 |
| **Max Attempts** | 2 | 2 |
| **Post-qualifier step** | Proposal defense ≤ 6 months after qualifier | No separate proposal defense step |
| **Nature** | Two-stage sequential (qualifier → proposal defense) | Two-part combined (written + oral) |

## Key Distinction

> **The most significant difference is that the Engineering PhD qualifier uses a COMMON syllabus across all ICT/Engineering PhD candidates, while the HSS PhD examination syllabus is SET INDIVIDUALLY for each student by their specific committee. Additionally, the Engineering exam has a separate sequential Proposal Defense step, whereas the HSS exam integrates the oral component directly.**
