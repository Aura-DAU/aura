## Detailed Course Content


- Course Abstract: PC 722 FOD 1 for MDes CD 2 module is to acquaint students with the means and methods of approaching, investigating and solving problems creatively by providing them with appropriate design and conceptual skill sets – one such primary skill being that of ‘Drawing and Observation’ and ‘Composition’. Drawing and observation are fundamental to design because they are the primary tools for understanding and translating the world around us. They help designers move from abstract ideas to concrete, viable solutions. Observation is the bedrock of the design process. It's about more than just looking; it's about seeing, and it’s about a mindful, purposeful study of people, objects, and environments. By observing users in their natural habitats, designers can gain empathy and uncover unspoken needs, pain points, and behaviors that people may not even realize they have. This direct insight is crucial for creating designs that are truly user-centred. Observation also helps designers to gather data, identify opportunities by noticing what is and what is not working and find inspiration through observing nature, art, and daily life that can spark new ideas and creative connections between disparate concepts. Following on from observation, Drawing is the language of design. It's not about creating a perfect piece of art, but about a practical tool for thinking and communicating. Drawing gives shape to abstract ideas, allowing designers to quickly externalize and refine their thoughts. As a critical skill drawing helps in visual thinking and work through problems, explore multiple solutions, and develop ideas rapidly. It's far faster and more flexible than using digital tools in the early stages of a project. The act of drawing forces you to look at a subject more closely, paying attention to details like proportion, form, light, and texture. This trains your eye to see the world with a designer's perspective. By adding a layer of critical and reflexive thinking, drawing becomes a tool for communication – a rapid way to visually communicate the idea, something that is visual and tangible, which otherwise would be challenging to use words to communicate. Course Abstract: PC 722 FOD 1 for MDes CD 3 While Part A: Drawing and observation trains the eye to see and the hand to represent, Part B: Composition introduces the critical process of strategically arranging of elements to create a cohesive, clear, and impactful message. A good composition helps one control how a user's eye moves through a design, ensuring they see the most important information first. Without it, a design can feel chaotic, cluttered, and confusing, making it difficult for users to understand its purpose. Composition relies on several key cognitive principles (for example the Gestalt Theory) that work together to create an effective design. Composition looks at visual hierarchy which is the order in which a viewer's eye is drawn to elements. One can create a visual hierarchy by adjusting size, colour, contrast, and position to make some elements stand out more than others. Using the broad concepts if visual balance, contrast, rhythm and harmony and integrating with concepts of proximity and repetition the design can be perceived as a structured group. By grouping related items, one makes information easier to scan and understand. By using consistent elements like a specific colour palette, typography, or spacing, the design creates a sense of unity and reinforces a communication and identity, making the design feel cohesive and professional. By integrated negative space (or White Space) it gives the user's eye a place to rest, prevents the design from feeling cluttered, and can be used to emphasize a focal point by isolating it from other elements. For any design, whether digital interfaces or analog products, composition directly impacts user experience. It determines whether a user can quickly find what they're looking for and accomplish a task. A good composition increases comprehension with a well-composed design reducing cognitive load. The visual hierarchy guides them, making the information easy to process. It also improves usability by can creating intuitive layouts. This leads to higher conversion rates, lower bounce rates, and a more pleasant experience for the user. Beyond function, a good composition is simply more visually appealing. It creates a sense of harmony and professionalism that builds trust and makes the user want to engage with the design. Course Abstract: PC 722 FOD 1 for MDes CD 4 This module emphasizes the latitude and value of individual thinking and one’s ability to observe and see, which would enable you to apply yourself creatively in solving problems. Composition in design terms is defined as structured, thoughtful, compatible placement or arrangements of elements in space (2-dimension/3-dimension/4-dimension) and/or time. Composition allows us to incorporate varied elements, combining them into reasonable and working whole – an example that can be referred to here can be that of cooking – by combining varied elements in a particular manner and in particular proportions it produces a palatable preparation. Good composition conveys information, tells a story, draws attention to the important and useful bits, segregates data, brings in elements of aesthetics and leaves an indelible imprint in the mind’s eye.
