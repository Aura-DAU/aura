## Course Description
Time Series Analysis is an elective course offered to third and fourth-year students of the B.Tech program.

This course introduces students to the essential tools and concepts in time series analysis, covering both time and frequency domains. Time series typically arise in many processes that require consecutive measurements of quantities. Understanding the basic structure and information of a time series requires concepts often used in signal and image processing, statistics, dynamical systems, and statistical physics. The course aims to develop a working foundation at the undergraduate level that enables systematic analysis of time series arising in various disciplines. The course primarily focuses on traditional statistical methods used in time series analysis. In addition, it aims to expose students to modern challenges and selected nonlinear methods that are used extensively in the research literature. The course will primarily focus on EEG and financial time series to develop a more hands-on understanding of the concepts discussed in class.

A single textbook is insufficient for this course; multiple reference resources are used (see Textbooks and References).

## Course Structure
Note: the source document explicitly caveats "* The number of topics covered will depend on the overall enthusiasm and commitment of the class."

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Overview and Introductions: Examples of time series, Trends in Time Series (Parametric, Non-parametric trends, Periodicity, seasonality, etc.) | 2-3 |
| 2 | Fundamental Concepts from Probability and Random Variables: Time Series and Stochastic Processes, Covariance and autocorrelation, etc. | 2-3 |
| 3 | Autocorrelation processes and the spectrum of stationary processes: Basic properties and linear processes, Introduction to ARMA models | 3-4 |
| 4 | Linear Stationary Models: Autoregressive and moving average processes, as well as mixed autoregressive and moving average processes | 4-5 |
| 5 | Linear Nonstationary Models: Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average processes, Integrated Moving Averages processes | 5-8 |
| 6 | Spectral Analysis of Time Series: Spectral Representation and Spectral Distribution, Estimating the Spectrum | 5-8 |
| 7 | Other Topics: Time Series models of Heteroscedasticity, ARCH, GARCH and Stochastic Volatility Models, Nonlinear models, Some concepts from nonlinear dynamical systems, etc. | Not stated (no lecture-count range given for this item) |

The Project component allows applying the learning in the course to a real data set. A relevant published paper must be identified in the first month of the semester; students will reproduce the key results in the paper and then further analyze the data set. Group projects are encouraged.

**Attendance Policy:** A minimum of 75% attendance (of all lectures in which attendance is taken) is mandatory. There will be a grade point penalty for students who do not meet the minimum attendance requirement.

**AI Policy:** Use of AI is permitted for the course project, but all submitted work must be genuinely produced and owned by the students. Each group must be able to explain every detail of their project independently and without relying on AI during evaluation.
