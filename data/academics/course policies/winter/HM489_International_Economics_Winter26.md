## Course Description

The aim of this course is to provide students with a grounding in the principles of international economics so that they understand the world trade and payment system. The course covers the pure theory of trade and extensions thereof. Open-economy macroeconomics, including the determination of the exchange rate, is discussed, and consequences of capital mobility are explored. The balance of payments adjustment policies under alternative exchange-rate regimes is discussed. Political economy issues in trade are also introduced.

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction and International Trade Theory: Globalization of the World Economy, Mercantilism, Absolute advantage, Ricardian model of Comparative Advantage, Factor endowments and Hekscher-Ohlin theory, Rybczynski theorem | 6 |
| 2 | Imperfect Competition and International Trade: Economies of Scale, Monopolistic competition and Trade, Significance of Intra-industry Trade, and Dumping | 4 |
| 3 | International Trade Policy: Basic Tariff Analysis, Effects of a Tariff (Consumer and Producer Surplus), The Partial Equilibrium Analysis of Tariffs, The General Equilibrium Analysis of Tariffs, Other Instruments of Trade Policy | 6 |
| 4 | Political Economy of Trade Policy: Political Arguments for Free Trade (Free Trade and Efficiency), International Trade Agreements (from the GATT to WTO), Free Trade Areas versus Customs Union, Trade Creation and Trade Diversion | 4 |
| 5 | Exchange Rates and Open Economy Macroeconomics: Foreign Exchange Markets and Equilibrium in the Foreign Exchange Market, Exchange Rate and Relative Prices, Interest Parity: The Basic Equilibrium Condition, Effects of Exchange Rate Changes on Expected Returns | 6 |
| 6 | Price Levels and Exchange Rates: Price Levels and the Exchange Rates in the Long Run, The Law of One Price, Purchasing Power Parity, Absolute PPP and Relative PPP, Problems with PPP | 5 |
| 7 | Balance of Payments, Foreign Exchange Markets and Trade Elasticities: Output and the Exchange Rates in the Short Run due to Changes in Monetary and Fiscal Policy, Effects of a Devaluation on the Trade Balance, The J Curve Hypothesis, Exchange Rate Pass-through and Inflation, The Marshall-Lerner Condition, Monetary Approaches to BOP | 6 |
| 8 | International Monetary System: International Macroeconomic Policy under Gold Standard 1870-1914, The Bretton Woods System and the IMF, Central bank interventions in fixed and flexible exchange rate regimes, capital mobility | 5 |

HM489 International Economics is an Elective course for third-year (6th semester) B.Tech students, with 3 hours of lecture every week.

---

## Additional Notes

- The course integrates both classical/neoclassical trade theory (Ricardian model, Heckscher-Ohlin) and modern open-economy macroeconomics (exchange rate determination, PPP, Marshall-Lerner condition).
- The course covers the historical evolution of the international monetary system, from the Gold Standard (1870-1914) through the Bretton Woods System to modern central bank interventions.
