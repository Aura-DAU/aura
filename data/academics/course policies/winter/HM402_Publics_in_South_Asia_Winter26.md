## Course Description

We all assume that we are part of something called "the public," and assume its existence in our roles as students, teachers, "fans," citizens/"netizens," readers, spectators, and so on. Nobody invites us to become members, nor can anyone take away our participation in the public, active or passive. Publics are at the heart of the democratic imagination: just as we cannot imagine modern politics without democracy, democracy has little meaning without "the public."

Why and how did this come to be? What are the various practices and technologies through which we come to be members of publics? What are the spaces and representations of people, cultures, and events through which we may identify and analyse publics? Who draws the line between the public and the private, and why is that line important? What significance do national boundaries and histories have for such analyses?

This course is based on exploring these fundamental questions with the help of readings taken from recent ethnographic work by anthropologists of Bangladesh, Nepal, Pakistan, India, and Sri Lanka, aside from historical accounts and political theory. We will see whether there are threads of similarity which run through the social worlds of class, caste, gender, and sexuality in the region, and how these affect our ways of thinking about publics, publicity, and privacy. We will read about and work with the written word, films, music, and radio performances to discuss these questions.

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| I | Introduction: Framing Concepts — Enlightenment Legacies: What is Public Reason?; Habermas and the Public Sphere Debate; Where is South Asia?; Publics and the Public Sphere in India: Debates | 10–12 |
| II | Space — Public and Private Space; Urban spaces: class and gendered mobility; Digital publics | 10 |
| III | Words in the World — The printed word: language and group identity; Time and Circulation; Scandal and Gossip: circulation by other means | 10 |
| IV | Arts of the Public — Popular Music and Performance Art; Cinema posters and visual cities; Female stardom; censorship and sexuality | 10 |
| **Total** | | **~40-42** |
