## Course Description
**What is game theory?** Game theory analyzes situations in which the decisions and actions of two or more individuals (or firms, political parties, countries) are interdependent. The foundational assumption is that decision-makers are rational and reason strategically — meaning their reasoning takes into account their knowledge and expectations of other decision-makers. While originally developed for economics, Game Theory has proven essential for understanding conflict and cooperation in management, political science, law, biology, and recently, in the design of agentic AI systems.

**Methodology:** Delivered through conceptual lectures, supported by Jupyter notebook model demos and in-class game sessions.
- **Course Project:** Creates hands-on experience in identifying game-like problem situations, creating an evolutionary game model for them, and using the model to devise remedial measures.
- **Logistics:** Students must enrol in Google Classroom to access course materials and instructions. All communication will occur via this platform.
- **Technical Workflow:** Project submissions must be Jupyter notebooks coded in Python and executed using Google Colab within the Google Classroom/Drive environment.

## Course Outcomes (COs)
This course aims to provide a systematic introduction to the tools of game theory and its applications. By the end of the course, students will be able to create mathematical and computational models to understand:

| CO# | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | Market Dynamics: Competition among firms, Duopolies and oligopolies. |
| 2 | Strategic Behaviour: Credible deterrence strategies, Benefits of being unpredictable, Negotiation and bargaining, Strategic voting. |
| 3 | Information Economics: Eliciting information, Principal-agent games, Moral hazard in insurance, Signaling games and advertising, Adverse selection and the use of warranties. |
| 4 | Mechanism Design: Incentive design, Auction design, Product bundling. |
| 5 | Evolutionary Models: The evolution of cooperation and free-rider elimination. |

Students will demonstrate these outcomes by developing their own evolutionary game models for specific situations, documented as Jupyter notebooks.
