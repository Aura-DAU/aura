## Course Description

This course will introduce students to the economic, social, and cultural phenomena of globalization. Based on the understanding of the implicit relationship of productive processes to society and history, the course will present to students a series of cases, mainly drawn from Asia, to understand the multiple sites and forms of globalization.

**Course Objectives:**
- Unit One will introduce students to the concepts, definitions, and historical origins of the processes of globalization in the 20th century.
- Unit Two will focus on the complex travel of information, goods, technologies, ideologies, and people around the world — and how these are apprehended and negotiated by people in specific locations.
- Unit Three will draw attention to the argument that while globalisation is a formidable force for change, there are also equally formidable factors that resist it.
- Unit Four will introduce students to debates on migration, technology, media, and the environment.

Students will have the opportunity to investigate a relevant topic of their choice and apply social-scientific explanatory frameworks in a final paper.

---

## Course Outcomes (COs)

The intensive reading and analysis that the course expects from students is intended to make them critically engage with issues of social and economic "change" they see around them. At the end of the course, they will be expected to develop a deeper understanding of specific questions pertaining to globalization and social change, such as:
- Is change always a desired and set goal?
- Does it follow any standardized or uniform agenda?
- Is globalization an innate drive towards socio-cultural homogenization?
- Or does globalization invariably lead to cultural and social fragmentation?
- What is the defining role of communication technology in both driving the processes of globalization and generating resistance to it?

## Course Structure

A sequence of themed lectures will cover essential ideas the course intends to impart. Related readings help students understand the basic ideas, approached with critical attention rather than mere simplification — particularly readings portioned as discussion readings. Each unit of study will have a lecture or two and a set of case studies. Student groups will develop presentations based on readings and research conducted at both the individual and group level.

| Unit | Topic |
|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction; Making Sense of Globalization |
| 2 | Capitalism and Neoliberalism |
| 3 | Historical and Anthropological Frameworks |
| 4 | The Antecedents of Globalization — Colonialism and Imperialism |
| 5 | The Revolution in Communication Technologies |
| 6 | Transnationalism and Human Flows |
| 7 | The Global Flows of Culture as Pleasure and as Product |
| 8 | Cosmopolitanism and Localism: Culture and Resistance |
| 9 | Against Globalization |
