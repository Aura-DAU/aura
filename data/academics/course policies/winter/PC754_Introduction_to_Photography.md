## Course Description
Course Placement: Core course for MDes (CD) Semester 2. This is a specialised course in design and is aimed at integrating inputs provided in previous courses in semester 1 and 2 into a "multimedia" matrix and orient the students to its multimodal application.

Course Format: Module based. The course is taught as a combination of Lectures and Studio sessions. Lectures introduce concepts, which are then analysed and explored via studio sessions and assignments. Lecture and studio is integrated and in certain cases can overlap. Teaching is through hands-on learning and through learning-by-doing and via real-time demonstrations and guidance by faculty members. Students will be expected to conduct fieldwork.

**Course Objective and Content:** The Photography course aims to provide a holistic understanding of photography as a subject of historical study, a medium of capturing the world, and as a communication tool. A variety of photographic approaches, both current and historical, will be explored. The course introduces the 'why' (creative and communication aspects) and the 'how' (technical aspects) of photography, covering the mechanics of generating an image and the function of the camera using the DSLR as the representative device, with focus on the digital medium against traditional chemical photography, and subsequent image manipulation using software like Photoshop.

**Course Abstract:** Photography is the method of recording images by the action of light or similar radiation on a material or device sensitive to it. The term "Photography" (Photos = Light, graphein = to draw) was coined by John F. W. Herschel in 1839. The course briefly traces the history of image-capture technology: Ibn Al-Haytham's pinhole camera (Camera Obscura) around 1000 AD, built on Aristotle's earlier observations on optics (~330 BC); and the first "photograph," taken by Joseph Niépce in 1827 on a bitumen-coated engraving plate, which began the chemical-photosensitive-agent experimentation that led to the photographic camera and film.

The module explores photography as art, product, documentation, evidence, science, and experiment, building on previously learnt skillsets to reframe an image through observation, content, context, composition, colour and light, developing aesthetic sensibility and storytelling through still images. It also introduces the mechanics of taking a photograph — digital camera settings, use of light, image correction/manipulation, and the exposure triangle (aperture, ISO, shutter speed, depth of field) — and exposes students to Adobe Photoshop for post-production. The course culminates in a student exhibition of selected work for the community.

## Course Outcomes (COs)
The course aims to equip the students with professional knowledge – creative, narrative and technical – involving the art and craft of field and studio photography. This course prepares the students for professional application of photography as a communication and storytelling device and lays the groundwork for further specialized courses in this field.

**Knowledge and Skill targets, as listed in the source document:**

Knowledge: Understanding of film; film language and grammar; film semiotics; constructing a narrative; translation of thought into narrative to physical product; understanding sound; diegetic, non-diegetic, trans-diegetic and intra-diegetic sound; sound and emotion; visualising and planning sound; sound, image, text and culture; impact of sound, image and text in communication design; deploying as multimedia assets; film as a communication product; different film formats and their deployment; lighting; language of edit.

Skill: Scripting and narrative construction; production planning; camera use and understanding technical parameters and results; understanding light and its use; editing (visual and sound); recording and creating soundscape; video editing software; sound and image editing software; multimedia integration software; organising sound recording setup.
