## Course Description

This course introduces students to the fundamentals of creating mathematical models of physical systems and implementing them on computers to analyze the system, both to gain insight and to make predictions. The different mathematical approaches to modeling covered in the course can be characterized into differential and difference equation-based models, probability-based models (including stochastic differential equations), cellular automata and event-based approaches, and matrix-based models. The course is interdisciplinary in nature and looks at systems from physics, biology, social science, and engineering, among others, from a modeling perspective. Each topic is followed by examples from different disciplines. Students are expected to test and create models of these systems in the lab, gaining intuitive understanding and reporting on it following the complete modeling approach.

**Approach followed:** system → Analyze the problem (determine problem's objective) → Formulate model (Gather data, make assumptions, determine equations/functions) → Solve model (Computer implementation) → Verify and interpret solutions → make predictions.

---

## Course Outcomes (COs)

After successful completion of the course, students would be able to create a relevant model for a multitude of problems from science and engineering, by extracting the necessary and relevant information regarding the problem. They would also be able to define the different modeling terms by analyzing the system or the data that is present. They would be able to implement the model on the computer and, from the results, check the validity of the model and correctness of the assumptions present in the model. They should be able to analyze the outcomes (mostly through visualizations) and make predictions. They would also be able to understand the limitations of their model and nuances in computer modeling of systems.

## Course Structure

*Note: "The number of topics covered will depend on the overall enthusiasm and commitment of the class," per the source document.*

| Unit | Topic |
|---|---|
| 1 | Overview of Computational Science: The Modeling Process; Model Classification; Steps in the modeling Process |
| 2 | System Dynamics Model (Compartment Models): Unconstrained growth models; Constrained Growth; Applications (Radioactive decay, Drug dosage, population dynamics, growth of technologies, SI models for epidemic spread, etc.); System of Equations; Models from Epidemiology (SIR, SEIR, SEIZ etc., predator-prey models, modeling spread of infectious disease such as SARS, Malaria); Models from Social Science (Rumor spread, Models for Innovation Diffusion, etc.); Selective methods of analyzing nonlinear differential equations |
| 3 | Stochastic models (Monte Carlo simulations): Review of basic probability; Generating random numbers from different distributions on computer; Brownian motion and random walk in 1D, discrete and continuous time stochastic differential equation with applications; Symmetric and asymmetric random walk, Markovian and non-Markovian random walk; Markov Chains, Continuous Time Markov Chains, Birth and death processes, limiting time behavior |
| 4 | Agent-based Simulation (ABM): Cellular Automata simulation of random walk; Ising Model for ferromagnetic Systems with applications to social science, Metropolis algorithm; Applications (Modeling opinion formation on 2D lattice, segregation models, Rumor spread models, etc.) |

*No lecture-count breakdown per unit is available in the source document.*
