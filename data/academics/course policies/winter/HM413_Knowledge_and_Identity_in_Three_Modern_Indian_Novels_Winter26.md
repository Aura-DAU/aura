## Course Description

This course has been visualised for undergraduate students without an intermediate academic background in the liberal arts stream. It aims to encourage and contextualize some of the natural questions that might arise in a young mind regarding individual identity, as framed in Indian traditions and knowledge systems.

This course belongs to the academic category of the Indian Knowledge System (IKS). It will examine the concept of individual/communitarian identity within the Indian Knowledge System, which originated in the Indian subcontinent and evolved over time through interactions with other schools of thought, facilitated by political and cultural exchanges spanning two thousand years. This question of identity and knowledge systems will be explored through its depiction in three iconic modern Indian novels: *Saraswatichandra*, *Gora*, and *Aag Ka Dariya* (*River of Fire*), written by Govardhanram Tripathi in Gujarati, Rabindranath Tagore in Bengali, and Qurratulain Hyder in Urdu, respectively. The course engages with their English translations, but students are encouraged to read them in their original versions depending on their competency in Gujarati, Bangla, and Urdu.

It is essential to note that the course will not focus on a close reading of the entire body of the texts to be studied, especially given their considerable length. The course will focus on the theme of the course as explored in the three novels rather than on the study of the literary aspects of the novels in their entirety.

Within this broad framework of the Indian Knowledge System, the course — visualised around the concept of Identity as imagined and defined in the Indian knowledge system — aims to understand its relevance, outline, substance, and significance in a fast-changing present, and, in the Indian context particularly, its epistemic position in the context of representation and participation across genders, classes, and communities. The course also incorporates concepts such as truth, conscience, violence/non-violence, responsibility, and deprivation, from the perspective of their specific natures in Indian knowledge systems in interaction with modern thought, as depicted in these three important works by three influential figures in Indian literary history.

## Course Outcomes (COs)

The course aims to achieve the following outcomes:

| CO | Description |
|---|---|
| CO1 | Introduction to Indian Knowledge System. |
| CO2 | Understanding the ethical human responsibility for nurturing a peaceful comity of races and communities. |
| CO3 | Understanding knowledge as a repository, imagination, introspection, and evolution, and identity as a combination of a historical core and reflective process evolving through advancing knowledge arising from interaction among human communities — an intrinsic part of Indian knowledge systems, reflecting a universe intricately connected in both its creation and destruction. |
| CO4 | Knowing and understanding that this recognition is one of the highest functions of human consciousness — celebrating heritage while remaining mindful of its universal human characteristics, and developing sensitivity to global ecological concerns, commonality of concerns, and transnational moral claims to basic amenities across cultures. |

**Learning Outcomes:** The course aims to acquaint students with the formative themes that these three thinkers/authors have explored and contributed to the modern concept of India, and to identify their central arguments about identity as defined within the Indian knowledge system. Students are encouraged to raise their individual questions regarding the art and arguments of these three authors.

## Additional Notes

- **Attendance and Participation:** A minimum attendance rate of 60% is compulsory. The attendance policy will be strictly enforced, and students who fall short of the required attendance percentile will be subject to a grade reduction.
- **Mode of Teaching:** The course is organized around the concept of Indian identity as explored by the three authors, divided into four broad modules (one background module and three modules centred on each novel respectively).
- **Plagiarism and Academic Integrity:** Using words and phrases of authors without proper citation is considered a violation of norms of academic integrity and is strictly discouraged. Genuine effort and commitment will be duly appreciated.
- This course shares the same instructor (Gangeya Mukherji) and a very similar structural approach (background module + three thematic modules on individual works, similar evaluation weighting) as HM405 (The Idea of India) and HM410 (Debating Right and Wrong in the Mahabharata) from the Autumn 2025-26 batch.
