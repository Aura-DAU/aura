## Course Description

This is a core humanities/social sciences course which includes elements of both microeconomics and macroeconomics. It aims to develop an understanding of basic concepts of resource allocation, opportunity costs, and market failure. Students would also get an introduction to macroeconomic concepts such as national income accounting, unemployment, and inflation. References to monetary, fiscal, and foreign exchange rate policies will help students gain a greater understanding of the role of central banks and governments in the economy.

---

## Course Outcomes (COs)

After completion of the course, students should be able to:

| CO | Description |
|---|---|
| CO1 | Identify and distinguish between real-world examples of different types of markets, including monopoly, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, and perfect competition. |
| CO2 | Analyse market efficiencies, consumer surplus, producer surplus and social welfare in economic markets. |
| CO3 | Apply macroeconomics principles to estimate the welfare of countries, including the determination of GDP, standard of living and unemployment rates. |
