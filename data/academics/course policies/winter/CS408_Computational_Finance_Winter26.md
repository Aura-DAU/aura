## Course Description

A derivative is a financial instrument that derives its price from an underlying asset like a stock. An example is a forward contract where two parties agree to buy/sell an asset at a fixed price at some future date. Another example is a call option, where one party (option buyer) agrees to buy an asset from another party (option seller) at a future date — but in this case the contract is asymmetric: the option buyer has the right but no obligation to exercise the option, whereas the option seller is obligated to exercise the option at the end date.

How do you value such a financial contract? The value of the option is the option price of the call option. There is a well-developed mathematical theory of option pricing, based on principles of efficient markets and the mathematical theory of probability. The Black-Scholes-Merton model of pricing a European call option is a landmark work in financial and economic theory, which received the Nobel Prize for economics in 1997. Current financial markets are replete with complicated financial instruments; exotic options and option exchanges are commonplace. Billions of dollars of options are traded every day on various exchanges around the world, on underlying assets like stocks, currencies, or commodities. This course introduces students to the theory of option pricing and financial derivatives.

## Course Structure

| Sr. No | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce stock markets, importance of returns, information efficiency/arbitrage | 2 |
| 2 | Forward contracts and call and put options and option price determinants | 6 |
| 3 | Single period Binomial model. Equivalence between hedging, replication and risk-neutral valuation. Multi-period model | 6 |
| 4 | Random walks, scaled random walks and Geometric Brownian Motion. Introduction to stochastic calculus: Ito's lemma | 6 |
| 5 | Black-Scholes PDE via hedging argument and Ito's lemma. Black-Scholes PDE via the risk-neutral valuation argument | 4 |
| 6 | Solution to Black Scholes via Heat Equation | 1 |
| 7 | Basics of Greeks and their calculation | 2 |
| 8 | Monte Carlo methods for option pricing | 3 |
| 9 | Finite difference methods: Explicit, Implicit and Crank-Nicholson | 4 |
| 10 | American and Exotic options | 3 |
| 11 | Jump diffusion model for stock pricing | 1 |
| **Total** | | **38** |
