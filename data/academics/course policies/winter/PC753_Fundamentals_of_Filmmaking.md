## Course Description
Course Placement: Core course for MDes (CD) Semester 2. This is a specialised course in design and is aimed at integrating inputs provided in previous courses in semester 1 and 2 into a "multimedia" matrix and orient the students to its multimodal application.

Course Format: Module based. The course is taught as a combination of Lectures and Studio sessions. Lectures introduce concepts, which are then analysed and explored via studio sessions and assignments. Lecture and studio is integrated and in certain cases can overlap. Teaching is through hands-on learning and through learning-by-doing and via real-time demonstrations and guidance by faculty members. Students will be expected to conduct fieldwork.

**Course Objective and Content:** The objective of this course is to introduce the various aspects of filmmaking – from concept till production. The course will synthesize and integrate the theoretical inputs the students have received in course "Multi-modal Storytelling," technical and creative inputs during "Basics of Photography" and "Image, Text, Sound" course, and expose them to the professional methodology and structured approach of constructing a filmic narrative and its execution, leading to a final finished film — whether it is a fictionalized story or a story from the real world in the documentary genre.

The core learning from this course is to gain greater appreciation of planning, budgeting, logistics, human-resource management, storytelling and narration, and integrating different visual (and auditory) mediums into a coherent communication.

**Course Abstract:** The art of filmmaking is a complex process requiring perfect synthesis of multiple elements from domains of storytelling, narrative, photography, design, technology, physics and management. The course examines "continuity of filmic time and space" through the history of filmic narrative construction — from the actuality films of the Lumiere Brothers through Edwin S. Porter and D. W. Griffith, Pudovkin's theories of Constructive Editing, Eisenstein's Thematic Editing and Intellectual Montages, and Andre Bazin's appreciation of neo-realist film form — as well as the parallel development of Indian cinema. Based on this historical overview, the course explores principles of filmic construction (narrative, continuity, cutting point, image size and angles, sense of direction, overlapping, cross-cutting), 'real' time versus 'reel' time, and how cinematography, lighting, sound and editing assist in creating continuity. Students will script, storyboard and direct short fiction films and documentaries, following the three-stage film production routine: pre-production, production, and post-production. The course also covers video as a technological medium (versus film production) and basic lighting/cinematography techniques using a video camera.

## Course Outcomes (COs)
The course aims to equip the students with professional knowledge – creative, narrative and technical – involving the art and craft of videography. This course prepares the students for professional application of videography as a communication and storytelling device and lays the groundwork for further specialized courses in this field.

**Knowledge targets:** Understanding of film; film language and grammar; film semiotics; constructing a narrative; translation of thought into narrative to physical product; understanding sound; diegetic, non-diegetic, trans-diegetic and intra-diegetic sound; sound and emotion; visualising and planning sound; sound, image, text and culture; impact of sound, image and text in communication design; deploying as multimedia assets; film as a communication product; different film formats and their deployment; lighting; language of edit.

**Skill targets:** Scripting and narrative construction; production planning; camera use and understanding technical parameters and results; understanding light and its use; editing (visual and sound); recording and creating soundscape; video editing software; sound and image editing software; multimedia integration software; organising sound recording setup.
