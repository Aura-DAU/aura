## Course Description

Plato is a charming writer of philosophy in the form of dialogues. He wrote more than thirty dialogues, ranging from short to long ones, which deal with almost all the important problems and topics of philosophy. More importantly, all his dialogues contain, in varying degrees, literary embellishments in the form of striking character portrayals, powerful metaphors and analogies, drama, and vivid exchanges, and so on. In this course, we'll study a few Platonic dialogues with the aim of understanding Plato's controversial views on politics and democracy.

## Weekly Schedule / Lecture Plan

*Note: No weekly schedule with dates is available in the source document.*

**Mode of Teaching:** The course will be organized around several issues which arise from each dialogue studied. Lectures will help students critically approach the assigned readings. Discussions are aimed at targeting specific aspects of the readings — such as ideas, difficult passages, and arguments. Freewheeling discussions are not encouraged at all. There will be periodic quizzes. Independent study and initiative will be highly encouraged, with required guidance provided.

**Writing:** Students will be asked to write three short papers, varying between 1000 to 3000 words each, over the course of the semester. The word/page limit will be specified at the time of assigning the task.
