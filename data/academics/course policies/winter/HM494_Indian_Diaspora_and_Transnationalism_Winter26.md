## Course Description

According to the Ministry of External Affairs of India, thirty-two million two hundred eighty-five thousand four hundred twenty-five Indians live abroad, making it the largest diaspora in the world. Indians have existed transnationally for centuries. Although initially it was mostly through trade, the concept of international migration is familiar to India. The nature of migration changed over time and circumstances. Various cultural, political, and economic developments transformed the social rubric of India. As a result, the process of migration emerged as one of the means to tackle those unexpected challenges.

India's first major tryst with migration began during the 19th-century colonial period, when people from different states migrated to several other "colonies" of the British as indentured labourers. Over the years, the nature of diasporic communities became more layered, as individuals of Indian origin made formidable "connections" with other members of the community — both in their host land and homeland. Indians across the diaspora are linked by a shared "Indian-ness," an enduring sense of cultural belonging, and the willingness to maintain a close association with home. This, however, does not come without its own set of drawbacks — for members of the diaspora, homeland, and "hostland."

The course is designed to familiarise students with, and critically engage with, the topics of present-day diaspora, transnationalism, and international migration. Comprehensive themes include the formation and historical background of the Indian diaspora, overseas experiences of Indians, links with homeland, kinship, and ethnicity.

## Course Outcomes (COs)

As an introductory course, the aim is to familiarise students and help them critically engage with concepts of diaspora, transnationalism, and international migration. The four themes explained as part of this course attempt to trace the journey of members of the diaspora to express themselves — collectively and individually, their position in the "host land," and their changing equation with the concept of "home" and "homeland." The course also discusses the formation of migrant subjectivity and the factors that impact it, thereby addressing the various concerns that members of the diaspora navigate in their daily lives. The objective is also to understand how the Indian diaspora's engagement with their place of origin changed over the years, and the complex reality of being "diasporic."

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Indian Diaspora: How Did It Come to Be? — Notion of 'diaspora' and 'diasporic communities'; ethnographic case studies central to the emergence and development of 'Diaspora'; origins and formation of the Indian diaspora across ancient, modern, and post-modern phases. Sub-themes: (1) What and who does the term 'Diaspora' mean? (2) Emergence of the Indian Diaspora | 10 |
| 2 | To Be Here and There, At Once: Introduction to Transnationalism — the consistent pattern of maintaining a 'connection' with the place of origin (physical or virtual) among diasporic communities, known as 'transnationalism.' Sub-themes: (1) What is transnationalism? What does it mean to have a 'simultaneous existence'? (2) Indian Diaspora and the effect of transnationalism on everyday life | 10 |
| 3 | Longing to Belong?: Unpacking Diasporic Identity — the process of migration and identity based on ethnicity ("who you are" and "where you are from"), and resulting conflicts manifesting as transnational activities, love for the homeland, and a sense of belonging. Sub-themes: (1) Multiculturalism (2) Class and Caste in Diaspora — Select Case Studies | 10–12 |
| 4 | Indian Diaspora in Bollywood Cinema — the two-way exchange between Bollywood Cinema and the Diaspora, including how the Indian diaspora is portrayed on screen and how diasporic communities relate to 'Bollywood'; concepts of kinship, everyday life, relationships, and hybrid identities among the diaspora, discussed through screening of select movies. Sub-themes: (1) Introduction to Bollywood and Indian Diaspora (2) Movie Screening(s) | 10 |
| **Total** | | **~40-42** |
