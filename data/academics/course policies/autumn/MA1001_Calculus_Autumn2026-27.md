## Course Description

The course introduces the fundamental concepts of calculus and numerical methods, providing a rigorous foundation for mathematical analysis and scientific computation. It develops an understanding of limits, sequences, series, continuity, and differentiability, followed by the study of single-variable and multivariable differential calculus. Students learn the principles of integral calculus, including the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and the properties of definite integrals. The course emphasizes analytical techniques such as Taylor series expansions and optimization in one and several variables. It also introduces numerical methods for differentiation and integration, along with their accuracy and stability considerations. Through theoretical concepts and practical computational approaches, the course equips students with essential mathematical tools for engineering, science, and data-driven applications.

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| Foundations of Limits, Sequences, and Series | Functions and their graphs; limits of sequences and series; convergence criteria; comparison tests for series; limits of functions; continuity of functions of one real variable; differentiability and basic derivative rules | 6 |
| Single-Variable Differential Calculus | Derivative as the best linear approximation; rules of differentiation; Mean Value Theorem; Taylor's theorem and Taylor series; applications of derivatives to find maxima and minima; curvature intuition | 9 |
| Integral Calculus | Fundamental Theorem of Calculus; definite integrals; Darboux sums and approximation; properties of integrable functions; elementary integrals and interpretations | 7 |
| Multivariate Calculus | Functions of several real variables; multivariable limits and continuity; partial derivatives; gradient and directional derivatives; differential as best linear approximation; Hessian matrix, second-order behaviour; multivariable Taylor expansions; optimisation in multiple dimensions | 10 |
| Numerical Differentiation and Integration | Finite-difference approximations for first and second derivatives; numerical integration methods including trapezoidal rule and Simpson's rule; error intuition and stability considerations | 7 |
| **Total** | | **39** |
