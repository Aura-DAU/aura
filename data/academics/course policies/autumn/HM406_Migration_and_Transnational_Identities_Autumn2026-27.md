## Course Description

In an ever more interconnected world, migration generates new identities, enriches political, social, and cultural experiences, and plays a pivotal role in their formation. The extensive reach and influence of migration compel us to reconsider essential aspects of social structures, moving beyond traditional concepts like families and nations to embrace the complexities of transnational communities.

This course explores migration by delving into conceptual frameworks such as ethnicity, transnationalism, multiculturalism, technology, and cinema. Case studies are employed to illustrate contemporary inquiries into multiculturalism, diversity, and a sense of belonging, explaining the reciprocal effects of multiculturalism on people and society, and vice versa.

## Additional Notes

**Outcomes and Objectives (as stated in source, beyond the CO list):** The course aims to cultivate intercultural perspectives by examining the social and cultural aspects of globalisation, transnational migration patterns, and multiculturalism. Students will also be acquainted with the social and cultural changes that migration brings about. The objective is to provide a detailed understanding of how multiculturalism functions in everyday life and how it impacts people from different backgrounds. The course builds on previously taught concepts, such as kinship, class, and gender, within a broader framework of multiculturalism.
