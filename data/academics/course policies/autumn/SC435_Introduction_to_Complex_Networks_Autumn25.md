## Course Description

Many systems spanning multiple disciplines are composed of many interacting components. Examples include neurons in the brain, interaction of people, entities in markets, businesses, and computers interacting over the internet. Universality and emergence are two essential properties of such systems. Two directions of exploration, which are also intertwined, are analyzing large-scale data and constructing simple models. Complex networks provide a way to look at/develop understanding/explore/draw conclusions in such varied systems. It also forms the basis of many machine learning and deep learning methods, making these tools essential for large-scale data analysis and mathematical/computational modeling professionals. This course aims to provide a rigorous foundation and practical implementations of the concepts learned.

Since many problems are interdisciplinary, the course has elements of computer science, statistical physics, social sciences, and biology. The course covers three main topics of network science: (i) network structure and metrics, (ii) network models, (iii) dynamical processes on networks.

The course includes a strong project component. The project aims to apply class discussion to various online network databases across any field (social science, finance, biological systems, computer science, physics, etc.). Students work in small groups, using available data or generating model-based data, to implement, characterize the network, and identify relevant metrics, algorithms, or procedures.

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction to Complex Network: Introduction to Networks, Empirical study of Real-world networks (social, Technological, Biological, Information and neural networks). Concept of hubs | 2–3 |
| 2 | Basic Concepts in Graph Theory: Network representation (adjacency matrix and edge lists), Types of Networks (Weighted, directed, bipartite, planar network), Graph Laplacian | 5–6 |
| 3 | Measures and metrics: Degree, betweenness and closeness centrality, Eigenvector centrality, PageRank and HITS, Katz centrality, Groups of vertices, Transitivity, Clustering coefficient, Reciprocity, Signed edges and structural balance, Similarity and mixing | 5–7 |
| 4 | Community Structure: Dividing network into groups, Modularity Maximization, Algorithms for Community Detection (Greedy algorithms, Spectral methods, Kernighan-Lin, Louvain Method), Hierarchical Clustering — Agglomerative (single-linkage, complete linkage, average linkage) and Divisive algorithms (edge betweenness method) | 4–5 |
| 5 | Large scale structure of networks: Components, shortest path and small world effect, Degree Distributions, Power laws and scale-free networks | 5–6 |
| 6 | Models of Networks: Random Networks (Properties of ER network), Configuration model (arbitrary degree distribution, comparison with ER model), Barabási-Albert model (Preferential attachment model, mean field equation and master equation), Small world model (Watts-Strogatz) | 5–7 |
| 7 | Dynamical Processes on networks: Epidemic spread through basic compartmental models like SIS, SIR, etc. on networks; Diffusion on networks; Resilience of networks | 6–8 |
| **Total** | | **~32–48 (variable, per source note below)** |

*Note: "Total number of topics that will be covered will depend on the student participation and preparedness to understand new topics," per the source document.*

## Evaluation / Grading Scheme

| Component | Weightage |
|---|---|
| Mid-Semester Exam | 30% |
| Final Exam | 30% |
| Project | 40% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

**Project details:** Students will implement a relevant computational paper from the literature. The outcome of the project includes: (i) documented and working code for the project (existing libraries can be used); (ii) a report on the work carried out with proper citation to the references used to develop understanding; (iii) viva/presentation.

**Attendance Policy:** Any student with less than 75% attendance in lectures will not be allowed to appear for the final exam.

**Plagiarism and Cheating Policy:** Plagiarism and/or cheating will result in disqualification from the course.
