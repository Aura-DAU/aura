## Course Description

This course is composed of two parts, taken consecutively: **Part A: Typography** and **Part B: Colour**.

This module deals with the advanced aspects of colour, composition, virtual 3D spaces, Typography as image, and Illustration for information design, as means to explore, visualize, and communicate complex ideas with high levels of data density. It presents an entry point to moving image and sound analysis, structure, methodology, concepts, and experimentation for communicating specific ideas, focusing on mediums including cinema, animation, multimedia, and Sound Design for 2D and virtual 3D environments. Students will also engage with 'Design History' as a means of understanding visual language applied across sketching, photography, film, and animation.

**Typography** is the art and technique of arranging type to make written language legible, readable, and visually appealing. It involves selecting typefaces (fonts) and adjusting spacing between letters (kerning), words (tracking), and lines (leading). Key elements include typeface, font variations, kerning, tracking, leading, hierarchy, alignment, and contrast. Good typography significantly affects how a message is perceived, enhances readability, establishes visual hierarchy, conveys tone and emotion (e.g., serif fonts as formal/classic vs. sans-serif as modern/clean), and is central to brand identity and digital user experience.

**Colour** (Part B) aims to sensitise students to the 'idea' of colour, understanding that there is no single, absolute colour — colour is shaped by light, culture, perception, biology, emotion, chemistry, and physics. The module teaches colour theory, colour derivations, shades, tints, tones, colour harmony, and colour interaction. Colour plays a crucial role in shaping visual and emotional experience, brand identity, accessibility (e.g., text/background contrast), and carries cultural meanings that vary across regions (e.g., white symbolizing purity in Western cultures vs. mourning in some Eastern cultures). Colour theory principles (complementary, analogous, triadic schemes) guide designers toward harmonious, visually engaging combinations, adding another dimension to the composition principles explored in FOD 1.

## Course Outcomes (COs)

The course aims to equip students with professional knowledge — creative, narrative, and technical — on the fundamental building blocks of the design approach: (Part A) colour, colour usage, colour psychology; (Part B) typography, type and its meaning, type and emotion, type and usage — along with integrating aspects of FOD 1 and FOD 2 in a final segment where these concepts, skills, and knowledge are applied in a final project (e.g., designing a poster or complex visual representation). This course prepares students for professional application of these basic guidelines, applied in all design fields going ahead.

**Knowledge outcomes:**
1. What is colour
2. History of Colour
3. Colour and emotion
4. Colour interaction
5. Colour and culture
6. Colour and Design
7. Typography and its component
8. History of typography
9. Type and emotion
10. Type and design
11. Type as display, type as body text, digital type
12. Type and scale
13. Integration — judgement, interaction and decisions

**Skill outcomes:**
1. Colour application and mixing
2. Hue, Saturation, tint, shade
3. Greyscale and RGB Wheel
4. RGB vs CMYK
5. Physical colour vs screen colour
6. Using colour for representation
7. Elements of type — size, leading, kerning, spacing etc.
8. Type narrative

## Course Structure

**Growth Matrix (Exercise Flow):**

| Flow | Exercise | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exercise 1.1: Understanding shade & tint | To understand and appreciate how black and white behaves, as well as tool usage — brush, paint, palette, fluidity, paper |
| 2 | Exercise 1.2: Understanding colour — primary, secondary, tertiary using a colour wheel | To understand primary, secondary and tertiary colours |
| 3 | Exercise 1.3: Understanding colour interaction | Understanding what colour works with what |
| 4 | Exercise 1.4: Application of colour knowledge through deconstruction of a painting and changing the colour to change the emotion | Understand colour and emotions |
| 5 | Exercise 1.5: Colour and abstraction — listen to a musical piece and draw it in colour | Applying colours based on feelings |
| 6 | Exercise 1.6: Colours in 3-Dimension | How colours work in the 3-Dimensional space |
| 7 | Exercise 2.1: Understanding typography | To understand the elements of typography |
| 8 | Exercise 2.2: Elements of type — deconstruct and reconstruct a type to understand elements like x-height, etc. | To appreciate the derivation of form and shape of type and the stories behind them |
| 9 | Exercise 2.3: Compare a serif and a san-serif type | What do each different types of type communicate |
| 10 | Exercise 2.4: Using type — leading, kerning, spacing, etc. | How to use these features in a composition |
| 11 | Exercise 2.5: Type in a composition | How is type used to communicate |
| 12 | Exercise 2.1 (final, numbered again as "2.1" in source): Integration through spread, poster, or infographic design | Fundamentals of design I & II applied in this exercise |

*Note: The final exercise in the source document is labeled "Exercise 2.1," which duplicates the numbering of the earlier "Exercise 2.1" (Flow 7) — this repetition exists in the source document itself.*

## Additional Notes

- **Course Format:** Module-based, taught as a combination of Lectures and Studio sessions, with hands-on, learning-by-doing pedagogy and real-time demonstrations/guidance by faculty.
- **Attendance is mandatory** — students must be present during class hours.
- All assignments must be completed.
- **Use of any form of AI tools to generate concepts is strictly forbidden** unless discussed in class and explicitly allowed for a specific assignment.
- Students are encouraged to link the use and significance of colour to the concurrent course in Culture and Communications (cross-referencing cultural colour connotations).
