## Course Description

Design is an interdisciplinary activity — characterized by the constant preoccupation of co-relating disciplines, dealing with and trying to understand the complex and interesting worlds of the physical, biological, imaginary, and human, all of which are multivariate in character. 'The Fundamentals of Design 1' course is an attempt to sensitize students to this complex, dynamic, and multidimensional scenario — to expand the focus of their discipline and place them within the larger context and discourse of design. The main objective of this module is to acquaint students with the means and methods of approaching, investigating, and solving problems creatively by providing them with appropriate design and conceptual skill sets — one such primary skill being that of 'Drawing and Observation' and 'Composition.'

This course is composed of two parts, taken consecutively: **Part A: Drawing and Observation**, and **Part B: Composition**.

Drawing and observation are fundamental to design because they are the primary tools for understanding and translating the world around us. They help designers move from abstract ideas to concrete, viable solutions. Observation is the bedrock of the design process — not just looking, but seeing, through mindful, purposeful study of people, objects, and environments. By observing users in their natural habitats, designers gain empathy and uncover unspoken needs, pain points, and behaviors.

Drawing is the language of design — a practical tool for thinking and communicating rather than for creating perfect art. It gives shape to abstract ideas, allowing designers to quickly externalize and refine their thoughts, and helps in visual thinking, exploring multiple solutions, and developing ideas rapidly.

Part B: Composition introduces the critical process of strategically arranging elements to create a cohesive, clear, and impactful message. A good composition helps control how a user's eye moves through a design. Composition relies on cognitive principles such as Gestalt Theory, visual hierarchy, balance, contrast, rhythm, harmony, proximity, repetition, and negative space (white space).

This module emphasizes the latitude and value of individual thinking and one's ability to observe and see, enabling creative problem-solving. Composition is defined as the structured, thoughtful, compatible placement or arrangement of elements in space (2D/3D/4D) and/or time.

This is a critical foundational course in design.

## Course Outcomes (COs)

The course aims to equip students with professional knowledge — creative, narrative, and technical — on the fundamental building blocks of the design approach: observation, representation, and composition. This course prepares students for professional application of these basic guidelines, which will be applied in all design fields going forward.

**Knowledge outcomes:**
1. Observation of objects, nature, indoor, outdoor
2. Observation and imagination
3. Hand, eye, perception
4. Representation, Perspective & Proportion
5. 1-point, 2-point & 3-point perspective
6. Analytical and isometric drawings
7. Human sketching and capturing motion
8. Distilling to capture the essence
9. Composition and its rules
10. Principles of Composition: Balance, Contrast, Harmony, Rhythm, Repetition, Proximity, Negative Space, Figure-ground
11. Gestalt Theory and its application
12. Application of Gestalt Laws
13. Nature of 2D and 3D spaces
14. Composition in the real world

**Skill outcomes:**
1. Drawing & Sketching skills
2. Understanding tools — pencil, pen, paper, ink, brush
3. Quality, precision and professionalism
4. Visualisation and eye-trace training
5. Judgement
6. Communication
7. Reflexive thinking

## Course Structure

**Growth Matrix (Exercise Flow):**

| Flow | Exercise | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exercise 1.1: Understanding tools | To understand and appreciate the different tools and how they behave |
| 2 | Exercise 1.2: Observation & Sketching | To see and perceive |
| 3 | Exercise 1.3: Observation from nature | To record ever-changing conditions of nature |
| 4 | Exercise 1.4: Perspective | Understand the different forms of perspective and how to record them in a 2D space |
| 5 | Exercise 1.5: Human drawing | Understanding proportions, posture, movement, shape and form of human figures and how they are perceived in space |
| 6 | Exercise 2.1: Principles of composition — in a 20cm × 20cm square, articulate Balance, Contrast, Rhythm, Harmony, Motion, Pattern, Focus, Proportion using lines, blocks (square, triangle, circle), and newspaper cutouts (text only, not images) | To understand principles of composition |
| 7 | Exercise 2.2: Organisational aspects (Gestalt rules) — in a 20cm × 20cm square, articulate Similarity, Continuation, Closure, Proximity, Figure-ground, symmetry & order using blocks and digital vector software | To understand organisational aspects of composition |
| 8 | Exercise 2.3: Choose a magazine spread or poster; analyse composition elements and explore rearranging them to create a new composition (using Photoshop/vector software) | To understand and appreciate how composition rules are used in 2D space |
| 9 | Exercise 2.4: Use mobile camera to shoot images off-campus communicating Similarity, Continuation, Closure, Proximity, Figure-ground, symmetry & order | To understand composition in the real world and train the eye to see it |
| 10 | Exercise 2.5: Observe a complex place (Pani-puri vendors, shopping mall, lotus pond, etc.) and understand how different elements work, their hierarchy, and present findings | To appreciate that design and composition permeate all aspects of life |
| 11 | Exercise 2.6: Choose one artwork from Exercise 4 and replicate it in 3D space using a paper box (minimum 20×20 cm) | To understand composition in space and how elements behave in relation to each other when placed in depth |

## Additional Notes

- **Course Format:** Module-based, taught as a combination of Lectures and Studio sessions. Lectures introduce concepts, which are then analysed and explored via studio sessions and assignments. Lecture and studio integration can overlap. Teaching is through hands-on learning, learning-by-doing, and real-time demonstrations/guidance by faculty.
- **Attendance is mandatory** — students must be present during class hours.
- All assignments must be completed.
- **Use of any form of AI tools to generate concepts is strictly forbidden** unless discussed in class and explicitly allowed for a specific assignment.
