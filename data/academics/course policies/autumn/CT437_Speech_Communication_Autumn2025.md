## Course Description

Speech communication is an interdisciplinary subject with different research areas ranging from electrical and communication engineering, linguistics, phonetics, psychology, computer science, etc. Speech communication is the transfer of information from one person to another or from person to machine via speech. This course equips students with different fundamental problems in speech science such as speech production process, speech perception, time-domain and frequency-domain processing, homomorphic speech processing and most popular linear prediction (LP) of speech and finally different diverse speech communication applications such as speech analysis, speech coding, speech synthesis, speech recognition and speaker recognition are discussed. The course is designed in such a way that relationships between different parts of speech communication can be understood easily. In addition to this, cohesive discussion on speech production and speech perception will help the audience to understand the speech communication process better.

**Course Outline**: The course will first review very briefly the basic concepts of DSP and random processes, followed by detailed discussion on speech production process from the viewpoint of physiological aspects to acoustic theory of speech production. The unique feature of the course will be a detailed discussion on aeroacoustic theory of speech production and development of the Teager Energy Operator (TEO) and recently proposed variable length TEO (VTEO).

**Course objective**: To study fundamentals of speech production (physiological, acoustical and aeroacoustic perspective), speech analysis methods - STFT, spectrograms, LP analysis, and cepstrum analysis. A case study on Automatic Speech and Speaker Recognition.

**Activity tree for the course (as stated in source)**: Basic Signals and Systems (background readings) branches into: (1) Speech Production — physiological factors, acoustics of speech production, Teager Energy Operator (TEO) and applications; (2) Speech Analysis — Short-Time Fourier Analysis, Cepstral Analysis, Linear Prediction (LP) Analysis; (3) Applications — speech coding, speech recognition, speaker recognition, language identification, speech synthesis, speech-to-speech translation.

**Key Laboratory Experiments (Tentative):**

| Lab | Topic |
|---|---|
| LAB1 | Introduction to MATLAB Programming |
| LAB2 | Speech Analysis: Reading Speech Waveform, Speech Framing at suprasegmental (< 4 ms), segmental (10-30 ms), and suprasegmental level (~200 ms) |
| LAB3 | Significance of Phase of Fourier Transform: A Case Study for Speech |
| LAB4 | Spectrographic Analysis of Speech, Audio, Music, and Infant Cry Signals — Narrowband vs. wideband spectrograms, Displaying Mel Spectrograms |
| LAB4 (duplicate numbering in source) | Estimation of Instantaneous Frequency (IF) using Hilbert Transform and Relevance to Speech Processing |
| LAB5 | Design of 2nd order Digital Resonator and its relevance to Speech Formant Estimation and their -3dB Bandwidths |
| LAB6 | Linear Prediction Analysis of Speech Wave: Estimation of Speech Excitation Source using LP residual, LP Spectrum vs. STFT Spectrum |
| LAB7 | Formant and -3dB bandwidth Estimation using LP Methods |
| LAB8 | Cepstrum Analysis of Speech Signals => Estimation of Voiced vs. Unvoiced Regions, Fundamental Frequency (F0) using Cepstrum, cepstrally-smoothed spectrum |
| LAB9 | Estimation of Fundamental Frequency (F0) using Autocorrelation, LP Residual, Hilbert Envelope, etc. |
| LAB10 | Case Study 1: Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) for Speech Applications |
| LAB11 | Case Study 2: Web-Scale Supervised Pre-training for Speech Recognition (Whisper) |
| LAB12 | Case Study 3: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for Speech Enhancement, Voice Conversion, etc. |
