## Course Description

This is a foundational course in design, aimed to integrate the inputs from previous courses into a 'multimedia' matrix and orient students to the application-oriented Semester 2 that follows.

This course aims to introduce elements of multi-media in Communication Design. It encourages students to explore how words, images, and sounds are put together for a range of communication events and forms. Students are expected to observe, research, document, and compose presentations deploying multi-media. They are encouraged to read and explore ideas from design aesthetics and semiotics to understand compositional styles and aesthetic choices in traditional as well as contemporary forms of communication media, in which visual, verbal, and aural elements are combined to make effective communication events.

In communication design, the integration of image, text, and sound is essential to creating effective, engaging, and immersive user experiences. This course investigates the individual and collective roles of these three elements in crafting messages that resonate emotionally, intellectually, and sensorially with the audience.

**Images** are integral to communication design because they convey meaning and emotion with immediacy — transcending linguistic barriers to communicate universal concepts. Photographs, illustrations, icons, and graphics evoke emotional responses, simplify complex information, and provide context and meaning.

**Text** remains the primary medium for delivering detailed information, articulating complex ideas, and guiding audience understanding, offering clarity and specificity through strategic typography (building on Fundamentals of Design 2) — conveying information, setting tone/voice, enhancing accessibility, and providing structure/navigation.

**Sound** is an often-overlooked yet powerful element of communication design, particularly in digital and multimedia contexts. It adds an additional layer of sensory engagement, enhancing emotional connection and user experience — through sound effects, music, and spoken word — establishing atmosphere and immersion in films, multimedia applications, games, websites, and haptic feedback in the digital realm.

When combined, image, text, and sound create a powerful, unified communication design platform, offering advantages through multisensory engagement, memory retention and recall, enhanced emotional impact, and improved user experience.

## Course Outcomes (COs)

The strategic use of image, text, and sound in communication design is fundamental to creating designs that are effective, engaging, and memorable. These elements, when used independently or in combination, enhance emotional engagement, clarify complex ideas, establish brand identities, and create immersive experiences. The synergy between image, text, and sound forms the backbone of modern communication design, offering a more dynamic and multisensory approach to conveying messages. By understanding the distinct and collective roles of these elements, designers can craft powerful, user-centered designs that resonate deeply with diverse audiences. This course is the precursor course to more complex and challenging courses like Fundamentals of Filmmaking (Semester 2), Interaction Design (Semester 2), and Animation and Motion Graphics (Semester 3).

**Knowledge outcomes:**
1. Understanding sound
2. Diegetic, Non-Diegetic, Trans-diegetic and Intra-Diegetic sound
3. Sound and emotion
4. Visualising and planning sound
5. Sound, Image, Text and culture
6. Impact of sound, image and text in communication design
7. Deploying as multimedia assets
8. Seamless user experience design
9. Recap of typographic concepts from FOD 2
10. Recap of visual design concepts from FOD 1 and FOD 2
11. Recap of History of Design

**Skill outcomes:**
1. Appreciating soundscape
2. Recording and creating soundscape
3. Integrating image and sound
4. Sound and image editing software
5. Multimedia integration software
6. Organising sound recording setup

**Software:** Adobe Premiere Pro, Adobe Animate, Adobe Audition, Audacity

**Hardware:** Sound recording equipment (zoom mic, microphones, boom rods, etc.), Sound studio

## Course Structure

**Growth Matrix (Exercise Flow):**

| Flow | Growth Matrix | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Growth Matrix 1: Understanding multimedia — exploring different modalities and synchronicity | To understand and appreciate the integrated nature of multimedia communication — as exists in our surroundings and as a way to replicate an experience |
| 2 | Growth Matrix 2: Understanding Sound as part of multimedia experience | To understand and appreciate how sound is structured and layered as part of audio modality |
| 3 | Growth Matrix 3: Recording sound | To understand how to deconstruct and reconstruct sound |
| 4 | Growth Matrix 4: Integrating sound with image(s) | Adding an additional layer of sensorial information to emphasise the communication |
| 5 | Growth Matrix 5: Integrating text with sound and image to create a multimedia experience | Multi-modal nature of communication design integrating text, image and audio |
| 6 | Growth Matrix 6: Integrating sound, text with a group of images to create a title sequence or an immersive multimodal experience | Integrating image, text and sound for a specific culture-specific edutainment communication package |

## Program Structure Context

PC749 Image, Text & Sound is a core, foundational course for MDes (Communication Design) Year 1, Semester 1, with a 3-0-4-5 credit structure. The course runs over 18 full days combining lecture and studio sessions, and serves as the precursor to Fundamentals of Filmmaking (Semester 2), Interaction Design (Semester 2), and Animation and Motion Graphics (Semester 3).

## Additional Notes

- **Course Format:** Module-based, taught as a combination of Lectures and Studio sessions, with hands-on, learning-by-doing pedagogy and real-time demonstrations/guidance by faculty. Students are expected to do fieldwork.
- **Attendance is mandatory** — students must be present during class hours.
- All assignments must be completed.
- **Use of any form of AI tools to generate concepts is strictly forbidden** unless discussed in class and explicitly allowed for a specific assignment.
- This course explicitly recaps and builds upon concepts from FOD 1 (visual design), FOD 2 (typography), and History of Design, integrating them into a multimedia framework.
