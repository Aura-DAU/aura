## Course Description

**Objective/Motivation:** Graphs have a wide range of applications across various fields. They have proven to be an invaluable tool in establishing results in numerous areas of mathematics and, by extension, in engineering. Many graph-theoretic proofs are constructive, leading directly to the development of algorithms for solving diverse problems and puzzles. An engineer with a solid understanding of graph theory and its algorithms is better equipped to address and solve a broad array of complex challenges efficiently.

---

## Course Outcomes (COs)

Students should be able to model and solve real-world problems algorithmically using graph-theoretic techniques, while also providing proof of algorithm correctness. In cases where efficient algorithms are unavailable, students should be capable of developing practical, effective alternatives. Furthermore, students should experience a notable improvement in both analytical and programming skills.

## Course Structure

*Note: The source document presents topics as a collection of fundamental theorems and algorithms rather than a numbered unit table with lecture counts:*

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| Theorems | Handshaking lemma, Havel-Hakimi theorem, Erdős–Gallai theorem, The matrix-tree theorem, Cayley-Kirchhoff theorem, Menger's theorem(s), Characterization of Eulerian graphs, Dirac's theorem, Ore's theorem, Chvátal's theorem, Hall's matching condition, König-Egerváry theorem, Tutte's theorem, Brooks' theorem, Vizing's theorem, Shannon's theorem, Robertson-Seymour theorem, Kuratowski's theorem, Five colour theorem |
| Algorithms | Graph traversal algorithm(s), Shortest path algorithm(s), Warshall's algorithm, Prüfer sequence and labeled tree construction, Minimum spanning tree algorithm(s), Fleury's algorithm, Colouring algorithm(s), Planarity testing algorithm(s), Finding strong components, Ranking in tournaments; for NP-complete problems, polynomial algorithms available for special cases will be discussed |

## Weekly Schedule / Lecture Plan

*Note: No weekly schedule with dates is available in the source document.*

**Lab Sessions:** Mostly unsupervised, involving writing graph algorithms, analysis and proof of correctness of these algorithms, and optionally computer programs for the algorithms.
