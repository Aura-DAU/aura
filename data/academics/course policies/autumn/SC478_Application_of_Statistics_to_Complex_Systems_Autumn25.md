## Course Description

Concepts of statistics have proved to be highly useful in probing the behaviour of different physical systems, typically consisting of a large number of particles. These were traditionally first developed in the context of thermal physics, but have subsequently been used in other diverse areas. This course is intended to introduce probabilistic approaches to studying systems with a large number of degrees of freedom. These systems can range from ideal gases, which consist of large numbers of particles, to modeling the dynamics of the stock market using unifying concepts such as entropy and ensembles. This course will introduce both the formalism needed for systematically studying such systems and many concrete applications wherein the techniques are applied.

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | Time Allotted (Lectures) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Concepts of Probability — Theory (5 lectures): General definitions and statistical terminology, Random variables, Probability Distributions, Probability distributions for large n, Information and Entropy, Random Walk and fat-tailed distributions. Applications in brief (5 lectures): Using random variables as a tool to study physical systems, Sampling from Probability Distributions, Monte Carlo Integration and applications in simple systems | 10 |
| 2 | Statistical Formulation of a Physical Problem: How to specify state of a system, Statistical Ensemble, Density of states | 6 |
| 3 | Concepts of Thermodynamics: Zeroth Law, First Law and Second Law of Thermodynamics, Entropy, Concept of equilibrium and thermodynamic potentials, Uses of the above concepts in areas such as finance and the stock market, Maximum entropy principle in complex systems | 7 |
| 4 | Classical Statistical Mechanics — Theory (10 lectures): Microcanonical, Canonical and Grand Canonical Ensembles, Partition Function, Ising model and its uses in physics and in other areas such as the stock market, Exact solution of the Ising model. Applications in brief (3 lectures): Metropolis-Hastings algorithm for Monte Carlo simulation of the Ising model and calculating different physical quantities in this context, Scaling and its applicability in complex networks | 17 |
| **Total** | | **40** |

## Program Structure Context

SC478 Application of Statistical Physics to Complex Systems is a Science Elective for B.Tech (Honours) ICT students and an elective for MnC students, with a 3-0-0-3 (3.0) credit structure, consisting of 3 hours of lectures each week.

---

## Additional Notes

- The objectives of this course are to teach students how to formulate a mathematical model of a physical problem and select appropriate methods to solve it. Laboratory sessions (where mentioned) mainly supplement lectures, giving students an opportunity to put theory into practice, rather than constituting a separately-graded lab component.
- The course connects statistical physics concepts (entropy, ensembles, the Ising model) to applications in finance and stock market modeling.
