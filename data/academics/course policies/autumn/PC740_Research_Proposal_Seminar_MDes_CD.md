## Course Description

This course is part of a two-course sequence to help students identify a communication problem, conduct research, and craft a narrative on the possibilities of a specific design intervention based on field and archival work, literature, and product reviews. This is the **first part** of the sequence — the second part being **PC745 – Research Applications: Constructing Narratives**.

**Course Themes:**
- Understanding the purpose(s) of Communication Design — to design multi-media content that informs, explains, argues, persuades, advocates, and inspires citizens and consumers about products, services, and ideas integral to their lives and livelihoods.
- Introduction to the "four orders" of communication: Government to citizen; business to consumers; political parties to voters; civil society to citizens — understanding the forms and intentions of each.
- Understanding the commercial vs. social implications of communication design, and introducing students to the 4 areas of Communication Design encouraged in the MDes programme: education, health, culture, environment.
- Introduction to social science research methods and Design thinking — literature review, ethnography, archival research, surveys.
- Discussion of possible Communication Design products — feature film, documentaries, animation, graphic design products in print, websites, mobile apps, social media content.

---

## Course Outcomes (COs)

At the end of the course, students are expected to have a deeper and more robust understanding of the role of Communication Design in society. They would use this understanding to identify a problem and write up a research proposal to develop a specific design product.

## Evaluation / Grading Scheme

Students are evaluated at three stages in the formulation of their Research Proposal: (1) at the stage of identification of a problem, (2) after research, and (3) after the final formulation of the proposal. At each stage, students write a paper and make a formal presentation in class for review by both instructor and peers.
