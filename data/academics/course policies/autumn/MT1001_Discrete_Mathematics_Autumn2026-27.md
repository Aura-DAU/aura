## Course Description

This course is about discrete mathematical structures and is designed as a theoretical course for BS/MS students in Computer Science. The primary aim of this course is to introduce students to the fundamentals of Discrete Mathematics. Students are expected to develop logical reasoning skills to solve a variety of problems, learn different methods of proof, understand basic algorithms for problem-solving, and learn discrete probability, number theory, and graph theory for applications in computer science and engineering contexts.

---

## Course Outcomes (COs)

On successful completion of this course, the students will be able to:

| CO | Description |
|---|---|
| CO1 | Understand and construct mathematical arguments using logic, proof techniques, and formal reasoning for solving problems |
| CO2 | Understand discrete mathematical structures such as sets, relations and functions, graphs, trees and learn their applications in computer science |
| CO3 | Apply combinatorial techniques such as permutations, combinations, recurrence relations, and counting principles to solve engineering problems |
| CO4 | Develop skills towards mathematical modelling and analysis of engineering problems |
| CO5 | Understand discrete probability distributions based on simple combinatorial processes, and calculate the probabilities and expectations of simple events |

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| Unit I: Basic Discrete Structures and Mathematical Logic | Set operations, functions, relations and their properties, equivalence relations, closure of relations, partial orderings, sequences and summations, matrices. Propositional logic, predicate logic and quantifiers, logic operators, logic proposition, introduction to different standard proof techniques | 8 |
| Unit II: Combinatorics and Counting Techniques | Combinatorial principles and techniques, permutations and combinations, binomial coefficients and identities, pigeonhole principle, inclusion-exclusion principle, recurrence relations, solving linear recurrence relations and applications | 8 |
| Unit III: Number Theory and Probability | Induction, strong induction, well-ordered property, structural induction, and generalized induction, modular arithmetic, primes and representation of integers, linear congruences, cryptography, RSA cryptosystem, basic discrete probability, probabilistic counting, conditional probability, Bayes' theorem, random variables | 10 |
| Unit IV: Graph Theory | Undirected graph, representation on the computer: adjacency matrix, handshaking lemma, Euler path and circuit, Hamiltonian cycle, complete graph, cycles, bipartite graph, planar graphs, Euler formula, shortest path algorithm, Traveling Salesman Problem, trees, properties of trees, spanning trees | 9 |
| Unit V: Boolean Algebra and Models of Computation | Boolean functions, logic gates, minimization expressions, K-maps, DFA, NFA, languages, regular expressions, grammars, PDA, introduction to Turing machines | 7 |
| **Total** | | **42** |
