## Course Description

This course introduces nonlinear dynamics to undergraduate engineering students. It first establishes the necessary theoretical and mathematical foundations and then focuses on developing practical problem-solving skills in a wide variety of domains (many of engineering, socio-economic, industrial, biological, and environmental relevance). The two broad classes of mathematical problems in this course pertain to one-dimensional and two-dimensional systems. Special emphasis is laid on first-order problems, as they provide technically accessible concepts that are nonetheless effective for analyzing a wide range of problems in depth. All theoretical concepts introduced in one-dimensional systems are supplemented strongly by practical examples and exercises. Two-dimensional systems are of an advanced nature, but they can be understood easily once the theoretical foundations of one-dimensional systems have been laid.

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Review Topics: Methodology of nonlinear dynamics and its broad perspectives; Differential equations and their dynamic features | 5 |
| 2 | One-Dimensional Systems: Autonomous systems, flows on the line and their dynamics; Fixed points and linear stability analysis; The logistic equation, tumour growth, Newton's law of cooling, etc.; Bifurcations — saddle-node, transcritical and pitchfork; Flows on the circle | 20 |
| 3 | Two-Dimensional Systems: Coupled autonomous systems, Phase portraits; Linearization, fixed points and their classification; Systems in social and biological dynamics (love affairs, arms race, predator-prey interactions, competitive exclusion, etc.); Conservative and reversible systems; Limit cycles, Bifurcation in two-dimensional systems; Introduction to chaotic systems | 15 |
| **Total** | | **40** |
