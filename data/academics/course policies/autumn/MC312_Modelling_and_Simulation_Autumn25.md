## Course Description

This course introduces students to the fundamentals of constructing mathematical and computational models of physical, biological, social, and engineering systems. The course covers differential and difference equation-based models, probability-based models, stochastic models, cellular automata, event-based approaches, and matrix-based models. The laboratory component requires students to implement models computationally, test assumptions, analyze outputs mainly through visualizations, and comment on validity and limitations. The course can serve as a foundation for domain-based specialization electives such as Computational Finance, Computational and Systems Biology, Computational Physics, and Computational Social Science.

## Course Structure

*APPENDIX-I: Detailed Course Content (Session-wise/Module-wise). Note: "The exact depth of coverage within modules may vary slightly depending on the pace and progress of the class."*

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| Module 1: Overview | Introduction to complex systems; the modelling process; model classification; steps in the modelling process | 2 |
| Module 2: System Dynamics Models (Compartment Models) | Unconstrained growth models; constrained growth; applications (radioactive decay, drug dosage, population dynamics, growth of technologies, and SI models for epidemic spread); systems of equations; models from epidemiology (SIR, SEIR, SEIZ, predator-prey models, and models for infectious diseases such as SARS and malaria); models from social science (rumor spread and innovation diffusion); selected methods for analyzing one- and two-dimensional nonlinear differential equations | 15-18 |
| Module 3: Stochastic Models and Monte Carlo Simulations | Review of basic probability theory; generating random numbers from different distributions on a computer; Brownian motion and random walk in 1D; discrete- and continuous-time stochastic models with applications; symmetric, asymmetric, Markovian, and non-Markovian random walks; Markov chains, continuous-time Markov chains, birth-death processes, and limiting-time behavior | 12-16 |
| Module 4: Agent-Based Simulation (ABM) | Cellular automata simulation of random walk; Ising model for ferromagnetic systems with applications to social science; Metropolis algorithm; applications (opinion formation on 2D lattices, segregation models, and rumor-spread models) | 8-12 |
| **Total** | | **37-48** |
