## Course Description

This course introduces students to the fundamentals of creating mathematical models of physical systems and implementing them on computers to analyze the system, gain insight, and make predictions. The different mathematical approaches to modeling covered in the course can be characterized into differential and difference equation-based models, probability-based models (including stochastic differential equations), cellular automata, event-based approaches, and matrix-based models. The course is interdisciplinary and looks at systems from a modeling perspective drawn from physics, biology, social science, engineering, etc. Examples from different disciplines follow each topic. Students are expected to test and create models of these systems in the lab, gaining intuitive understanding and reporting on it following the complete modeling approach.

---

## Course Outcomes (COs)

After completing the course, students would be able to create a relevant model for many problems from science and engineering by extracting the necessary information regarding the situation. They would also be able to define different modeling terms by analyzing the system or the available data. They would be able to implement the model on the computer and check its validity and the correctness of the assumptions present in the model. They will analyze the outcomes (mainly through visualizations) and make predictions. They would also be able to understand the limitations of their model and nuances in the computer modeling of systems.

After successful completion of the course, students will have the ability to:

| CO | Description |
|---|---|
| CO1 | Apply the knowledge of mathematics and computer science to model complex problems in science. [P1, P2, P3, P12] |
| CO2 | Carry out model-based investigations to derive insight into the problem at hand. [P4] |
| CO3 | Communicate effectively through vivas, course presentation, and report writing. [P10] |

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Overview: Introduction to Complex Systems; The Modeling Process; Model Classification; Steps in the Modeling Process | 2 |
| 2 | System Dynamics Model (Compartment Models): Unconstrained growth models; Constrained Growth; Applications (Radioactive decay, Drug dosage, population dynamics, growth of technologies, SI models for epidemic spread, etc.); System of Equations; Models from Epidemiology (SIR, SEIR, SEIZ etc., predator-prey models, modeling spread of infectious disease such as SARS, Malaria); Models from Social Science (Rumor spread, Models for Innovation Diffusion, etc.); Selective methods for analyzing one- and two-dimensional nonlinear differential equations | 15–18 |
| 3 | Stochastic models (Monte Carlo simulations): Review of basic probability theory; Generating random numbers from different distributions on computer; Brownian motion and random walk in 1D, discrete and continuous time stochastic differential equations with applications; Symmetric, asymmetric, Markovian and non-Markovian random walk; Markov Chains, Continuous Time Markov Chains, Birth and death processes, limiting time behavior | 12–16 |
| 4 | Agent-based Simulation (ABM): Cellular Automata simulation of random walk; Ising Model for ferromagnetic systems with applications to social science, metropolis algorithm; Applications (Modeling opinion formation on 2D lattice, segregation models, Rumor spread models, etc.) | 8–12 |
| **Total** | | **~37–48 (variable, per source note below)** |

*Note: "The number of topics covered will depend on the overall enthusiasm and commitment of the class," per the source document.*
