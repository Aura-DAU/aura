## Course Description

**Course Objective:** This course will introduce students to the emergent field of Design History or Design Studies as part of a broader attempt to instill in students a critical engagement with their discipline. It will map the evolution of interest in 'historical' questions related to Design, both as practice and as discipline. Before that, the course will try to understand the many meanings of Design in use and ask which of these helps understand the true nature of the practice.

**Course Structure (Description):** The course introduces students to a range of approaches to the study of the History of Design — particularly conventional, narrative approaches to Design History and conceptual and critical approaches to the same.

Students will be able to make the distinction between practice and discipline, even if both are interlinked, in order to understand how historians have approached the subject of their study. The module also includes an important connecting thread in the History of Design: the larger economic, social, political, and cultural contexts within which Design took shape both as practice and as pedagogy. There are two entangled strands of historical inquiry followed — one tracing the development of Design, and the other looking at the global and national contexts that shaped it.

These global contexts are indicated by historical periodizations such as the Industrial Revolution, the First Great War and the rise of Communism in Russia, the inter-war years, the rise of Fascism, the Cold War era, the era of Mass Consumption, the Counter-Culture, and the contemporary era of Globalization.

The final segment of the course turns to India and looks at the colonial context in which India found itself during the period of the Industrial Revolution. The course examines how the first consciousness of Indian-designed and made products was emerging in the context of the Swadeshi movement (1905–1911), the impact of the emergence of Gandhi in sensitizing us to the power of Design in nationalist politics, and finally the ways in which both modern western Design practice and pedagogy, as well as India's own craft traditions and material culture, served to shape the context within which India's first modern Design school was established in 1961.

## Course Outcomes (COs)

Toward the end of the course, students will try to understand how Design History helps engage with questions of form, function, and aesthetics in completely different ways and generate new ways of looking at the products and services consumed as part of everyday life. Students will seek to understand how Design History prompts us to question the larger implications of what we buy or use in terms of the impacts these have on human health and wellbeing, on the built environment, and on the planet at large.

## Course Structure

| Segment | Topic |
|---|---|
| 1 | Understanding the many meanings of Design in use |
| 2 | Approaches to the study of the History of Design: conventional/narrative approaches vs. conceptual/critical approaches |
| 3 | Distinction between Design as practice and Design as discipline |
| 4 | Global and national contexts shaping Design: Industrial Revolution, First Great War and rise of Communism in Russia, inter-war years, rise of Fascism, Cold War era, era of Mass Consumption, Counter-Culture, contemporary era of Globalization |
| 5 | Final segment — India: colonial context during the Industrial Revolution; the Swadeshi movement (1905–1911); Gandhi's impact on Design in nationalist politics; the establishment of India's first modern Design school in 1961, shaped by both modern western Design practice/pedagogy and India's own craft traditions and material culture |

## Textbooks and References

### Selected Readings

1. Nikolaus Pevsner, *Pioneers of Modern Design* (1936; revised ed. 1960).
2. John Heskett, *Industrial Design* (1980) and *Design: A Very Short Introduction* (2002).
3. Jonathan Woodham, *Twentieth Century Design* (1997).
4. Victor Margolin, *World History of Design* (2015, 2 vols.).
5. H.K. Kumar Vyas, *Alternative Histories of Design* (essays).
6. S. Balaram, *Thinking Design*.
