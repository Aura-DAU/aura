## Course Description

This course provides foundational mathematical concepts required for M.Sc. Agriculture Analytics students who have studied mathematics up to secondary (10th) level. The emphasis is on geometric intuition, concrete examples from agriculture, and simple computational skills to prepare students for further coursework in analytics and statistics.

**Course Objectives:**
1. Reinforce essential mathematical ideas required for quantitative courses in analytics.
2. Provide geometric and intuitive understanding of vectors, functions, calculus, and probability.
3. Equip students with basic skills to interpret and manipulate simple mathematical models relevant to agricultural data.

## Course Structure

| Module | Topic | Hours | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Linear Algebra (Basics) | Vectors as arrows; representation of points; linear equations; 2×2 matrices and operations; solving small linear systems by elimination; visual intuition for span and direction | 8 | Visual + computational |
| 2. Sets, Relations, Functions | Set operations, Venn diagrams, Cartesian product, relations and properties, mapping and functions, domain and range, plotting simple functions | 5 | Visual examples |
| 3. Calculus (Elementary) | Concept of limit intuitively; derivative as slope/rate; simple derivative rules and interpretation; increasing/decreasing; integration as area under curve (Riemann idea with rectangles); basic applications | 8 | Geometric intuition + computational |
| 4. Probability (Introductory) | Random experiments; sample space; events; axioms (informal); simple/combinatorial probability; conditional probability and independence (basic intuition); expectation (simple cases) | 7 | Applications in agriculture (rainfall, germination) |
| **Total** | | **28** | |

## Program Structure Context

MA614 Preparatory Mathematics is a 2-credit bridging/preparatory course for M.Sc. Agriculture Analytics Semester 1 students, requiring mathematics up to secondary (10th) level as a prerequisite. It prepares students for further coursework in analytics and statistics.

---

## Teaching–Learning Methods

- Blackboard/Openboard demonstrations with step-by-step geometric sketches.
- PPT slides as class notes, and for summary and graphs.
- In-class short exercises and discussions using agricultural examples (crop yields, rainfall patterns, simple surveys).

## Additional Notes

- The course is explicitly designed as a foundational/bridge course, emphasizing geometric intuition over rigorous proofs, given its target audience of students with only secondary-level mathematics background.
- Agricultural context examples are integrated throughout (e.g., rainfall and germination probability examples, disease-given-moisture conditional probability examples).
- The course is well-documented, including a full lecture-wise plan with per-lecture activities and learning outcomes, in addition to a module-level breakdown.
