## Course Description

The course will cover the evolution of India into a plural, multi-cultural, and democratic nation over the centuries. It will have a brief introduction to Multi-culturalism, Secularism, and Diversity in Indian society. It will describe some important events, personalities, and acts which led to India's emergence as a democratic, secular republic. The relationship between Indian culture and its philosophical traditions with the constitutional principles will be brought out. Relevant portions from Indian and western philosophy will be covered to explain democracy, secularism, socialism, and the role of religion in Indian society.

The course will comprise topics related to the making of post-independence India, such as general elections, five-year planning, ethnic and religious conflicts, along with other social and national issues. Important issues such as Communalism and Left Wing Extremism will be included, related to governance and security problems facing India. The course will further include the structure and functioning of Central and State governments, the Panchayat system, and local self-government. There will be exposure to governance at the grassroots and district level.

The course will also contain exposure to State police organization, its functioning, and Central Police Organizations (CPOs). It will introduce the National Security Structure and its components. Serious social problems like Communalism and Corruption, related to day-to-day governance, will be covered. There will be brief exposure to Coastal Security.

This course will be beneficial to those students trying to gain better social awareness about present times and who have some interest in finding their positive role in society. It will be especially useful to students who choose to go into the social sector and use their engineering background to make a difference in society.

**Course Objectives:**
1. To understand the evolution of India as a nation with reference to Democracy, Socialism, Equality, and Secularism as mentioned in the Indian Constitution, and to give social, philosophical, and cultural context in which the Indian political system has evolved.
2. To help students understand the functioning of union, state, and local self-government and the associated issues.
3. To provide an understanding of the National Security Structure, response mechanism, and working of security agencies.
4. To give insight into some contemporary social and security problems such as communalism, naxalism, and corruption.
