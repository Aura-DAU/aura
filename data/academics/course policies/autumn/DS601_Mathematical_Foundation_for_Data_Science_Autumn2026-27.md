## Course Description

This course provides a strong foundation of the important mathematical concepts for modern data science, covered in three primary modules: M1) Probability and Random Variables, M2) Linear Algebra, and M3) Calculus. It empowers students with the mathematical insight needed to move beyond using data science tools to understanding, designing, and improving data-driven and ML algorithms.

Course Format: Three lecture sessions and one tutorial session per week.

---

## Course Outcomes (COs)

After successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| M1A: Basics of Probability | Probability definition, sample space, events, principles of counting, sampling table, axioms of probability, inclusion-exclusion principle, independence, conditional probability, Bayes theorem (prior and posterior; probabilities of hypotheses when given evidence), law of total probability, Simpson's paradox | 6 |
| M1B: Random Variables, Probability Distribution | Random variables (Bernoulli, discrete uniform, binomial, geometric), discrete and continuous RVs, PMF, binomial distribution, PDF, expected value of a RV and of a function of a RV, linearity of expectation, variance and standard deviation, CDF, multiple random variables and joint PMFs, normal/Gaussian RV (standard normal, using areas-under-curve tables), general normal RV; optional: exponential RVs and PDF, memorylessness of exponential distribution, Poisson distribution | 10 |
| M2A: Vector Spaces and Subspaces, Matrix | Data as vectors, operations on vectors, linear dependence and independence, spaces of vectors, subspaces, span, basis and dimension, vectors and linear combinations, norms, representation of linear transformations as a matrix; four fundamental subspaces of a matrix and their dimensions, transpose, rank, determinant, matrix inverse, determinants (properties, determinant of a triangular matrix, determinant of a large matrix via product of pivots), trace; special matrices (square, identity, triangular, sparse/dense, symmetric) | 6 |
| M2B: Solving Linear Equations | Vectors and linear equations, the idea of elimination, elimination using matrices, rules for matrix operations, inverse matrices, Gauss elimination, Gauss-Jordan elimination, row reduced form, row reduced echelon form, factorization A = LU, transposes and permutations, the nullspace of A (solving Ax = 0 and Rx = 0), the complete solution to Ax = b | 6 |
| M2C: Orthogonality and Matrix Diagonalization | Orthogonality of the four subspaces, projections, least squares approximations, orthonormal bases and Gram-Schmidt, eigenvectors and eigenvalues, diagonalizing a matrix, SVD | 5 |
| M3: Calculus | Functions, continuous functions, differentiable functions, functions of several variables, gradient of a function and directional derivatives, minima of a convex function, gradient descent method | 5 |
| Case Studies using Real Data | Case studies applying the above mathematical concepts to real data | 2 |
| **Total** | | **40** |
