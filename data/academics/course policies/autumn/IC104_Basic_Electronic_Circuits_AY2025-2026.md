## Course Description

The objective of this course is to provide students the fundamental idea of electronic circuits and components. The course will enable students with the skills of working with different circuit elements like resistor, capacitor, inductor, diode, transistor, and operational amplifier. The content is designed to help students not only analyze a circuit but also design an electronic circuit with the necessary components.

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction to the Course and Lab: basic concept of electronic circuits, active and passive circuit elements, representations of basic components, revisiting physical quantities (force, energy, power, charge, current, potential, electric field, magnetic flux density) | 2 |
| 2 | Introduction to Systems & Signals: linear/non-linear, time-invariant/time-variant systems; continuous and discrete time signals, periodic and aperiodic signals; DC, AC, sinusoidal, complex signals, square wave, triangular wave, duty cycle; unit step, unit ramp, representing a signal using step function | 3 |
| 3 | Circuit Elements and Laws: ideal voltage and current sources, controlled sources; resistors, resistivity, conductors, conductivity, mobility, insulators, semiconductors, capacitor, inductor; Ohm's Law, KCL, KVL; node and loop equations (Cramer's Rule, Gauss-Jordan Method); current/voltage division, series and parallel combinations, star-to-delta and delta-to-star conversion, Wheatstone Bridge | 4 |
| 4 | Network Theorems: Thevenin and Norton's theorems; source transformation, max power transfer, superposition theorem; input and output resistances, finding V and I in resistive circuits with controlled sources | 3 |
| 5 | OP-AMP: idea of amplifiers, ideal OP Amps, inverting and non-inverting amplifiers with resistors; adders, difference amplifiers, instrumentation amplifiers; common mode gain, differential gain, CMRR, voltage saturation, slew rate, offset voltage; designing controlled sources using Op-Amp, analysing linear circuits with Op Amps | 5 |
| 6 | Diode: n and p type semiconductor, p-n junction; semiconductor diodes with forward and reverse bias, ideal diode; half-wave and full-wave rectifiers | 3 |
| 7 | Energy Storage Elements: capacitors (energy storage, series and parallel capacitors); inductors (energy storage, series and parallel inductors); Op-Amp circuits with capacitors and inductors | 2 |
| 8 | Basic RL and RC Circuits: source-free RL circuit and properties of exponential response; source-free RC circuit and time constant; driven RL and RC circuits, natural and forced response | 4 |
| 9 | Sinusoidal Steady-State Analysis: characteristics of sinusoids (period, frequency, amplitude, average and RMS values, phase, phase lag/lead); forced response to sinusoidal function; complex forcing function; phasor and phasor relationships for R, L, C; impedance, admittance; circuit laws and network theorems | 6 |
| 10 | Frequency Sensitive Circuits - Filters: low pass, high pass, band pass, and band stop filters; frequency response of a filter, designing LPF/HPF/BPF/BSF using passive components; cut-off frequency, bandwidth, quality factor; active filters, designing 1st and 2nd order active filters using Op-Amp | 4 |
| 11 | BJT: operating regions and applications of BJT; biasing circuits (fixed bias and potential divider); load-line, Q-point; small signal operation and models; CE, CB, CC amplifiers focusing on input resistance, output resistance, and gain | 6 |
| **Total** | | **42** |
