## Course Description

The main objective of this course is to conduct a study on the fundamental aspects of signals and systems. This course is the gateway to many important courses at undergraduate and postgraduate level such as Analog and Digital Communication Systems, Digital Signal Processing (DSP), Advanced DSP, Digital Image Processing, VLSI Signal Processing, Machine Learning and Deep Learning, Radar Signal Processing, Acoustics, Speech Communication, Seismology, Circuit Design, Control System Engineering, Robotics, chemical process control, etc. The scope of potential and actual applications of the methods of signals and system analysis continues to expand as engineers face new challenges involving the synthesis or analysis of complex processes.

The course is designed to develop — in parallel — the methods of analysis of continuous-time and discrete-time signals and systems so that students can use computers to develop and implement signal and systems algorithms. This approach enables students to draw on the similarities between continuous-time and discrete-time methods in order to share insights and intuition, and to exploit the differences between them to sharpen understanding of the distinct properties of each.

**Course Outline:** The course first builds up fundamentals and an introduction to signals and systems ranging from their types, properties and different examples. It then discusses the extremely useful class of linear time-invariant (LTI) systems and their representation with convolution sums. Fourier analysis — ranging from Fourier series, Fourier transform, and time and frequency characterization — is discussed in depth, followed by Shannon's sampling theory. The course then shifts to Z-transform and Laplace transform with detailed discussion on properties and applications. It also covers the design of digital resonators and their applications, and concludes with signals and systems methods applied to control systems and communication systems.

## Course Structure

| Lecture No. | Topic |
|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction and motivation for signals and systems; potential applications in engineering; four distinct problems addressed in signals and systems |
| 2 | Mathematical background-I: Complex number system; importance of j = √−1; linear combination of vectors. Mathematical background-II: Derivation of Euler's relation e^jθ = cos(θ) + jsin(θ) and corollaries; concept of inner product |
| 3 | Introduction to discrete-time signals and systems: signal types and classifications; signal energy and average power; standard test signals (impulse, step, ramp, real and complex exponential) |
| 4 | Periodic and aperiodic signals; periodicity in CT vs DT; basic algebraic operations on signals; sifting property |
| 5 | Introduction to systems I: examples of systems; modeling of R-C circuits and automobile systems by first-order differential and difference equations |
| 6 | Properties of systems: causality, stability, inverse systems, linearity, time-invariance, systems with or without memory |
| 7 | Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) systems and impulse response |
| 8 | Convolution sum and its properties (commutative, cascade, association, parallel); graphical and analytical methods of convolution |
| 9 | Problem solving on convolution for finite/infinite duration sequences |
| 10 | Causality and stability conditions for LTI systems in terms of impulse response |
| 11 | Autocorrelation and cross-correlation functions; problem solving |
| 12 | Linear Constant Coefficient Difference Equation and its link with LTI system properties; FIR and IIR systems with examples |
| 13 | Singularity analysis: impulse response characterizes an LTI system; area under impulse; sampling property; doublet and kth-order derivative and integration of impulse |
| 14 | Fourier Analysis: introduction and motivation; eigenvalue and eigenvector in LTI/Fourier context; sinusoidal signal excitation |
| 15 | Fourier Analysis (contd.): inner product interpretation; trigonometric Fourier series via orthogonal functions |
| 16 | Fourier Analysis (contd.): exponential Fourier series for pulse train; Dirichlet conditions; Michelson's effect; Fourier series and LTI systems |
| 17 | Fourier Analysis (contd.): development of continuous-time Fourier transform (CTFT) from limiting process; FT interpretation — graphical and inner product |
| 18 | Fourier Analysis (contd.): CTFT problem solving (rectangular pulse, sinc, periodic signal, impulse train); Heisenberg's uncertainty principle in signal processing |
| 19 | Fourier Analysis (contd.): properties of FT (shifting, scaling, convolution, multiplication, duality, Parseval's theorem); Dirichlet conditions; negative frequency |
| 20 | Fourier Analysis (contd.): amplitude modulation from modulation theorem; energy spectral density (ESD), average power and power spectral density (PSD) |
| 21 | Fourier Analysis (contd.): Hilbert transform and applications in communications (SSB modulation) |
| 22 | Fourier Analysis (contd.): LTI filtering using Fourier analysis |
| 23 | Fourier Analysis (contd.): time and frequency characterization; magnitude-phase representation of Fourier transform |
| 24 | Fourier Analysis (contd.): magnitude-phase of frequency response; effect of phase in signal reconstruction; linear phase |
| 25 | Fourier Analysis (contd.): group delay concept; pulse dispersion due to non-constant group delay; Bode plot for analog system function |
| 26 | Laplace transform: introduction and motivation; definition of unilateral and bilateral Laplace transform; examples with Region of Convergence |
| 27 | Laplace transform (contd.): geometric evaluation of Fourier transform from pole-zero plot; properties of Laplace transform |
| 28 | Laplace transform (contd.): inverse Laplace transform; transient analysis in electric circuits; solution of linear differential equations |
| 29 | Weierstrass-Stone Approximation theorem and system function of an LTI system |
| 30 | Z-transform: introduction and motivation; Z-transform of elementary signals; Region of Convergence (ROC); ROC for finite and infinite duration sequences; properties of ROC |
| 31 | Z-transform (contd.): geometric evaluation of FT from pole-zero plot; inversion by inspection, power series method, partial-fraction method |
| 32 | Z-transform (contd.): Cauchy's Residue method; solution of difference equations using Z-transform; relation between Fourier and Z-transforms; stability in Z-domain |
| 33 | Design of Digital Resonator: −3dB bandwidth and digital pole radius; resonant frequency and digital pole angle |
| 34 | Design of Digital Resonator (contd.): impulse response of resonator; trade-off between design parameters; relevance to Biquad Circuits in EVD |
| 35 | Feedback Control Systems: open loop vs closed loop systems; linear feedback systems; design of feedback amplifiers; design of inverse systems |
| 36 | Feedback Control Systems: stabilization of unstable systems; sampled data feedback control systems; design of tracking systems |
| 37 | Feedback Control Systems: Root Locus Analysis; Nyquist Stability Criterion; Bode plots; gain margin and phase margin |
| 38 | Concluding discussion on signals and systems |
