## Course Description

This course has been visualised for undergraduate students without an intermediate academic background in the liberal arts stream. It aims to encourage and contextualise some of the natural questions that might arise in a young mind regarding individual identity, framed in ethical concepts such as truth, community living, and individual duty of contributing towards the preservation and growth of material and intellectual ecology, broadly defined as public service.

This course falls under the rubric of intellectual history, also known as the history of ideas. Drawing an analogy with the organic sciences — where the growth of an organism is governed by conditions that can promote or retard its development — the course frames the evolution of an idea as similarly shaped by the genius of its proponent, its interaction with historical forces, and its affinity with human psychological traits.

Under this broad framework of the genesis and evolution of ideas, this course on the concept of India is an attempt to understand the reality, relevance, and potential of this ideal for a country working out issues of representation and participation across diverse classes and communities. The course searches for the genesis of this idea of India in 19th-century India, amid a vortex of national and international currents, with particular reference to Vivekananda, Tagore, and Gandhi. It brings on board concepts such as truth, conscience, violence/non-violence, responsibility, deprivation and suppression, and issues of culture, to better understand the idea of India formulated by these three dominant figures of history. The course will involve detailed readings and discussion of fundamental writings of these three thinkers, regarding specific themes and events of their respective lives.

## Course Outcomes (COs)

**Learning Outcomes:** The course aims to acquaint students with some of the formative themes and thinkers who have contributed to the modern idea of India, and to familiarise them with different approaches to reading texts, identifying their central arguments, and raising their individual questions regarding these arguments.

**Additional Course Outcomes:**

| CO | Description |
|---|---|
| CO1 | Acquaint students with formative themes and thinkers who contributed to the modern idea of India, and develop skills in reading texts and identifying central arguments. |
| CO2 | Understand the broader ethical, human responsibility of overriding importance while approaching scientific and technical challenges relating to building amenities and individual and corporate wealth. |
| CO3 | Understand the thinkers' shared conception of life as a continuous form of sacrifice extending beyond ritualistic practices, and of the universe as closely connected in its creation and destruction, with all human activity being part of this sacrifice. |
| CO4 | Recognize the highest function of human consciousness in knowing and understanding this fact, including the importance of sharing social and economic advantage — reflected in global ecological concerns, common ownership of resources, and usage limits. |

## Additional Notes

- **Mode of Teaching:** The course is organised around the ideas of four thinkers/modules — a background module followed by three modules centred on Vivekananda, Tagore, and Gandhi respectively.
- **Attendance and Participation:** The success of the course depends significantly on students' engagement with the readings and with one another in class.
- **Plagiarism and Academic Integrity:** Using words and phrases of authors without proper citation is considered a violation of norms of academic integrity and is strictly discouraged. Genuine effort and commitment will be duly appreciated.
- The instructor, Gangeya Mukherji, has authored two of the recommended reference readings (*An Alternative Idea of India: Tagore and Vivekananda* and *Gandhi and Tagore: Politics, Truth and Conscience*).
