## Course Description

The course covers digital communication systems starting from digitization of analog signals through to information theory. Topics include digital modulation, demodulation and detection in AWGN channels, and the elements of information theory including Shannon capacity limits. The course has both analytical assignments and laboratory experiments — hardware and software based — with a primary focus on performance analysis in the lab.

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| Module 1 | Introduction to Digital Communication Systems and Digitization of Analog Signals — Analog vs digital communications, overview of digital communication based system, review of sampling theorem, ideal and practical sampling, aliasing, analog signal reconstruction from discrete-time samples, pulse code modulation (PCM): uniform and non-uniform quantization and companding, Differential PCM | 12 |
| Module 2 | Digital Modulation — Signal space concepts: representation of signals as vectors and Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization. Signal representation and constellations: ASK, PSK, rectangular and non-rectangular QAM, and FSK. Design for band-limited channels: power-bandwidth tradeoff and Nyquist criterion for ISI avoidance | 10 |
| Module 3 | Digital Demodulation and Detection — Optimal demodulation in AWGN: maximum-likelihood (ML) decision rule and minimum probability error (MEP) decision rule. Realization of optimal receiver using matched filters. Performance analysis of ML reception and link-budget analysis | 10 |
| Module 4 | Elements of Information Theory — Notion of channel capacity, capacity of discrete-time AWGN channel: sphere packing interpretation, capacity of band-limited AWGN channel, power-bandwidth tradeoff in band-limited AWGN channel, and design implications of Shannon limits | 8 |
| **Total** | | **40** |

## Additional Notes

**Attendance Policy:** Attendance will be marked in each lecture. At the end of the semester, if a student has an overall attendance of less than 70% and has scored a total marks of Y out of 100, then the total marks will be recalculated as Y − min(5, 70 − attendance%). Note that the 30% permissible absence includes all medical and co-curricular-activity-related leaves.

**Assignments & Laboratory:** Analytical problems will be regularly given to be solved. In the laboratory, concepts learned in lectures need to be validated through hardware and software based experiments. For the software part, performance analysis will be the primary focus.
