## Course Description

At the end of the course, students are expected:
(i) To obtain a basic understanding of most known modal logics and their applications.
(ii) To know the nature of the tableaux method for decidability.
(iii) To understand different properties of models in modal logic.
(iv) To learn various systems of modal logic and how to apply them to solve problems.
(v) To write a scientific report about an application of modal logic in AI, distributed systems.

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Propositional Logic, Syntax and Semantics, Semantic tableau, Hilbert proof system, Basic properties of propositional logic | 6 |
| 2 | Intuitionistic logic, Kripke frames and models, Truth preserving operations, Hintikka systems, Intuitionistic frames and formulas, Intuitionistic calculus, Basic properties of intuitionistic logic | 10 |
| 3 | Modal logic, Possible world semantics, Modal frames and models, Truth preserving operations, Hintikka systems, Modal frames and formulas, Calculus K, Basic properties of K, A few more modal logics, From logics to classes of logics | 12 |
| 4 | Completeness theorems, Canonical models and filtration, Henkin construction, filtration method, Incompleteness | 5 |
| 5 | First Order Logic, Syntax and semantics, Hintikka systems, first order calculus | 5 |
| **Total** | | **38** |
