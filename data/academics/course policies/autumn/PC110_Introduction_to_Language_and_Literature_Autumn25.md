## Course Description

This course is designed to introduce students to the study of the English language and literature at the undergraduate level. It will follow a two-pronged approach: first, of teaching language through literature, and second, of introducing students to the world of literature and teaching them strategies of reading and interpreting texts. Literature offers perspective to readers on their own experiences and introduces them to worlds and worldviews alien to their understanding and experience. Involved engagement with literature can sensitize the reader to themselves and to the world around them, and enhance their ability to distance themselves both from the texts they read and from their own lives and think critically and creatively about these.

In this course, themes relevant to students' lives like ideas of success/failure, identity, relationships, sexuality, family, war and media will be explored through literary texts, films, advertisements, etc. Students will be asked to engage with texts in multiple ways through class discussions, written assignments, presentations, etc. One of the key aims of the course is to increase self-reflexivity in students in addition to equipping them with reading and writing skills; hence the recommended theme of the course is humour (one could take as broad a view of this theme as one likes), a trope which challenges the reader to think about the self by imagining [text cuts off in source document at this point].

## Course Outcomes (COs)

After successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

| CO | Description |
|---|---|
| CO1 | Separate the content of a text from its form and understand the idea of perspective |
| CO2 | Acquire a nuanced understanding of written and audio-visual texts through analysis |
| CO3 | Have a more objective and distant perspective on the themes covered in the course |
| CO4 | Organize their thoughts and ideas into coherent text |
| CO5 | Acquire the confidence to speak in public |
