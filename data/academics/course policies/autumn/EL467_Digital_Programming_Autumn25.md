## Course Description

The course aims to impart digital logic concept realization using hardware description language (HDL) and its practical implementation on FPGA-based hardware kits. The HDL will be discussed in detail with all its abstraction levels and modeling schemes. The focus of the course will be to develop practical realizable projects using HDL and FPGA.

---

## Course Outcomes (COs)

After successful completion of this course:

| CO | Description |
|---|---|
| CO1 | Students shall be able to define specifications and then design digital systems conforming to those specifications, and implement these designs efficiently using Verilog HDL. |
| CO2 | Extensive hands-on training on FPGA-based projects shall give students confidence to realize digital circuits on hardware, facilitating state-of-the-art practical research projects and meeting industrial demands. |

## Course Structure

| Unit | Topic | No. of Lectures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction to Digital Design, Concept of Hierarchical and Structured Design: Digital meaning and essence, basics of digital VLSI design, role of CAD tools in VLSI design process, hierarchical and design methodologies in VLSI systems, CAD algorithms for device/circuit/gate/system levels of abstraction, physical design process in VLSI design, testing | Not specified |
| 2 | Introduction to HDL, Synthesis and FPGA: Introduction to hardware description language, basic language elements of Verilog, behavioral/data flow/structural/switch level modeling, constructs and conventions in Verilog, data types, number system, operators; modeling and synthesis issues in Verilog; familiarization with analog and mixed signal designing in Verilog; concept of CPLD, FPGA; familiarization with Xilinx Spartan-3E FPGA development board; design constraints using FPGA; implementing designs using FPGA | Not specified |
| 3 | Combinational Systems Realization Using HDL and FPGA: Verilog description of gates, user-defined primitives, instantiation, design of arithmetic functions (adder, subtractor, multiplier, divider), implementation of multiplexer/demultiplexer/encoder/decoder/code converters, design of complex systems, parameterized module, realization of test benches, timing and delay models | Not specified |
| 4 | Description and Design of Sequential Circuits: Defining procedural blocks, procedural flow control, blocking and non-blocking assignments, realization of latches/flip-flops/register/counter, implementation of synchronous and asynchronous designs | Not specified |
| 5 | Data Subsystems: Storage modules, memory, stack, queue, functional modules, data paths, control subsystems | Not specified |
| 6 | FSM and I/O Subsystem: Finite state machine, Mealy and Moore designs, system task and functions, operations related to I/O subsystems, compiler directives, VeSPA (Very Small Processor Architecture) processor — developing behavioral and structural modeling using HDL and FPGA, design of practical systems | Not specified |
| **Total** | | Not specified in source |

## Program Structure Context

EL467 Digital Programming is an elective course offered to M.Tech. ICT students in their First Semester and B.Tech. ICT/ICT-CS students in their 7th Semester. It requires a basic understanding of digital logic design as a prerequisite and has a 3-0-2-4 credit structure with integrated lab work involving Xilinx Spartan-3E FPGA development boards.

---

## Additional Notes

- The course places strong emphasis on hands-on FPGA-based project work using Verilog HDL.
- The VeSPA (Very Small Processor Architecture) processor project is a key practical component of the course (Unit 6).
