## Course Description

We live in a world replete with complex systems of systems that need to operate in a dynamic and uncertain environment, thus necessitating continuous adaptation to deliver stated goals which may also change over time. These systems, characterized by large size and non-linear interactions, are found across domains such as information-only, cyber-physical, societal, and biological. They raise new concerns to be addressed, such as: (i) Why things are the way they are? (ii) What are the right interventions to bring the system back to the desired state? (iii) Is a better state possible? And (iv) How best to reach the desired to-be state from the as-is state?

With current practice found wanting, digital twins have emerged as a promising technology supporting a simulation-based approach that is data-driven as well as justification-backed. This course aims to provide an introduction to this promising technology in a structured manner, backed by several use cases to be discussed in depth.
