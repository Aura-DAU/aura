# HM402 PublicsinSouth Asia Winter26

## Main Content

HM 402. Publics in South Asia: Contemporary Perspectives
Winter 2025-26
Instructor: Dr. Shefali Jha
Course Placement: HM 402 is an Open Elective
Course Format: 3 hours of lectures each week.
Pre-requisites: None
Course Content
We all assume that we are part of something called ‘the public’, and assume its existence in
our roles as students, teachers, ‘fans’, citizens/ ‘netizens’, readers, spectators and so on.
Nobody invites us to become members, nor can anyone take away our participation in the
public, active or passive. Publics are at the heart of the democratic imagination: Just as we
cannot imagine modern politics without democracy, democracy has little meaning without ‘the
public.’
Why and how did this come to be? What the various practices and technologies through which
we come to be members of publics? What are the spaces and representations of people, cultures
and events, through which we may identify and analyse publics? Who draws the line between
the public and the private, and why is that line important? What significance do national
boundaries and histories have for such analyses? This course is based on exploring these
fundamental questions with the help of readings taken from recent ethnographic work by
anthropologists of Bangladesh, Nepal, Pakistan, India and Sri Lanka, aside from historical
accounts and political theory. We will see whether there are threads of similarity which run
through the social worlds of class, caste, gender, and sexuality in the region, and how these
affect our ways of thinking about publics, publicity, and privacy. We will read about and work
with the written word, films, music and radio performances to discuss these questions.

Method of Assessment: Class Participation (20%); Class Presentation + write-up (10 + 20%);

Reviews + Response papers (20%); final paper to be written in class (30%).

Participation will be judged by attendance, tasks, as well as helpful and relevant interventions

in class discussion.

Plagiarism on assignments and unacknowledged, thoughtless use of AI tools will result in a

failing grade for the course.

Course Outcomes: On successfully completing the course, students will have a basic

familiarity with India’s neighbouring states and the socio-cultural fabric of South Asian

societies. They will be able to reflect on their own experience, perspectives and responsibilities

as future knowledge workers and citizens. They will also be able to read and think with research

material in the social sciences, becoming conversant with ethnography as a method of research.

Their writing and analytical skills should improve, and they should learn the skill of

communicating complex ideas to different kinds of audiences.
