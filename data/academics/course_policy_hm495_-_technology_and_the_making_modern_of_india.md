# HM495 (HM495)

## Main Content

Course name: Technology and the Making of Modern India
Course code: HM 495
Course type: Open elective
Instructor: Purbasha Das
Credit: 3-0-0-3
Semester: Winter Semester 2026
Course Outline
This course seeks to explore the various connotations of technology and comprehend how it
came to be seen as a defining feature of modernity. Technologies and scientific thinking were
indispensable for the building of the modern Indian nation state. The objective is to observe
how the nationalist and the colonial understanding of science and technology relate to each
other, as well as how the governing classes’ perspective changes over time. The Global South
and the Global North now engage in an interchange of labour and knowledge instead of the
former metropole and colony. The readings will allow the students to reflect on the
Eurocentric notion that scientific and technological knowledge always flows from the Global
North to the Global South. The goal is to understand the uniqueness of the Indian experience
of science and technology while at the time understanding whether this experience can be
called a part of the broader global realm of science and technology. The readings explore the
interconnection between science and technology and larger processes of nationalism,
colonialism and industrialization. They also point to other intersections with environment,
labour and health. The key takeaway of the course is that technology is imbued with meaning
and technological change impacts and is shaped by all levels of society.
Aims and Objectives
 The course will enable students to think critically about technology beyond its
technical applications.
 The students will be introduced to a historical understanding of science and
technology in the post-colonial period.
 Class discussions will unpack key concepts like nation, modernity, labour and
technological progress.
 The students will be able to understand the favourable and unfavourable impact of
technology on the economy, environment and other facets of society. The course will
aid them in thinking about developing technology that is beneficial for different
sections of society.
 The writing assignments and project will improve their written and oral
communication skills.

Grading Policy
 Two in-semester exams (30% +30%)
 End semester project (40 %)
Course Outcomes:
Upon passing the courses, students should have a:
● Basic critical and analytical understanding of how technology is shaped by society and also
how technology impacts society. [PO1, PO7, PO12]
● How the progress of technology is shaped by political, social, environmental factors. This
can in turn be used to understand the ethics of technological production. [PO6, PO7, PO8].
