## Detailed Course Content


- The gripping heroic narrative of the Mahabharata also contains a vast number of subsidiary stories and d iscussions, which together re�lect the entire range of human emotions and situations , while debating the nature of right and wrong , both in the context of immediate human situations and the overarching philosophy of life . This essential debate over right and wrong , presented in different forms within the epic, above all in the Bhagavad Gita, might be the main reason for the epic to be regarded variously as a treatise on ethics and metaphysics ( dharmashastra ), a repository of the highest knowledge (the �ifth veda) , a guide to right action (itihasa) , and the human condition itself. The epic transforms the account of a usual rivalry and battle between two groups of the same family on to a plane that lends universality , symbolic power , and ethical relevance to its narrative. The discussions and episodes of the epic primarily involve the question of the ultimate goal of human life , emphasised in the epic yet again in its closing section: that of the question of right and wrong. The narrative contains such episodes that may make the reader doubt whether the Mahabharata is a moral text . The human dilemmas are at places so acute and familiar as to evoke sympathy for immoral characters. It might be pointed out that Dharma, as in the philosophical universe of the Mahabharata, is a means and an end in itself. The Mahabharata does not advocate a withdrawal from life but a participation in life according to the norms of dharma without undue attachment to those aspects of life commonly or primarily experienced through sensory perception. Action can be considered as emerging from desire. Desire in the present course of action is not meant merely as wish or bodily urge, but the mental impulse to perform action, when the performer becomes an agent, or a means of action which has an objective. But the de�inition of desire is problematic. Is it always ethical and altruistic? Or completely self- centred? Does a impulse which is completely self- centric actually always serves the self? What is the self, for instance? 2 This de�inition of desire might not c onvey an easy and uncomplicated path, but the concept of dharma has never been expected to present easy options for its observance. If we proceed with the premise that dharma is in a sense an objective, the fundamental objective no doubt, but an objective nevertheless, the state of bein g dharmic does not alone lead to liberation, as identi�ication in life with the practice of dharma retains the self within the play of gunas or the three composite qualities of life. The observance of dharma, it can be argued, is only possible with an attitude of detachment. For the Mahabharata, it is therefore not enough for Yudhishthira, the dharma king, to have throughout his life observed dharma in exactness; he must in the end forsake his identi�ication with the observance of dharma to attain the ultim ate realisation of the self, that of liberation of the soul. An ethical dilemma might not necessarily undermine ethical ideas. They source the existential strength of the epic insofar as the demonstration of the resilience of dif�icult situations regar ding ethics and virtue is concerned. With all the inequality and injustice that have characterised social systems across time, the epic seems to suggest that such seeming resilience of reality to ideals reinforces the validity of ideals, particularly on th e individual plane. Some prominent s cholars tracing the changing form of the Mahabharata over the early centuries of its existence, point out that after the period of its earliest development through an oral tradition practised by singer -poets, the Bhrigu clan became its custodians for the most extended duration . While forming its present shape and volume , they took particular interest in incorporating the subjects of dharma (law) and niti (conduct), which were their unique preserve, mainly in th e Santi and Anushasana parvas of the epic . This explains the profound emphasis laid on the two subjects throughout the epic. The course will involve detailed readings of some chapters of the Mahabharata and Bhagav ad Gita, as well as discussion and presentation of the fundamental questions in those chapters regarding right and wrong. LEARNING OUTCOMES : The course aims to acquaint students with the narrative and themes of the Mahabharata and invites them to think critically about the complex issues of pain, untruth, violence, compassion, and equanimity. It will also familiarise them with different approaches to reading texts by examining various textual devices, identifying their central arguments, and raising questions that may arise from individual perspectives . COURSE REQUIREMENTS Attendance and Participation : The success of the course depends signi�icantly on students' engagement with the readin gs and with one another in class. Mode of Teaching : The course will be divided into three broad modules. The �irst module will focus on the Narration of the Mahabharata, the epic story, and specific complicated ethical issues, such as Karna's destiny, Amba/Shikhandi's anxiety, Ekalavya's injustice, Draupadi's marriage, and the episode of the dice game . The second module will comprise the study of the short conversation between Yudhishthira and the Yaksha , the theme of the Bhagavad Gita, the imagery of t he two trees – those of Dharma and Adharma

The third module will focus on the question of Non- Violence in the Mahabharata, exploring aspects of pain, truth, and the eternal essence of the Mahabharata, as well as the final journey of the Pandavas from Earth . This unwieldy variety of interpretations goes towards contributing to the attractiveness and longevity of epics.

- The Mahabharata prov ides us with a deep understanding of life ’s complicated realities, and regards life as a continuous dedication to ethical purpose that is simultaneously individual and collective . To serve a social purpose and uplift the individual soul, every person needs to make complex moral choices, with easy options often unavailable.

- Debating right and wrong is an ever -continuous human characteristic , and embarking on the right course of action is almost always tricky . The course will highlight the importance of this demanding and exciting exercise of distinguishing between the correct choice and the merely attractive one, as well as the importance of due diligence in acting on the proper course of action in various situations of material existence. ***
