So there will be two entangled strands of historical inquiry we will follow- one following the development of Design and the other looking at the global and national contexts that shaped it. These global contexts are indicated by such historical periodizations such as the Industrial Revolution, the first Great War and the rise of Communism in Russia, the inter-war years, the rise of Fascism the Cold War era, the era of Mass Consumption, the Counter-Culture and the contemporary era of Globalization. The final segment of the course will turn to India and look at colonial context in which India found itself during the period of the Industrial Revolution . We will see how the first consciousness of an Indian-designed and made products was emerging in the context of the Swadeshi movement in 1905-1911, the impact of the emergence of Gandhi in sensitizing us to the power of Design in nationalist politics and finally we will look into the ways in which both modern western Design practice and pedagogy as well as our own craft traditions and material culture served to shape the context within which India’s first modern Design school was established in 1961. Course Outcome : Toward the end of the course, we will try to understand how Design history helps us engage with questions of form, function and aesthetics in completely different ways and generate new ways of looking at the products and services they consume as part of our everyday lives. They will seek to understand how does Design History prompt us to question the larger implications of what we buy or use in terms of the impacts these have on human health and wellbeing, on the built environment and the planet at large. There will be continuous evaluation of students through class participation, individual presentations and a final research paper, seminar or in-class examination.
