# HM481 Reading Plato Winter 26

## Main Content

HM 481: READING PLATO: BETWEEN PHILOSOPHY AND LITERATURE
Instructor: Bharani Kollipara
COURSE ABSTRACT
Plato is a charming writer of philosophy in the form of dialogues. He wrote more than thirty
dialogues, ranging from short to long ones, which deal with almost all the important problems
and topics of philosophy. More importantly, all his dialogues contain, in varying degrees,
literary embellishments in the form of striking character portrayals, powerful metaphors and
analogies, drama and vivid exchanges and so on. In this course, we’ll study a few Platonic
dialogues with the aim of understanding Plato’s controversial views on politics and democracy.
We will study the following dialogues:
1. Apology
2. Gorgias
3. Laches
4. The Republic
Secondary Readings:
Monoson, Sarah. Plato’s Democratic Entanglements.
Allen, Danielle, S.,Why Plato Wrote, Malden, MA: Wiley-Blackwell.,2010
Kraut, Richard. Cambridge Companion to Plato. Cambridge: Cambridge University press.
Wolin, Sheldon. Politics and Vision
Euben, Peter. The Tragedy of Political Theory
Mode of Teaching: The course will be organized around several issues which arise from each
dialogue we study. The lectures will help you in critically approaching the assigned readings.
Discussions are aimed at targeting the specific aspects of the readings, such as ideas, difficult
passages and arguments. Freewheeling discussions are not encouraged at all. There will be
periodic quizzes. Independent study and initiative will be highly encouraged and the required
guidance would be provided.
Writing: You will be asked to write three short papers, varying between1000 to 3000 words
each, over the course of the semester. The word/page limit will be specified at the time of
assigning the task.
EVALUATION
The first paper will count for 30%, second paper for 40% and the final paper for 30%.
PLAGIARISM AND ACADEMIC INTEGRITY
Submitting someone else’s work as your own, or using others’ words and phrases without
proper citation, is a violation of norms of academic integrity, and may result in penalties up to
and including a failing grade in the course. Genuine effort and commitment will have direct
bearing on the evaluation.
