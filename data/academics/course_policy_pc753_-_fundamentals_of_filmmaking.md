# PC753 (PC753)

## Main Content

COURSE FILE
PC753: Fundamentals of Filmmaking
Credit Structure: 2-0-4-4
Program: MDes (Communication Design)
Year: 1
Semester: 2
Duration: 16 Days (Full Day – Lecture + Studio)
Timing: 9 AM – 6 PM
Course Instructor: Anirban Dutta Gupta
Couse Placement: Core course for MDes (CD) Semester 2. This is a specialised course in
design and is aimed at integrating inputs provided in previous courses in semester 1 and 2
into a ‘multimedia’ matrix and orient the students to its multimodal application.
Course Format: Module based. The course is taught as a combination of Lectures and Studio
sessions. Lectures introduce concepts, which are then analysed and explored via studio
sessions and assignments. Lecture and studio is integrated and in certain cases can overlap.
Teaching is through hand-on learning and through learning-by-doing and via real-time
demonstrations and guidance by faculty members. Students will be expected to conduct
fieldwork.
Course Objective and Content:
The terms film and video is used interchangeably.
The objective of this course is to introduce the various aspects of filmmaking – from concept
till production. The course will synthesize and integrate the theoretical inputs the students

Course Abstract:
The art of filmmaking is a complex process requiring perfect synthesis of multiple elements
from domains of storytelling, narrative, photography, design, technology, physics and
management. This course will give an overview and introduction to this world and expose
the students to the various elements that go into conceiving, planning and executing a
motion film project.
One of the aspect that the course will look at in detail is ‘continuity of filmic time and space’
and hopefully realize that it is a grammar that has come about, based on our natural sense
of editing, and which has been adapted to the art and craft of filmic space. To understand
this better the course will look at the history of filmic narrative construction, tracing from
actuality films of Lumiere Brothers through Edwin S Porter and D W Griffith, Pudovkin’
theories of Constructive Editing and Eisenstein’ Thematic Editing and Intellectual Montages.
One will see how these early masters explored and laid down the basic grammar of film
narrative which is followed even today, though with modifications to assimilate the current

refined film sense. The course will look at the counterpoints placed by Andre Bazin and his
appreciation of the neo-realist film form. One will also see how, historically, the Indian film
industry (not just Bollywood) has matched and mirrored the developments in Europe and
the United States.
Based on this historical overview the course will explore the various principles of filmic
construction – the narrative, storytelling, continuity, smoothness, cutting point, image size
and angles, sense of direction, overlapping, cross cutting. The course will explore the idea of
‘real’ time versus ‘reel’ time and the illusion of ‘continuousness of narrative’ or continuity.
We will also look at temporal and spatial continuity and how these can be maintained while
translating it into a video/film production. We will see how cinematography, lighting, sound
and editing all assist in the creation of continuity.
The course will try and assimilate these inputs and explore how they can be incorporated
into a video production. To achieve this, the students will script, storyboard and direct short
fiction films and documentaries. The execution of these films will be done in a professional
manner following the three-stage film production routine: pre-production stage, production
stage and post production stage. In the process, the students will experience the intricacies
of film production, production management, art direction, cinematography, acting etc.
An overview will also be provided on a typical studio set-up. The course will also be
exploring video as a technological medium and compare the pros and cons of video
production versus the film production with special emphasis on how to work around the
restrictions posed by video, and how using various optical tricks can ‘cheat’ the minds-eye
into believing that the quality of product is better than what it is. In this regard, the course
will explore cinematography using the video camera and basic lighting techniques.
Students are encouraged to recall the learnings from the Photography module as there are
multiple overlaps between photography and filmmaking.

Course Outcome:

The course aims to equip the students with professional knowledge –creative, narrative and

technical – involving the art and craft of videography. This course prepares the students for

professional application of videography as a communication and storytelling device and lays

the groundwork for further specialized courses in this field.

Growth Matrix:


| Flow | Objectives |
