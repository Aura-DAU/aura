# Modelling and Simulation (MC312)

## Overview

This document presents the detailed policy and syllabus structure for the course Modelling and Simulation (MC312). It includes details on course objectives, credits, prerequisites, syllabus content, evaluation schemes, and contact information where available.

## Main Content

MC312 – Modeling and Simulation

Course Title: Modelling and Simulation

Credit Structure(L-T-P-Cr): (3-0-2-4)

Course Code: MC312

Prerequisites (if any)/desired skill set: Introductory Calculus, Probability and Statistics, Linear

Algebra, and basic programming is required.

Level: Advanced Undergraduate

Course Placement: Modeling and Simulation is a core course offered to third year students of 
B.Tech  Mathematics and Computing program. 
 
Course format:  It is 3 hours’ lecture and 2 hours lab every week

Course content:  This course introduces students to the fundamentals of creating mathematical 
models of physical systems and implementation on computers to analyze the system to gain insight 
and make predictions. The different mathematical approaches to modeling covered in the course 
can be characterized into differential and difference equation-based models, probability-based 
models, including stochastic differential equations, cellular automata, event-based approaches, and 
matrix-based models. The course is interdisciplinary and looks at systems from a modeling 
perspective from physics, biology, social science, engineering, etc. Examples from different 
disciplines follow each topic. The students are expected to test and create models of these systems 
in the lab, gaining intuitive understanding and reporting on it following the complete modeling 
approach. It may be the foundation for domain-based specialization electives such as Computational 
Finance, Computational, and Systems Biology and Computational Physics, Computational Social 
Science, etc.

Text Book:

Angela B. Shiflet & George W. Shiflet. Introduction to Computational Science: Modeling and 
Simulation for the sciences, Princeton University Press, 2006. 
 
Reference Book:

1) Sheldon M. Ross, Simulation, Academic Press, 2006.

2) Hiroki Sayama, Introduction to the Modeling and Analysis of Complex

Systems, Open SUNY Textbooks, Milne Library, 2015.

3) Taylor & Karlin, An Introduction to Stochastic Modeling, Academic Press,

1998.

4) S. Strogatz, Nonlinear Dynamic and Chaos, Oxford University Press,

2001.

Assessment method: Two in-semester examinations (40%), final examination (30%) and lab viva 
 
Course Outcomes:  
After completing the course, the students would be able to create a relevant model for many 
problems from science and engineering by extracting the necessary information regarding the 
situation. They would also be able to define the different modeling terms by analyzing the system 
or the available data. They would be able to implement the model on the computer and check for 
its validity and the correctness of the assumptions present in the model. They will analyze the 
outcomes (mainly through visualizations) and make predictions. They would also be able to 
understand the limitations of their model and nuances in the computer modeling of systems. 
 
  After succesfull completion of the course the student will have the ability to

 Apply the knowledge of mathematics and computer science to model complex problems in

science (P1,P2,P3,P12). 
 Carry out model based investigations to derive insight into the problem at hand (P4). 
 Communicate effectively through vivas, course presentation and report writing (P10)
