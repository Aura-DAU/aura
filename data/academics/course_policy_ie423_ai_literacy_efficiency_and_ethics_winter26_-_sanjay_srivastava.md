# IE423 AI Literacy, Efficiency, and Ethics Winter26

## Main Content

Course Title - AI Literacy, Efficiency, and Ethics
Course Code: IE423
Instructors: V Sunitha and Sanjay Srivastava
Frequency: 2 lectures/Week (90 Minutes each). Lab Assignments to be done in the take-home
mode
1. Course Overview
This course bridges the gap between technical capability and academic utility. The goal is
Metacognition—using AI to "Learn how to Learn," conduct rigorous research, and prepare for
an AI-driven job market.
2. Eligibility and Target Audience
This course is nominally offered for credit as an ICT elective for BTech 6th semester students.
However, this course is also open to BTech 6th/8th semester, MTech, and PhD student for audit.
3. Evaluation Components
(a) In-class assignments / quizzes - 30%
(b) Take-home assignments – 30%
(c) Term Project and viva – 40%
Audit students are expected to perform at the minimum level of a CD grade in (a) and (b) to
receive a PASS grade.
4. Required Resources
Software Stack (Institute Provided)
● Text/Reasoning: ChatGPT Team or Claude (Enterprise privacy).
● Research: Elicit or Consensus (Academic search).
● Data: Julius AI or Tableau (Visualization).
● Accessibility: Otter.ai (Transcription).
Reading List (Selected)
● “The jagged frontier of AI” (Harvard Business School Working Paper).
● “Stochastic Parrots” (Bender et al.).
● “Bloom’s Taxonomy Revisited” (Oregon State University AI Guide).

5. Weekly Detailed Schedule
MODULE I: LITERACY & INCLUSION (The Foundation)
Goal: Moving from "Prompting" to "Algorithmic Thinking."
Week 1: The Paradigm Shift
● Tue (Joint): Predictive vs. Generative AI. The history from LSTMs to Transformers. Why
"Chatting" is the wrong mental model.
● Thu: The Ethics of Assistance. Defining the "Gray Line" between Co-piloting (allowed) and
Plagiarism (banned).
Week 2: Algorithmic Design Thinking
● Tue (Joint): It's not just about prompts; it's about Decomposition. Breaking complex
problems into logic chains.
● Thu: "Logic Mapping." Mapping out a thesis argument's logic tree before asking AI to draft
text.
Week 3: Inclusive Pedagogy (UDL)
● Tue (Joint): AI as the Great Equalizer. How AI supports neurodiversity (Dyslexia, ADHD,
Language barriers).
● Thu: "The Reader Bot." Building a custom "Reader Assistant" that simplifies dense
academic papers into bullet points/analogies.
Week 4: The Hallucination Problem
● Tue (Joint): Probabilistic Reasoning. Why AI lies? The concept of "Temperature" in
models.
● Thu: "Spot the Bot." A gamified session where Faculty and Students compete to identify
AI-generated text vs. human text, analyzing tone and variance.
MODULE II: RESEARCH & DATA FLUENCY (The Workflow)
Goal: Accelerating discovery without compromising rigor.
Week 5: The Literature Review
● Tue (Joint): Synthesis vs. Search. Using Semantic Scholar and Consensus.
● Thu: "The Research Matrix." Generating a comparative analysis table of 10 papers in 15
minutes to find research gaps.

Week 6: Data Storytelling
● Tue (Joint): Visualizing Complexity. Moving beyond static Excel charts to interactive
dashboards.
● Thu: "Thesis Viz." Using AI to suggest 3 alternative ways (Heatmap, Scatter, Sankey) to
visualize their project data.
Week 7: Code & Simulation (Guest: CS Dept)
● Tue (Joint): AI in the Loop. Understanding code generation even if you aren't a coder.
● Thu: "Chat with Data." Using Python libraries (via AI) to clean messy CSV datasets for their
thesis.
Week 8: Mid-Semester Review
● Tue (Joint): "Time-Saved" Showcase. Lightning talks sharing the most efficient workflows
discovered so far.
● Thu: Open Q&A and "Prompt Clinic" (Troubleshooting specific issues).
MODULE III: PEDAGOGY & CAREER (The Divergence)
Goal: Preparing for the future of work and assessment.
Week 9: Assessment Redesign
● Tue (Joint): Bloom’s Taxonomy 2.0. Moving from "Remembering" to "Evaluating."
● Thu: "Exam Prep." Using AI to generate infinite variations of practice problems for
upcoming finals.
Week 10: Personalized Learning
● Tue (Joint): The Age of the Personal Tutor. Adaptive learning systems.
● Thu: "The Super-Tutor." Training an AI to teach a complex concept (e.g., Quantum
Mechanics) via Socratic questioning.
Week 11: The Future of Work
● Tue (Joint): Human-AI Collaboration Models. The "Centaur" vs. the "Cyborg" approach to
work.
● Thu: "Resume Optimization." Beating the Applicant Tracking System (ATS) by optimizing
keywords and formatting.
Week 12: Discipline Deep Dives

● Thu: "The Shadow Portfolio." Documenting their new AI skills (Prompting, Data Viz) to add

6. Certification Requirements
(AI Literacy Certificate)
To earn the completion certificate, students must submit:
1. The Research Matrix: An AI-generated literature review table.
2. The Career Packet: An AI-optimized Resume & Cover Letter.
3. The Study Log: Evidence of using AI for deep Socratic learning.
