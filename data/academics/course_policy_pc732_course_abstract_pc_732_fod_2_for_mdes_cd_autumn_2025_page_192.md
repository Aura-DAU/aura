COURSE FILE

PC732: Fundamentals of Design - II

Credit Structure: 3-0-4-5

Program: MDes (Communication Design)

Year: 1

Semester: 1

Duration: 18 Days (Full Day – Lecture + Studio)

Timing: 9 AM – 6 PM

Course Instructor: Anirban Dutta Gupta + Visiting Faculty

Couse Placement: Core course for MDes (CD) Semester 1. This course is composed of two

parts – part A: Typography and Part B: Colour. Part A and Part B is taken consecutively. This is

a foundational course in design.

Course Format: Module based. The course is taught as a combination of Lectures and Studio

sessions. Lectures introduce concepts, which are then analysed and explored via studio

sessions and assignments. Lecture and studio is integrated and in certain cases can overlap.

Teaching is through hand-on learning and through learning-by-doing and via real-time

demonstrations and guidance by faculty members.

Course Objective and Content:

This module deals with the advanced aspects of colour, composition, virtual 3D spaces,

Typography as image, Illustration for information design, as means of explore, visualize and

communicate complex ideas with high levels of data density.

Typography is the art and technique of arranging type to make written language legible,

readable, and visually appealing. It involves selecting typefaces (fonts), adjusting the spacing

between letters (kerning), words (tracking), and lines (leading), and overall creating a visual

style that complements the content and enhances the message being conveyed.

Key elements of typography include Typeface which is the design of the letters with each

shape communicating a distinctive emotion, which evolves into a font which are the

variations within the family for example, Helvetica Bold or Times New Roman Italic. Each

type and its categories are steeped in history as it is usage and the message it

communicates. For example, the gothic Germanic type is associated with the fascist

ideology, hence its usage today may lead to negative interpretation with the values

associated with fascism.

Beyond the selection of type, the second level is to manipulate the font to match to its

usage. This includes understanding kerning - the space between two specific letters to

improve legibility or visual balance, tracking - the space between all letters in a word or

sentence, leading - the vertical space between lines of text, hierarchy - the arrangement of

type elements to show their relative importance, often using different font sizes, weights, or

styles to guide the reader, alignment – whether to position it left, right, center, or justified

and contrast which is the differences in weight, size, or colour to create emphasis and

improve readability.

Good typography can significantly affect how a message is perceived and can even evoke

different emotional responses from the audience. It enhances readability and legibility

ensuring that the text is easy to read. It helps in establishing a Visual Hierarchy by organizing

information by signalling what’s most important. This overlaps with some of the concepts

that were discussed in FOD 1 – Composition module.

Typography also conveys tone and emotion, for example, Serif fonts (like Times New Roman)

tend to be seen as formal, classic, and reliable. Sans-serif fonts (like Helvetica) are more

modern, clean, and minimal. This leads into type as an element of brand design be creating

a distinctive identity (Coca-Cola for example). It also helps in the aesthetic appeal with the

right combination of font choices, spacing, and layout can make a design feel polished,

cohesive, and visually striking, which grabs attention and makes an impact.

In the digital domain, typography is essential for a smooth user experience. If the type is

hard to read, poorly spaced, or mismatched, users will likely get frustrated and leave.

Consistent and accessible typography helps users navigate the content effortlessly.

Overall it guides the message by shaping how the message is understood and sets the mood

for the audience. The wrong choice of typography could make a brand appear

unprofessional, outdated, or inconsistent with its message. Typography, therefore, is a

critical component of visual design because it goes beyond simply choosing a font. It's the

art and technique of arranging type to make written language legible, readable, and

appealing when displayed. This part of the module aims to sensitise the students to the

variations of types and its uses.

The objective of part B i.e. Basics of colour is to sensitise the students to the ‘idea’ of colour

and understand that there is no ‘one’ colour. Colour is tainted by light, culture, our

perception, biology, emotion, chemistry and physics. Within this maze, we will try and

identify and understand some of the broad rules that will allow us to make sense of colour.

The objective is to teach and familiarize students with different methods of determining

Colour is an integral component of design, playing a crucial role in shaping the visual and

emotional experience of an audience. Colour is one of the most immediate and influential

elements in design. The strategic use of colour can transform a design from a mere visual

arrangement to a meaningful experience. The psychological effects of colour have long been

studied in fields such as marketing, psychology, and design. Different colours evoke distinct

emotional responses, which can influence perceptions and behaviour. For example, warm

colours are commonly associated with energy, warmth, and urgency, while cool colours like

often evoke calmness, trust, and professionalism. Understanding these emotional

associations allows designers to select colours that align with the desired mood and tone of

the design. It therefore, plays a fundamental role in establishing and reinforcing brand

identity. The selection of a particular colour palette is often a deliberate decision aimed at

differentiating a brand from its competitors while conveying specific values and messages.

Consistent use of colour across various media platforms enhances brand recognition and

fosters a sense of familiarity and trust with the audience.

Beyond its aesthetic and emotional impact, colour is also critical to the functionality and

accessibility of a design. In digital design, for example, the contrast between text and

background colours directly affects readability. A lack of sufficient contrast can make

content difficult to read, leading to a poor user experience. Therefore, colour choices must

be made with consideration of legibility and ease of navigation.

Colours also carry specific cultural meanings that vary across different regions and societies.

The students are encouraged to link the use and significance of colour to the course in

Culture and Communications. For instance, in Western cultures, white is often associated

with purity and peace, whereas in some Eastern cultures, it symbolizes mourning and death.

Designers must be aware of these cultural connotations to avoid miscommunication or

unintended negative associations when designing for global audiences. Moreover, colour

symbolism can be employed strategically to reinforce the intended message of a design.

Colour is fundamental to the creation of aesthetic cohesion and visual harmony within a

design. A well-thought-out colour palette can unify disparate elements, creating a balanced

and pleasing composition. The principles of colour theory, including complementary,

analogous, and triadic colour schemes, guide designers in selecting harmonious

combinations that are visually engaging and balanced. Colour adds another dimension to

the principles of composition that was explored in FOD 1.
