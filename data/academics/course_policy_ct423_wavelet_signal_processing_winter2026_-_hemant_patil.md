| 9. Abstract Content | After a few minutes in a restaurant we cease to notice the annoying sound of surrounding conversations, but a sudden silence reminds us of the presence of neighbors. Our attention is clearly attracted by transients and movements as opposed to stationary stimuli, which we beautifully ignore in our auditory perception. Concentrating on transients is probably a strategy for selecting important information from the overwhelming amount of data recorded by our senses. This course will prepare students in wavelet signal processing algorithms such as signal (image) compression, signal denoising, edge detection, etc. |

| Course Evaluation Policy (Tentative) | First In-Sem= 15 % Second-In-Sem=15 % Course Project work =20 % Endsem = 40 % Attendance= 10 % |
| Program Outcomes and Objectives | 1) The course builds foundation for the signal processing algorithms, such as short-time Fourier transform (STFT), Gabor transform and wavelet transforms (ie., CWT and DWT). 2) In addition, it will also discuss recently developed auditory transform and it’s applications in speech processing. 3) In course evaluation scheme, project work is also considered. Students will be asked to choose any one of the project topic and develop algorithms to implement the objectives of project. This will help students to apply and appreciate the theoretical concepts discussed in the class to real practical problems of engineering and technological interest. 4) One of the important outcomes of this course will be to equip skills to read and understand research papers related to the area of wavelets and time-frequency analysis. This will be quite useful to the students who would like to go for higher studies in near future. 5) This course will be of great value for those students who would like to do their thesis or project work (e.g., BTP, masters, doctoral) in various image and speech related pattern recognition, machine intelligence and signal coding applications. |
