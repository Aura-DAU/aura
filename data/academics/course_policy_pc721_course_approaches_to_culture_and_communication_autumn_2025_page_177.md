## Course Description

COURSE OBJECTIVES Communication between any form of transmitter and recipient is not just a matter of ‘hardware’ and ‘software’. Communication, what it achieves, how it is interpreted, what is understood, conveyed and represented involves social ideology, cultural techniques and whole society’s historical context. After all communication does not take place in vacuum! By the 18th century, mainly in western world, development of print culture lead to creation and evolution of a modern ‘public culture’. This included process of identifying, weighing, and discussing the issues of the day in ways hardly possible before the print revolution. This multidisciplinary approach seminar course students could explore the inter relationship of orality, writing. Printing, the visual image, theatre and music, by encouraging students to research and discuss the interaction between various modalities 1 of communication within Indian society and history. We intend to examine how culture communicates and communication creates culture with particular intend to consider relationship between image and sound. This course based on a seminar format. It is aimed to develop conceptual schemes and patterns by which historically (changing times-including future and its base in past) ideas objects and actions have been developed and or evolved to communicate within Indian culture and its contextual positioning with other cultures as broad as colonialism and globalization. This locating in understanding may include broadly language socialization, literacy, music and the visual arts, the power of language, miscommunication, and universal and culture-specific properties of human communication as embodied in ritual performances, organized spectacles and structured events in public spaces. Utilizing the ideas from Anthropology, Sociology, Semiotics History and Cultural Studies the course first will provide some understanding of basic conceptual and analytical foundations. So actual context and its depiction (ethnographic case) will be presented to you along with analytical frameworks that can be derived from it (theoretical) The course expects you to be open minded, critical and think about society and culture beyond India. You are expected to acquire basic sensibility to various assumptions about how and why societies communicate. The readings for each section will be dealt in the class as discussion sessions with student participation and presentation. Do not expect that lecture would solely be based on the readings. Readings are just a base to back up the ideas presented by the faculty. COURSE CONTENT AND THEMES 2 With globalization, the Internet, and the general shrinking of time and space, cross- cultural interaction has become a necessity in people’s everyday lives. This course is designed to interrogate different aspects of cross-cultural communication and cultural differences: family life, social relationships, the workplace, government, education, gender, romance, and religion. Throughout exploring these topics, we will strive to engage in personal self-reflection, hands-on experience, and to understand the connections to larger social structures. Specifically, the goals of this course are: 1) to describe, learn, and see common and important patterned social and cultural differences, 2) to provide a space for students to reflect on their own personal experience with cultural difference, and 3) to encourage students to engage with different cultures in a hands-on way and to meaningfully experience cross-cultural communication.
