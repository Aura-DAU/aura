## Textbooks and References

References: Vast resources on graph theory are available today and one has an easy access to many of these either online or in our RC. The following collection is a good starting point.  A. Gibbons, Algorithmic Graph Theory, Cambridge University Press.  S. Even, Graph Algorithms, Computer Science Press.  D.B. West, Introduction to Graph Theory (2nd edition), Prentice Hall of India.  J.A. Bondy and U.S.R. Murty, Graph Theory with Applications, MacMillan Press.  G. Agnarsson and R. Greenlaw, Graph Theory: Modelling, Applications and Algorithms, Pearson Prentice Hall.  R. Diestel, Graph Theory (3rd edition), Graduate Texts in Mathematics Series, Springer.  J. B-Jensen and G. Gutin, Digraphs: Theory, Algorithms and Applications, Springer- Verlag. 11. Lab sessions: Mostly unsupervised and will involve writing graph algorithms, analysis as well as proof of correctness of these algorithms, and optionally computer programs for the algorithms. A short introduction will be given for each lab assignment and the students are expected to work independently on the assignment. Access to Institute’s Computing facilities for assignments requiring the same shall be provided as per the lab time-table for the course. 12. Assessment: The assessment will consist of the following components, each contributing 25% to the final grade: 1. Exam-1 2. Exam-2 3. Exam-3 4. Assignments and Quizzes Plagiarism of any kind will result in an 'F' grade. Exam-1 will take place during the in-semester 1 examinations, as per the Autumn 2025-  26 Academic Calendar, and will cover topics taught up until that point. Exam-2 will be held during the in-semester 2 examinations and will cover topics taught  after Exam-1. Exam-3 will take place during the end-of-semester examinations, covering topics taught  after Exam-2. If the number of students enrolled in the course permits, a project may be introduced as part of the evaluation
