COURSE OBJECTIVES

Communication between any form of transmitter and recipient is not just a

matter of ‘hardware’ and ‘software’. Communication, what it achieves, how it is

interpreted, what is understood, conveyed and represented involves social ideology,

cultural techniques and whole society’s historical context. After all communication

does not take place in vacuum! By the 18th century, mainly in western world,

development of print culture lead to creation and evolution of a modern ‘public culture’.

This included process of identifying, weighing, and discussing the issues of the day in

ways hardly possible before the print revolution.

This multidisciplinary approach seminar course students could explore the

inter relationship of orality, writing. Printing, the visual image, theatre and music, by

encouraging students to research and discuss the interaction between various modalities

This course based on a seminar format. It is aimed to develop conceptual

The course expects you to be open minded, critical and think about society and culture

With globalization, the Internet, and the general shrinking of time and space, cross-

cultural interaction has become a necessity in people’s everyday lives. This course is

designed to interrogate different aspects of cross-cultural communication and cultural

differences: family life, social relationships, the workplace, government, education,

gender, romance, and religion. Throughout exploring these topics, we will strive to

engage in personal self-reflection, hands-on experience, and to understand the

connections to larger social structures.

Specifically, the goals of this course are: 1) to describe, learn, and see

common and important patterned social and cultural differences, 2) to provide a

space for students to reflect on their own personal experience with cultural

difference, and 3) to encourage students to engage with different cultures in a

hands-on way and to meaningfully experience cross-cultural communication.
