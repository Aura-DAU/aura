## Main Content

Numerical Methods for Data Science

Course Name:   Numerical Methods for Data Science Course Code:  SC 602 Prerequisite:  Linear Algebra
and Calculus Credit Structure:  3-0-2-4 (3 hours lecture and 2 hours lab per week) Course objective:
In the absence of analytical solution of a problem, we look for approximate solution or numerical
solution. The objective of this course is to make the students learned various numerical methods
that are used in convex optimization problems, machine learni ng problems, data compression and data
analysis problems.

Course Contents: Interpolations and Approximations: Lagrange and Newton’s interpolation,
Construction of linear, quadratic and cubic splines, Applications of natural cubic splines. Basic
linear algebra concepts, Matrix decomposition: LU decomposition, QR factorization, Least squares
problems, Eigenvalues and eigenvectors, Eigenvalue decomposition, Singular value decomposition,
Principal component analysis, Dimensionality reduction and application s, CUR Decomposition.

Numerical methods for convex optimization problems: Unconstrained problems: Gradient descent method,
Steepest descent method, Newton’s method, Conjugate direction methods , Problems with equality
constraint: Lagrange multiplier method,  Penalty method. Numerical methods for graph data :
Adjacency matrix, Laplacian, and other graph matrices; function interpolation on graphs; spectral
clustering and graph partitioning; centrality measures.

### Course Outcomes

After the end of this course:

 Problem analysis : Student should be able to Identify, formulate, review research literature, and
analyze data science problems reaching substantiated conclusions using various numerical methods. 
Design/development of solutions : Design solutions for complex data science problems and  develop
the processes that meet the specified needs with appropriate  consideration for the public health
and safety, and the cultural, societal, and environmental  considerations.

  Conduct investigations of complex problems : Use research -based knowledge and research  methods
including analysis and interpretation of data, and synthesis of the information to provide valid
conclusions.

 Modern tool usage : Create, select, and apply appropriate techniques, resources, and modern
engineering and IT tools  with an understandin g of the limitations.

 Life-long learning : Recognize the need for, and have the preparation and ability to engage in
independent and life -long learning in the broadest context of technological change.
