# Overview

Starter reference for students considering an MS abroad (primarily US/
Canada/Europe) via the GRE + TOEFL/IELTS pathway. General content only —
does not include DAU-specific admit statistics, faculty recommendation-
letter norms, or past DAU alumni outcomes, since none of that was available
to source.

# What the pathway typically involves

1. **GRE General Test** — verbal reasoning, quantitative reasoning, analytical
   writing. Most CS/engineering MS programs weight the quant section most
   heavily; some programs have stopped requiring GRE entirely — this varies
   by university and changes often, so check each target program directly.
2. **English proficiency** — TOEFL or IELTS, required for most non-native-
   English-speaking applicants regardless of undergraduate medium of instruction.
3. **SOP (Statement of Purpose)** — a personal essay tying research/career
   interests to why a specific program fits.
4. **LORs (Letters of Recommendation)** — typically 3, usually from
   professors who supervised a project, thesis, or research assistantship
   rather than only coursework instructors.
5. **Transcripts** — official, often needing WES or similar credential
   evaluation for US applications.

# Typical timeline (general — always verify against each target program)

- Fall-intake US programs commonly have December–January application
  deadlines, meaning GRE/TOEFL should usually be completed by September–
  October the year before.
- Research-heavy programs (PhD-track or thesis-track MS) often expect
  applicants to have already emailed potential advisors before applying.

# Funding considerations

- MS programs abroad are frequently unfunded or partially funded (unlike
  PhD programs, which are more often funded via assistantships).
- Options students commonly explore: educational loans, university-specific
  merit scholarships, and on-campus TA/RA positions (competitive, not
  guaranteed on entry).
