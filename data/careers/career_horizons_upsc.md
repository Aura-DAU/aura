# Overview

Starter reference for students considering the UPSC Civil Services
Examination (CSE) — a less common but real pathway from an engineering
background. General content only.

# What the exam involves

Three stages:
1. **Prelims** — two objective papers (General Studies, and CSAT which is
   qualifying-only for most candidates).
2. **Mains** — nine descriptive papers over several days, including an
   optional subject the candidate chooses (engineering graduates commonly
   pick their own engineering discipline, though this is a personal choice
   and not the only viable option).
3. **Interview** — a personality/general-awareness round with the UPSC
   board, following Mains.

# Why an engineering graduate might pursue it

Public administration, policy, and civil service roles (IAS/IPS/IFS and
allied services) are open to any degree background — engineering is not a
disadvantage, and many successful candidates each year come from
engineering programs.

# Typical timeline (general — verify each year)

- Notification and Prelims application usually open in February,
  Prelims exam typically held in May/June.
- Mains, for those who clear Prelims, is usually held in September.
- Interviews and final results typically follow through the early months
  of the next year — the full cycle from notification to final result
  commonly spans close to a year.

# Preparation approach (general)

- Most successful candidates report a 1–2 year sustained prep window,
  often overlapping with a job or final year of study.
- NCERT textbooks (class 6–12) are a commonly cited starting base for
  General Studies, followed by standard reference books per subject and
  current-affairs reading through the prep period.
- Choosing an optional subject candidates already have strong background
  in (e.g., their own engineering discipline) is common, though not
  mandatory.
