| 8.​ Abstract Content | According to the Ministry of External Affairs of India, thirty-two million two
hundred eighty-five thousand four hundred twenty-five Indians live abroad,
making it the largest diaspora in the world. Indians have existed transnationally
for centuries. Although initially, it was mostly through trade, the concept of
international migration is familiar to India. The nature of migration changed
over time and circumstances. Various cultural, political, and economic
developments transformed the social rubric of India. As a result, the process of
migration emerged as one of the means to tackle those unexpected challenges.
India’s first major tryst with migration began during the 19th-century colonial
period, when people from different states migrated to several other ‘colonies’ of
the British as indentured labourers. Over the years, the nature of diasporic
communities became more layered, as the individuals of Indian origin made
formidable ‘connections’ with other members of the community– both in their
host land and homeland. Indians across the diaspora are linked by a shared
‘Indian-ness’, an enduring sense of cultural belonging, and the willingness to |

| The Indian diaspora:
How did it come to
be? | The introductory section explains the notion of ‘diaspora’
and ‘diasporic communities’. Ethnographic case studies
central to the emergence and development of ‘Diaspora’ are
discussed. Following this, the origins and the formation of
the Indian diaspora will be traced across various phases -
ancient, modern, and post-modern. Readings will be divided
into the following categories:
1)​ What and who does the term ‘Diaspora’ mean?
2)​ Emergence of the Indian Diaspora | 10 |
| To Be Here and
There, At Once:
Introduction to
Transnationalism | As the diasporic communities formed and sustained
themselves abroad, the consistent pattern of maintaining a
‘connection’ with the place of origin, in any form - physical
or virtual, became a striking feature. This process is known
as ‘transnationalism’. This section introduces and explores
the various nuances of this phenomenon, such as are all
diasporic communities are transnational. Why do diasporic
communities want to be transnational? Are all generations of
diaspora transnational? Readings will be grouped under the
following categories:
1)​ What is transnationalism? What does it mean to have
a ‘simultaneous existence’?
2)​ Indian Diaspora and the effect of transnationalism on
everyday life | 10 |
| Longing to Belong?:
Unpacking the
Diasporic Identity | The process of migration affixes the importance of an
identity that is based on ethnicity, the label obtained from
“who you are” and “where you are from”. Though the
answers to those questions may not be essentially related to
the ethnic origin of a person, the subjectivity that results from
such kind of labelling leads to conflict. This conflict
manifests itself in different ways – transnational activities,
love for the homeland, and a sense of belonging to it.
1)​ Multiculturalism
2)​ Class and Caste in Diaspora - Select Case Studies | 10 - 12 |

| Outcomes and
Objectives | As an introductory course, the aim is to familiarise students and help them
critically engage with concepts of diaspora, transnationalism and international
migration. The four themes explained as a part of this course attempt to trace
the journey of the members of the diaspora to express themselves - collectively
and individually, their position in the ‘host land’ and their changing equation
with the concept of ‘home’ and ‘homeland’. The course also discusses the
formation of migrant subjectivity and the factors that impact it, thereby
addressing the various concerns that the members of the diaspora navigate with,
in their daily lives. The objective is also to understand how the Indian
diaspora’s engagement with their place of origin changed over the years and the
complex reality of being ‘diasporic’. |  |
