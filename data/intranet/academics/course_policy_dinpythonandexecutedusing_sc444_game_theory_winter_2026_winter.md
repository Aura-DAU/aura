What is game theory?

Game theory analyzes situations in which the decisions and actions of two or more individuals (or firms, political 
parties, countries) are interdependent. The foundational assumption is that decision-makers are rational and reason 
strategically—meaning their reasoning takes into account their knowledge and expectations of other decision-
makers. While originally developed for economics, Game Theory has proven essential for understanding conflict 
and cooperation in management, political science, law, biology, and recently, in the design of agentic AI systems.

Methodology:

Delivered through conceptual lectures, supported by Jupyter notebook model demos and in-class game sessions.

 Course Project: This creates hands-on experience in identifying game-like problem situations, creating an

evolutionary game model for them, and using the model to devise remedial measures. 
 Logistics: Students must enrol in Google Classroom to access course materials and instructions. All

communication will occur via this platform. 
 Technical Workflow: Project submissions must be Jupyter notebooks coded in Python and executed using

Google Colab within the Google Classroom/Drive environment. 
Prerequisites:

 Essential: Comfort with logical reasoning, basic probability, and coding in Python. 
 Desirable: Some background in intermediate microeconomics. 
Evaluation plan:  
Grading will be calculated based on the following components:

 Group Project: 30% 
 Quizzes: 30% (Based on the best 3 out of 4 quizzes) 
 End-term Exam: 40% 
Course Outcomes:  
This course aims to provide a systematic introduction to the tools of game theory and its applications. By the end of 
the course, students will be able to create mathematical and computational models to understand:

 Market Dynamics: Competition among firms, Duopolies and oligopolies 
 Strategic Behaviour: Credible deterrence strategies, Benefits of being unpredictable, Negotiation and

bargaining, Strategic voting. 
 Information Economics: Eliciting information, Principal-agent games, Moral hazard in insurance,

Signaling games and advertising,  Adverse selection and the use of warranties. 
 Mechanism Design: Incentive design, Auction design, Product bundling. 
 Evolutionary Models: The evolution of cooperation and free-rider elimination. 
Students will demonstrate these outcomes by developing their own evolutionary game models for specific situations, 
documented as Jupyter notebooks.
