## Main Content

HM116: Principles of Economics (Winter 2026) 
 
Instructors: Parul Gupta (Room 1402, FB-4), Cyril Jos (Room 4206, FB-4) 
 
Course Placement: B.Tech (ICT), Semester IV 
 
Course format: 3-0-0 (3 lecture hours) 
 
Course content: This is a core humanities/social sciences course which includes elements of 
both microeconomics and macroeconomics. It aims to develop an understanding of basic 
concepts of resource allocation, opportunity costs and market failure. Students would also get 
an introduction to macroeconomic concepts such as national income accounting, unemployment 
and inflation. References to monetary, fiscal, and foreign exchange rate policies will help 
students gain a greater understanding of the role of central banks and governments in the 
economy. 
 
 
Textbooks and reference books:

Mankiw, N. G. (2016). Principles of microeconomics (8th ed.). Cengage Learning Custom

Publishing. (any recent edition may be used)

The CORE Econ Team (2023). The Economy 2.0: Microeconomics. Open access e-text

available at https://core-econ.org/the-economy/

Blanchard, O., & Johnson, D. R. (2012). Macroeconomics (6th ed.). Pearson.

Dornbusch, R., Fischer, S., & Startz, R. (2014). Macroeconomics (12th ed.). McGraw-Hill

Education.

Course Outcomes

After completion of the course, students should be able to:

● Identify and distinguish between real-world examples of different types of markets,

including monopoly, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, and perfect competition.

● Analyse market efficiencies, consumer surplus, producer surplus and social welfare in

economic markets.

● Apply macroeconomics principles to estimate the welfare of countries, including the

determination of GDP, standard of living and unemployment rates.
