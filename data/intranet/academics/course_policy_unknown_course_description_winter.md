Course Description  
 
How do nature and environment figure in both our ordinary and scientific 
discourses? The term environment mostly refers to the things which remain outside 
the human and social sphere, often hiding the complex relations of dependence and 
contiguity that define the human-environment nexus. Ecology is the field which 
investigates this nexus. In this course, we will study the main themes and ideas of 
the ecological debate, which intersects with hard sciences, technology and social 
sciences at the same time. This debate is roughly 150 years old and has 
contributions from across the board, from zoologists and geologists to poets and 
activists.

This course is divided into four parts.

•         Lectures

•         Thought Experiments and Case Studies

•         Films/Documentaries

•         Research Report along with the viva

Main Themes and Topics:

1. The Idea of Nature: From Greeks to Modern Times (Hadot)

a. Politics of Nature (Latour) 
2. The Logic of Totemic Classification   
3. Environmentalism and the Rise of Ecological Thought

2. Biophilia and Consilence – Wilson 
3. Birth of Ecological Economics – Georgescu Roegen 
4. Buddhist Economics-E. F. Schumacher  
5. Gaya Hypothesis– Lynn Margulis and Jame Lovelock 
6. Gandhi’s Environmentalism  
7. Ecological Thought – India (Patrick Geddes and Lewis Mumford)

2. Sustainability and Ecological Security. Reading Brundtland. 
3. Risk: Ulrich Beck and Beyond – (Mary Douglas, Beck and Scott Lash)
