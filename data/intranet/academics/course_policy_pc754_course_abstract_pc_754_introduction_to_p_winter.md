COURSE FILE

PC754: Introduction to Photography

Credit Structure: 1-0-4-3

Program: MDes (Communication Design)

Year: 1

Semester: 2

Duration: 13 Days (Full Day – Lecture + Studio)

Timing: 9 AM – 6 PM

Course Instructor: Anirban Dutta Gupta

Couse Placement: Core course for MDes (CD) Semester 2. This is a specialised course in

design and is aimed at integrating inputs provided in previous courses in semester 1 and 2

into a ‘multimedia’ matrix and orient the students to its multimodal application.

Course Format: Module based. The course is taught as a combination of Lectures and Studio

sessions. Lectures introduce concepts, which are then analysed and explored via studio

sessions and assignments. Lecture and studio is integrated and in certain cases can overlap.

Teaching is through hand-on learning and through learning-by-doing and via real-time

demonstrations and guidance by faculty members. Students will be expected to conduct

fieldwork.

Course Objective and Content:

The Photography course will aim to provide a holistic understanding of photography as a

subject of historical study, a medium of capturing the world and as a communication tool. A

variety of photographic approaches, both current and historical, will be explored to allow

you to understand the medium in its multiple representative forms.

The course will introduce ‘why’ and the ‘how’ of photography – with ‘why’ covering the

creative and communication aspect of photography, while the ‘how’ will define the technical

aspect of achieving it.

The technical and creative development of photography through time will broadly cover the

mechanics of generating an image and the function of the camera using the DSLR as the

representative device. The focus will be on the digital medium of photography against the

more traditional chemical medium, and its subsequent image manipulation using software’s

like Photoshop.

Course Abstract:

Photography, in simple terms, is the method of recording images by the action of light or

similar related radiation on a material or device specially designed to be sensitive to it.

The term ‘Photography’ comes from the word Photos=Light and graphein=to draw and was

coined by John F W Herschel in 1839. By that count, the world of photography is little more

than 180 years old. In the past century and half -  technology, science and art of

photography has expanded dramatically. However, the concept of photography is even older

than this. In 1000 AD Ibn Al-Haytham invented the pinhole camera - the Camera Obscura.

Interestingly the law of optics was observed even earlier by Aristotle in 330 BC. Therefore,

one can safely say that it is more than 2 millennia of quest that has led us into today’s world

of photography.

The first ‘photograph’ was taken in 1827 by Joseph Niepce. This is a defining moment as

before this the Camera Obscura was for observation and drawing, for example, by the

renaissance painters who used it for accurate reference of perspective. Niepce was the first

one to actually record the event on an engraving plate coated in bitumen. This was the

beginning of experimentation on chemical photosensitive agents eventually leading to the

photographic camera and film.

From its beginning as a method of observation till today - photography is seen as a medium

of expression as well as a science of documentation. Fields of photography extends from

photography as an art form, photography as a product, photography as documentation,

photography as evidence, photography as science and photography as experiment.

This module on ‘Introduction to Photography’ will explore certain key aspects of

photography and build on previously learnt skillsets to relook framing an image using the

camera - through observation, content, context, composition, colour, and light -  primarily to

understand and build an aesthetic sensibility. The students will learn to train the eye for a

perfect composition through the viewfinder and learn to effectively capture expressive

moments with accuracy and aesthetics to develop a sense of storytelling through still

images.

In parallel, this module will also introduce the mechanics of taking a photograph. The digital

camera, its various settings and its interplay, usage of light and image correction and

manipulation, the role of the exposure triangle. The objective would be to not just provide

aesthetic input but to also provide the technical inputs – concept of aperture, ISO, shutter

speed, depth of field – so that what is imagined and seen can be effectively captured.

The students will also be exposed to Adobe Photoshop for post-production processing of

images.

At the end of the course the students will put up an exhibition of their selected work for the

community to see.

Students will be given various assignments through which they will explore the concepts

discussed in class.

Readings will be given to the students which they are expected to complete during the

course. The learnings from this course will be continued and built upon in the Filmmaking

module.
