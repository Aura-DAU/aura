## Main Content

SC475 – Time Series Analysis

Course Placement: Time Series Analysis is an elective course offered to third and fourth-year students of 
the B.Tech  program. 
 
Course format:  3-hour lecture every week

Prerequisites: A basic course in statistics and probability, Linear Algebra, Calculus, and either Nonlinear 
Dynamics or Modelling and Simulation. Python, R or Matlab programming skills. 
 
Course content: This course introduces students to the essential tools and concepts in time series analysis, 
covering both time and frequency domains. Time series typically arise in many processes that require 
consecutive measurements of quantities. Understanding the basic structure and information of a time series 
requires concepts often used in signal and image processing, statistics, dynamical systems, and statistical 
physics. The course aims to develop a working foundation at the undergraduate level that enables systematic 
analysis of time series arising in various disciplines. The course primarily focuses on traditional statistical 
methods used in time series analysis.  In addition, it aims to expose students to modern challenges and 
selected nonlinear methods that are used extensively in the research literature.  We will primarily focus on 
EEG and financial time series to develop a more hands-on understanding of the concepts discussed in class.

 
Exam (60%): Mid-semester (30) + Final (30) 
 
Project (40%): The Project will allow applying the learning in the course to a real data set. A 
relevant published paper will have to be identified in the first month of the semester. Students will 
reproduce the key results in the paper and then further analyze the data set. Group projects are 
encouraged. 
 
Attendance Policy: A minimum of 75% attendance (of all lectures in which attendance is taken) is 
mandatory. There will be a grade point penalty for students who do not meet the minimum 
attendance requirement.  
 
AI Policy: Use of AI is permitted for the course project, but all submitted work must be genuinely 
produced and owned by the students. Each group must be able to explain every detail of their 
project independently and without relying on AI during evaluation.
