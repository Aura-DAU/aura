### 1. Increment in Seats - Student Placement Cell

Nature

### Voting Agenda

Proposed By

Student Placement Cell (SPC)

Proposal

SPC proposed an increment of two seats through open selection:

• One seat - open to BTech First Year students.

• One seat - open to MSc students, with preference given to MSc IT candidates first,

followed by MSc DS.

### Discussion

Point raised:
 Whether the seat increment was actually necessary, given the existing

committee size.

SPC’s response: Current working hours of SPC members during the placement drive are

at saturation. Two additional members would provide meaningful operational relief and

allow the committee to function more efficiently during peak placement season.

### Point raised (on the First Year seat):
 Why should a first-year student be given a

seat when they will not be participating in the placement drive itself?

SPC’s response: Inducting a first-year student is a deliberate long-term investment. Since

they are not directly involved in the drive, they would observe and learn the operations

from the ground up. Should they wish to continue in SPC in subsequent years, this early

exposure would mean they are significantly better prepared to take on responsibilities.

No further points were raised.

### Voting Result

Requirement:
 66% (supermajority, as this is a seat-increment agenda)

Result: PASSED - 86.1% votes in favour

### 2. Co-Curricular Activities (CCA) Course

Nature

### Non-Voting Discussion Agenda

Proposed By

Academic Committee (Acad)

Proposal

Acad proposed the following credit/attendance framework for the Co-Curricular Activities

course. Attendance units can be earned through participation in the events listed below:

Event Attendance Units

Hackathons 1 (participation) / 5 (win)

### GSOC, ICPC 5

RoboFest, Datathon 5

Public Lecture Not mandatory

Club Events 1 per event attended

Discussion - Hackathon Attendance Tiers

Point raised: Participating in a hackathon is easy, and awarding 1 attendance unit

for mere participation could be gamed as nearly every student could claim it without

meaningful effort.

### Outcome after deliberation:
 Acad consulted with the house and reached a consensus

to introduce a tiered attendance structure specifically for hackathons, recognising different

levels of engagement:

• 1 attendance unit - for submitting an ideation/project proposal.

• 3 attendance units - for clearing the mid-evaluation stage. This will be verified and

certified.

• 5 attendance units - for winning the hackathon.

Discussion - Weekend vs.

### Weekday Scheduling of CCA Events

In favour of weekends:
 Students pointed out that weekdays are hectic due to labs

and scheduled coursework, making it difficult to meaningfully participate in co-curricular

events. Weekends would allow for better engagement and less fatigue.

### Against weekends:
 Others felt strongly that weekends are personal time - to unwind,

socialise outside of campus, and pursue their own interests - and that mandating CCA

events on weekends would encroach on that.

### Outcome:
 No consensus was reached. The decision on event scheduling is left to the

discretion of individual clubs and committees, who may choose the timing that best suits

their members and audience.

### 3. Formation of New Club - eSports Social

Nature

### Voting Agenda

Proposed By

Shivam Ramoliya

Proposal

The proposers highlighted a growing sense of isolated and fragmented social dynamics on

campus. The club was intentionally named eSports Social to emphasise its core purpose:

to create a space for students to connect with their peers through gaming, rather than to

build a competitive team.

### Discussion

Point raised:
 There is no need to formalise such a community. A simple group chat

would serve the same social bonding purpose, without the overhead of a formal club

structure.

Proposers’ response: Formal recognition would allow the community to secure a budget

and book campus spaces, enabling organised events - something an informal chat group

cannot facilitate.

### Counter-argument:
 The moment the group gains formal structure, a budget, and

organisedevents, itwouldinevitablyshiftitscharacterfromasocialspacetoacompetitive

one, which defeats the stated purpose.

The proposers were unable to resolve this concern to the satisfaction of a majority of the

house.

### Voting Result

Requirement:
 50% (simple majority)

Result: FAILED - 39.8% votes in favour

### 4. Formation of New Club - Fashion Club

Nature

### Voting Agenda

Proposed By

Dhvanit Shah

Proposal

The proposers noted that the university draws students from diverse backgrounds, many of

whom are passionate about fashion. The proposed club aimed to build a fashion culture on

campus. Intended activities include, but are not limited to:

• Semester-long fashion catalogue design.

• Collaborations with other clubs for designing merchandise.

• Campus-wide events to encourage and celebrate fashion as a creative discipline.

The proposers also noted significant interest from students in the first year, as well as

Masters programmes.

### Discussion

Feasibility concern:
 Whether there is a sufficient number of genuinely interested

students to sustain a club of this nature.

Proposers’ response: There is strong interest from a considerable number of students,

and the proposers expressed confidence in the club’s ability to attract members.

### Suggestion:
 The club should be required to conduct one or two events before being

formally admitted into the SBG, to demonstrate viability.

Proposers’ response: The proposers respectfully pushed back on this. Building a fashion

culture from scratch on a technical university campus is a gradual process, and imposing a

fixed event deadline before recognition would place undue pressure on a nascent initiative.

They requested that the house extend patience and allow the club the space to grow

organically.

The house accepted this reasoning. No prior-event requirement was imposed.

### Voting Result

Requirement:
 50% (simple majority)

Result: PASSED - 64.8% votes in favour

### 5. Amendments to the SBG Constitution

Nature

### Voting Agenda

Proposed By

SBG Core

Overview

SBG Core proposed a set of amendments to the SBG Constitution to codify procedures that

were either informal, ambiguous, or not previously documented. The proposed amendments

are detailed below.

Resignation Procedure

The resignation procedure is formally defined as follows:

• The resigning member must send an email to their Faculty Mentor and their Committee,

with the Dean of Students and all SBG Core members copied, informing them of the

decision.

• Following the resignation, the vacant seat will be filled through a selection process.

• Ifamemberleavesunexpectedly(withoutfollowingtheformalprocedure), thecommittee

retains the authority to fill the vacant seat through selection until the next General

Elections. This was the existing practice, but had not been documented in writing.

Impeachment Procedure

A formal impeachment procedure is introduced as follows:

1. Any committee member may initiate an impeachment motion by writing to the Com-mittee Convener, with SBG copied.

### 2. The Committee Convener will schedule a meeting at a time convenient to the entire

committee, and invite at least one SBG Core member to serve as mediator.

### 3. Initiation threshold:

 If at least 33% of members present at the meeting agree to

proceed, the formal impeachment process commences.

### 4. Proceedings:

 Members who raised the motion present their grievances first. The

member against whom the motion is raised then has a full right to defence.

### 5. Voting threshold:

 A vote is held. 66% of members present must vote in favour for

impeachment to be carried.

### 6. Quorum:

 At least 50% of the entire committee must be present for the meeting to

be valid.

### 7. Safeguard:

 If a meeting is deliberately scheduled at a time when certain members are

unavailable, those members may formally request SBG to reschedule the meeting.

Dean and SBG Veto on Club Formation

Given that clubs often cater to niche interests, it is possible that the General Student

Body may reject a club proposal not due to any legitimate objection, but simply out of

unfamiliarity with the niche.

### To address this:

• The Dean of Students and the SBG, after independently hearing out the club’s proposal,

may exercise a veto to override a negative vote from the General Student Body and

approve the club’s formation.

• The club must still be proposed at a General Meet first; the veto mechanism does not

bypass this step.

• A club formed through this veto mechanism holds all the same powers and bears all the

same responsibilities as any regularly approved club.

Club Dormancy and Dissolution

• If a club does not conduct any event for a period of one year, the club will be declared

dormant.

• If a dormant club remains inactive for a further six months, the club will be shut

down. The General Student Body will be notified accordingly.

Clarification on Quorum Percentages

It is hereby clarified that all quorum thresholds mentioned in the SBG Constitution -

including the 50% requirement for passing a general agenda and the 66% requirement for a

seat-increment agenda - refer to the percentage of members present at the meeting,

not of the total membership.

Advisory Role of Outgoing SBG Core

Outgoing SBG Core members may, at their own discretion, choose to remain in an advisory

capacity after their term ends. In this role, they may:

• Participate as observers in the day-to-day activities of the incoming SBG Core.

• Offer guidance and advice to the incoming SBG Core based on their experience.

This is entirely voluntary and carries no formal authority or responsibility.
