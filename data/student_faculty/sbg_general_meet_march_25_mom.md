Club voting rights: Henceforth, in general meetings, each club shall be entitled to one vote under the one-vote-per-club rule. Clubs will conduct internal voting to determine their stance, and in the event of a tie, the convener shall cast the deciding vote. The presence of at least one club member shall be deemed sufficient for participation.

### Conclusion:
 The agenda was approved with a majority vote in favor.

### Dormant Clubs Process:
 Clubs that fail to attend consecutive meetings, including C/DC meetings, general meetings, and budget sessions, must ensure the execution of at least one activity. Failure to do so will result in the club being classified as dormant, in which case the SBG will no longer assume responsibility for its activities.

### Conclusion:
 The agenda was approved with a majority vote in favor. Increase in internship period for MTech VLS| and WCSP: Will not be governed by the SBG.

### Increase in wifi quota:
 Will not be governed by the SBG.

### Impeachment process of SBG core:
 Proposal to modify the impeachment voting criteria from a two-thirds majority to approximately 51% was discussed. Concerns were raised that biases could exist on both sides, regardless of the required majority. It was also noted that the policy of discussion helps eliminate most partiality. However, reducing the threshold may allow larger committees to influence decisions unfairly, leading to potentially biased impeachment outcomes. The student who proposed this agenda maintained that the overall voting criteria should be lowered.

### Conclusion:
 The agenda was rejected as the majority voted against it.

### Felicitation of sports team members and captains:
 A proposal was made to enhance the recognition of team members and captains, as acknowledgment solely during Concours was deemed insufficient. Moving forward, the SBG will felicitate team captains by awarding them certificates signed by the Dean Students and the Sports Officer.

### Conclusion:
 The agenda was approved with a majority vote in favor.

### 7. Addition of one more selection seat for 1st year students in SPC:
 A proposal was made to increase the seat matrix of 1st year students as the assignment of CRs to five students accelerates their transition to becoming CRs for another company more quickly than in other batches. Additionally, concerns were raised about the increasing workload, particularly with the inclusion of the EVD batch, which will now have its summer internship drive. Discussions centered on whether it is necessary to include EVD and make it a mandatory requirement. Students also debated whether future expansions, such as the introduction of new branches, would lead to an increase in the seat matrix which was left to the increase in seat matrix based on workload.

### Conclusion:
 The agenda was approved with a majority vote in favor.

### 8. Proposal to increase a second year election seat in TSC:

 A proposal was made regarding the current seat matrix and the increasing number of events, highlighting the necessity of having an additional member to encourage greater interest among second-year students. During the discussion, the audience questioned why the committee required only an elected seat and suggested that the TSC include associative members throughout the year. After deliberation, it was decided that the agenda would now focus on the selection seat, while other aspects would remain unchanged.

### Conclusion:
 The agenda was approved with a majority vote in favor for the selection seat.

### 9. Formation of core + plans for future of Sambhav:

 A proposal was made to convert the organization into a club under the SBG. The primary objective of this transition is to establish stability and attract more student participation and volunteers. Detailed discussions were held regarding the implications of converting the organization into a club. It was proposed that, in the future, funding might be requested for large-scale events; however, the club would primarily rely on donations and would not receive an SBG budget allocation. Additionally, if Sambhav’s initiatives benefit the non-DA public, SBG funding will not be granted. Many participants suggested that Sambhav should remain an organization, emphasizing the need to structure and operate it as such rather than converting it into a club. It was ultimately decided to defer the final decision to the next general meeting to further discuss and clarify the agenda and structure.

### 10. Increase in 3 selection seats (2 general and 1 for third year) for AFC (Synapse):

A proposal was made highlighting the increased workload in the committee compared to the number of members currently present. It was emphasized that various departments, such as production and events, require additional members due to overlapping responsibilities and department clashes. While associate members are part of the committee, they face challenges such as limited responsibilities and lack of recognition within the administration. Many associate members have contributed at the same level as core members, leading to a request for their recognition through a core seat. However, the agenda will not proceed to voting, as the constitution states that "The request for seat increment can only be proposed once in an academic year." Since a similar request was already proposed in the last general meeting, the current proposal cannot be considered at this time.

### 1. Farewell/Freshers Committee consisting of 2nd and 3rd year students:
 Due to certain issues, the Freshers' event could not take place this time. However, there is a strong desire to continue this tradition rather than discontinue it. To ensure its continuity, a proposal was made to establish a dedicated club or committee responsible for organizing the event and the farewell, with its budget allocated from the SBG.

### 12, Introduction of yearbook by Press Club:
 The Press Club has proposed taking responsibility for creating the yearbook for future batches. They plan to collect data from students and professors while incorporating photos from various events and fests. Additionally, they aim to minimize costs for students while ensuring the successful execution of the yearbook.
