## 3. Increase in MTech Internship Period (6 → 12 months)

- **Description:** Currently, the MTech internship period is 6 months, which is comparatively short for specializations like VLSI & Embedded Systems and Wireless Communication & Signal Processing, putting DAU students at a disadvantage in gaining industry-level experience versus other institutions.
- **Target Audience:** MTech ICT students specializing in VLSI & Embedded Systems and Wireless Communication & Signal Processing, along with BTech Electronics and VLSI Design students.
- **Proposed Solution:** Change the MTech ICT curriculum to allow an 11-month internship period as an optional choice for students who want more industrial experience.

## 4. Increase of Wi-Fi Quota

- **Description:** The current Wi-Fi quota doesn't sufficiently meet students' daily requirements, especially in rooms affected by poor cellular network coverage, making college Wi-Fi their only internet source.
- **Target Audience:** Entire student body.
- **Proposed Solution:** Increase the daily Wi-Fi quota (e.g., to 40 GB download/day), or allow unused daily data to carry over on a monthly basis.

## 5. Revising the SBG Impeachment Process

- **Description:** An elected member with significant support/backing can make the impeachment process biased, since existing alliances create pressure that isn't accounted for. The current threshold requires a two-thirds majority of total votes for impeachment.
- **Target Audience:** Student Body and SBG.
- **Proposed Solution:** Revise the current criteria to require approximately 51% instead of a two-thirds majority, to make the process more equitable while still maintaining accountability.

## 6. Felicitation for Sports Team Members and Captains

Introduce an annual felicitation for all sports team members and captains, awarding certificates that acknowledge their contributions and leadership throughout the year — similar to the recognition already given to SBG members.

## 7. Student Placement Cell (SPC): Additional Second-Year Seat

Proposal to add one more second-year student seat to the Student Placement Cell via a selection process.

**Rationale:**
- First-year students are elected/selected to serve on SPC through their second year. They start out managing mock interviews alongside third-years (while the third-year batch and Masters students are on summer internships), coordinating with students and alumni, managing interviews for ~600 placement candidates, and handling a large pool of alumni interviewers.
- In the odd semester, SPC runs both placement and internship drives — coordinating with ~100 companies within 4 months, each assigned a CR from each year. Senior batches have 9 representatives each; the second-year batch has only 5, creating a heavier, more simultaneous CR workload for them.
- Second-years also handle drafting emails and off-campus opportunities during drives (roughly 20 emails per company on average) — time-intensive work. Since third-years, fourth-years, and Masters students are occupied with their own placements, second-years end up taking on more of the physical workload than seniors.
- By mid-October, fourth-year members start transitioning out to prepare the next batch, further increasing the load on remaining members.
- Through the even semester, SPC stays active — managing ongoing company engagements, preparing for the next placement cycle (PPTs, GDs, new brochures, research sheets), revising placement policies, arranging company sessions, and running guidance programs, often without senior involvement.
- With the EVD branch joining placement drives from next year, SPC's workload will increase further.
- The combination of heavy workload and limited second-year positions has been leading to demotivation, discouraging continued involvement in the following year.

## 8. Tech Support Committee (TSC): Additional Second-Year Seat

Proposal to increase a second-year seat (via selection). As college events have grown in both frequency and scale, the current Tech Support Committee often struggles to manage all technical tasks smoothly. An additional second-year member would improve efficiency, reduce workload on current members, and let the committee train junior members for future sustainability and skill transfer.
