### 1 The Election Commission

The Election Commission (“the EC”) is an independent and autonomous body, constituted by a set of students, responsible for organizing and conducting periodic, free and fair elections of the student representatives to be a part of the

DAU Student Body Government (“the SBG”) as and when it is required to do so.

The Election Commission is mentored by and reports directly to the Dean

(Students).

### 1.1 Roles And Responsibilities

1. To take complete charge of all aspects involved in organizing elections of student representatives -

(a) To the Student Body Government via direct elections.

(b) To the SBG Core Committee via indirect elections.

(c) To fill particular vacant posts in the event of untimely resignation(s) or impeachment(s) of previously elected SBG Core members.

2. To ensure that the election management is fair and transparent, so as to minimize the scope of any complaint of partiality from the side of any student(s).

3. To deal with any grievances that are raised with regard to actions taken during the election process by the EC or any other student(s) promptly.

4. To issue guidelines and the code of conduct to be followed by the student body during the elections.

5. To issue warnings for violations of the Election by-laws during the period when the Code of Conduct is in effect.

6. To issue a complete schedule of elections giving therein the date and time for filing nominations, scrutiny of nominations, the authority with whom nominations are to be filed, the date for withdrawal of nomination and the date and time of holding the elections and other instructions, etc., if any.

### 1.2 Composition Of the Election Commission

A. Election Commissioners

1. The EC shall consist of four (4) Election Commissioners.

2. The composition shall include:

a. One student representative from each participating

Undergraduate (UG) batch b. One collective representative from the Postgraduate (PG)

batches.

3. The senior-most Election Commissioner, determined first by experience in the EC and thereafter by batch seniority, shall serve as the Chief

Election Commissioner (CEC).

4. In the absence of the CEC, the CEC may designate a member of the EC

to act as the Chief Election Commissioner for the concerned election(s)

during such period of absence.

5. The enforcement of the roles and responsibilities of the EC shall vest primarily with the Election Commissioners under the leadership of the

Chief Election Commissioner.

B. Election Officers

1. The EC may request the Dean (Students) to appoint additional members to the EC in the capacity of Election Officers.

2. At no time shall more than two (2) Election Officers serve simultaneously.

3. An Election Officer may be appointed from any participating UG or PG

batch (BTech 1st/2nd/3rd year or PG 1st year).

4. Responsibilities

Election Officers shall assist the Election Commissioners in the discharge of their functions, including but not limited to the conduct of elections, and shall monitor compliance with the Code of Conduct, reporting any violations to the Election Commissioners for appropriate action.

5. Limitation of Powers

1. Election Officers shall exercise such authority as is expressly delegated to them. They shall contribute to deliberations and implementation processes; however, decisions in matters relating to penalties, issuance of binding orders, and authoritative interpretation of rules shall rest with the Election

Commissioners.

2. Election Officers shall not be part of the decision-making authority of the Election Commission.

3. In urgent circumstances requiring immediate action in the larger interest of the EC, an Election Officer may take provisional decisions, provided that:

A. Such action is reported to the Chief Election

Commissioner at the earliest possible time; and

B. The Election Commissioners review the action and issue a final decision, which may uphold, modify, or overturn the same.

4. Election Officers shall be equally bound as Election

Commissioners to maintain strict confidentiality with respect to all internal matters of the Election Commission. This obligation shall continue to apply even after the completion or termination of their tenure.

### 1.3 Eligibility Criteria for The Post of The

Election Commissioner/Election Officer

1. The student should not have been placed on Academic Probation.

2. The student should not have any active backlogs (pending ‘F’ grades) at the time of application.

3. The student should not be placed on disciplinary probation at the time of application (or anytime in the past) by the institute. Note: If a currently serving Election Commissioner is put on Academic probation or is awarded an ‘F’ grade in any course, he/she must leave the post, and a new Election Commissioner will be selected for the respective batch.

If a currently serving Election Officer is put on Academic probation or is awarded an ‘F’ grade in any course, he/she must leave the post.

### 1.4 Appointment Of the Election

Commissioner/Election Officer

1. Annually, shortly after the commencement of the Winter Semester, the EC

will open applications for the Election Commissioner (batch representative)

of the BTech 1st year, and during the Autumn Semester, the Election

Commissioner (batch representative) of the PG Batches from the PG 1st year.

2. EC may, whenever required in the interest of its functioning, invite applications for the position of Election Officer, subject to the ratification of the Dean (Students).

3. The selection process taking place would involve the EC deciding in tandem with the Dean (Students) to appoint the new Election

Commissioner/Election Officer (s).

4. The candidates who apply for the post are to be screened and shortlisted in a preliminary selection process by the existing members of the EC, who are then recommended to the Dean (Students).

5. The final selection among the filtered candidates takes place via an interview with the Dean (Students), if required.

6. All members of the EC are appointed by and report to the Dean (Students), who is also the Faculty Mentor of the Election Commission.

7. In the event that the EC does not have any female representative, the EC can request the Dean (Students) to nominate an ad-hoc female representative to the EC.

### 1.5 Tenure Of the Election

Commissioner/Election Officer

1. Every academic year, Election Commissioners from the UG Batches shall continue to serve as the batch representatives for their respective batches

2. The Election Commissioner from the PG Batches has a term till the end of his/her third semester.

3. In case of any contingency, the Election Commissioner from the PG Batches

(if available) can be asked to return to his post to help the EC carry out their functions in a smooth and orderly manner.

4. The tenure of the Election Officer is one year, which can be extended with the ratification of the Dean (Students).

5. The tenure of the ad hoc female representative is limited to one year, or until the EC has a female representative as a member, whichever is later.

### 1.6 Resignation Of the Election

Commissioner/Election Officer

1. Any member of the EC may resign at any time he/she wishes to do so, barring the time period two weeks prior to any upcoming Elections.

2. Any member of the EC may resign by writing under his hand, to the Chief

Election Commissioner, with a copy addressed to the Dean (Students).

3. The Chief Election Commissioner may resign by writing under his hand, directly addressed to the Dean (Students).

Note: An Election Commissioner/Election Officer who has resigned from the post is not barred from contesting for elections throughout the duration of tenure of his/her program.

### 1.7 Impeachment Of the Election

Commissioner/Election Officer

1. Any EC Member (Election Commissioner/ Election Officer) may be impeached by the Chief Election Commissioner with the ratification of the

Dean (Students).

2. The Chief Election Commissioner can be impeached by the Dean(Students).

Note: An Election Commissioner/Election Officer who is impeached from the post is not barred from contesting for elections throughout the duration of tenure of his/her program.

### 1.8 Voting By the Members of the EC

Each and every member of the EC (Election Commissioner/Election Officer) has a vote that may be cast during the General Elections. Each vote cast by the EC is exactly equivalent to one that is cast by any general member of the student body.

### 1.9 Members Of the EC In the Elections

To uphold the unbiased and democratic nature of the elections, every member of the EC (Election Commissioner/Election Officer) is strictly barred from taking part in any election process as a candidate, with no exceptions.

1.10 Autonomy Of the Election Commission

1. The EC is detached from the SBG with regard to carrying out its functions and fulfilling all its roles and responsibilities.

2. The EC is not bound to function under the instructions of, or report to any particular member(s) of the SBG on any matter with regard to the execution of its responsibilities.

3. At least one member of the EC will be present in all SBG General Meets with the agenda of engaging in healthy discussion on matters related to Elections and putting forward any concerns that the EC might have in relation to the current policies.

1.11 Discretionary Powers

The EC shall have total powers as far as the conduct of the elections is concerned.

This shall include the disqualification of candidates from contesting, of voters from voting, and even the interpretation of this document. This is conditional on the fact that they (the EC) take such a decision by a simple majority among themselves and give reasons for the same. All decisions of the Election

Commission will be final and binding on the student(s) concerned. The EC is obliged to inform the student community via mail regarding any major decision being taken or change that has been put into effect as soon as possible.

### 2 The General Elections

### 2.1 Eligibility To Vote

The following group of students will take part in the process as voters and constitute the electoral college for the General Elections of the SBG -

1. All 1st, 2nd and 3rd year Under-Graduate students shall vote to elect representatives to the SBG from their respective batches.

2. All 1st-year PG students shall vote to elect representatives to the SBG from the PG students.

3. All the Doctoral students shall vote to elect representatives among themselves and/or PG.

4. All residents of their respective floors can vote while choosing the Hostel

Management Committee (HMC) representative of their floor, irrespective of their batch and year.

5. Students who have been placed under any form of disciplinary probation shall vote to elect students from the batch that they will graduate with.

6. Students who have been placed under any form of disciplinary probation shall vote to elect HMC representatives of their respective floor, if they are living in the HOR themselves.

### 2.2 Eligibility To Contest

A candidate can contest for a post through general elections if he/she satisfies the following criteria -

1. The candidate is eligible to vote in the General Elections in accordance with the criteria stated above.

2. The candidate has not been placed under academic probation and has no active backlogs.

3. The candidate has not been placed under disciplinary probation.

4. A candidate is not allowed to contest for the post of the HMC representative of any floor other than the one on which he/she resides.

5. To contest for the post of the Academic Committee representative, the candidate must satisfy -

• Undergraduate candidates must have a CPI ≥ 7.0

• Postgraduate candidates must have a CPI ≥ 8.0

Note: In the event that the person does not have a CPI for any reason, he/she is not allowed to contest for elections

6. A person can contest elections for the next term while holding a post in the current term, as long as he/she satisfies the respective eligibility criteria for contesting in the post at the time of filing his/her nomination.

7. A person cannot hold more than one post during the same term. Therefore, a person can stand for only one post in the election.

### 2.3 Outline

The process of the general elections broadly includes the following events, mentioned in the chronological order in which they are to be conducted over the course of the Election period –

1. Opening Of Nominations

2. Address By the Previous Committee

3. Nomination Withdrawal Period

4. Janta Ki Adalat

5. Polling Day

### 2.4 Candidate Manifesto

It is mandatory for every candidate to submit a manifesto to the EC, which will be shown in the ‘Janta Ki Adalat’ by the EC. Some general guidelines regarding the format and submission of the candidate manifesto -

1. Every candidate is required to submit the manifesto (soft copy) before the end of the Withdrawal Period via his/her institute email. As an exception to this rule, unopposed candidates are not required to submit a manifesto.

2. The length of the document is restricted to one page of text (around 200

words) without the use of any images.

3. The document must clearly state the name and the Student Id of the candidate, and will be considered invalid otherwise.

4. The document should not contain any hate speech, negative propaganda or offensive/suggestive material.

5. The EC reserves the right to redact any part of the manifesto if it violates the previous clause.

6. The candidature of a student is subject to cancellation upon failure to submit his/her manifesto.

### 2.5 Opening Of Nominations

1. The opening of nominations serves as the formal announcement of the commencement of the General Elections.

2. The dates of the opening of nominations of the general elections are to be solely decided by the EC, with suggestion from the SBG Core.

3. The nomination period, under any circumstances should not be lesser than a duration of 2 days.

4. The nomination period, under any circumstances should not exceed a duration of 4 days. On account of unseen circumstances, the period may be extended in consultation with the Dean (Students).

5. Any candidate who wishes to stand for elections can nominate himself/herself through the process outlined by the EC when nominations are opened.

6. The Code of Conduct shall be put into effect from the day of opening of nominations, till the day of announcement of results. A partial code of conduct can be enforced by the EC as decided by the EC in consultation with the Dean (Students).

7. No withdrawals of nomination in oral or written form shall be accepted during the nomination period.

8. The nomination period must not end before the completion of the Address by the Previous Committees.

### 2.6 Address By the Previous Committees

1. The Address by the Previous Committees is the first formal event of the

General Elections. It directly follows the opening of nominations.

2. The event aims to direct one’s attention to three particular matters -

• To inform the student community about the roles and responsibilities of a particular committee member.

• To provide the student community with an informed opinion on agendas taken up by a particular committee in the recent past.

• To help them gain knowledge about the current issues a committee is facing and prospective agendas that are to be taken up by the particular committee in the near future.

3. The event will give a platform for a few Committee Members (Preferably

Committee Heads) to address the candidates and the student community as a whole about affairs in accordance with the objectives of the event as stated in the previous point.

4. The candidates, irrespective of their batch, who are contesting for a particular committee, and were not elected or selected members of that committee in the previous tenure, must attend the addressal. In the case of absence, their candidature is subject to cancellation if they fail to produce proof to the EC

(in the form of an email) that exempts them from the same. However, all other candidates are strongly advised to attend the address.

5. The rest of the student community is encouraged to attend the event to help them vote more responsibly.

6. It should be ensured that the Address by Previous Committees is completed a few hours prior to the closing of the nomination period.

Note: The candidates who turn in their nominations after the Address by the

Previous Committees has been completed are bound by the same rules as the candidates who have submitted their forms previously. In particular, forms submitted after the completion of the Address by candidates contesting for a committee of which they were not elected/selected members during the previous tenure shall be considered valid only if the student concerned has attended the

Address or has been granted an exemption by the Election Commission.

### 2.7 Nomination Withdrawal Period

1. The Nomination Withdrawal Period is a short-duration window that allows any candidates who have previously nominated themselves to withdraw their candidatures and remove themselves from the election process.

2. Any candidate who wishes to withdraw his/her nomination can submit an application for the same in the manner outlined by the EC at the beginning of the Nomination Withdrawal Deadline.

3. Once the withdrawal form is submitted, a candidate is not allowed to take it back and reinstate his/her candidature.

4. The Nomination Withdrawal Period, under any circumstances should not be less than a duration of 24 working hours.

5. The Nomination Withdrawal Period, under any circumstances cannot exceed a duration of 48 working hours.

6. It must be ensured by the EC that there is no overlap between the

Nomination Filing and Nomination Withdrawal Period.

### 2.8 Janta Ki Adalat

1. The Janta Ki Adalat serves as a formal platform for showcasing a candidate’s uniqueness in front of the student community.

2. The event would involve each candidate being given dedicated floor time to state their credentials and present his/her views and reasons to support his/her nomination to the respective committee.

3. The event can also serve as a formal platform for candidates to talk about his/her personal views on the shortcomings and issues faced with regard to the workings of the committee and how he/she plans to tackle those issues, if elected.

4. The dedicated floor time for every candidate is followed by a fixed duration of a one-to-one question/answer session with the candidates regarding their agendas, views and past experience.

5. The EC may declare a particular line of questioning as invalid, if it appears to be an attack on the personal character of the candidate.

6. The candidate may declare a particular line of questioning as invalid and refrain from answering questions related to that if it appears to be a personal attack on him/her.

7. It is mandatory for every candidate to attend the Janta Ki Adalat. As an exception to this point, when candidates are contesting unopposed, they need not be present during this event.

8. The rest of the student community is encouraged to attend the event to help them vote more responsibly.

9. The candidature of those, who are absent from the event is subject to cancellation if they fail to produce proof from the EC (in form of a mail) that exempts them from the same.

### 2.9 Polling Day

1. Students must refrain from campaigning in the period of 24 hours prior to the start of Polling.

2. Students must refrain from campaigning in the vicinity of the polling stations.

### 3 SBG Core Elections

### 3.1 Elected Officials

The SBG Core Elections are conducted to elect officials to the SBG Core

Committee. The SBG Core Committee consists of the following four posts -

• SBG Convener

• SBG Deputy Convener

• SBG Treasurer

• SBG Secretary

### 3.2 Election Format

1. SBG Core Elections by nature, are indirect elections.

2. Only those students who are a part of committees are allowed to vote during the SBG Core Elections.

3. Unless specified, the SBG Core Elections are not closed and hence all students can attend the event if they wish to as spectators.

4. Any and every elected member of the SBG is eligible to contest for a post in the SBG Core.

5. A candidate is not allowed to contest for more than one post(s) in the SBG

Core Committee.

6. In the event that an SBG Core Committee post remains uncontested, re-elections must be conducted. The EC may refer the case to the

Dean(Students) if it feels the need to do so.

### 3.3 Election Process

1. The SBG Core Elections take place in close succession to the General

Elections, preferably on the same day.

2. Under no circumstances should there be a gap of duration greater than 24

working hours between the announcement of results of the General

Elections and the commencement of the SBG Core Elections.

3. Elections for all the four posts are to be conducted sequentially starting with the elections for the post of the SBG Secretary, followed by the elections for the post of the SBG Treasurer, SBG Deputy Convener and concluded by the elections for the post of the SBG Convener.

4. The SBG Core Elections closely mirrors the process of the General Election, albeit condensed to a short span of time.

5. The Election process to elect a member of the SBG Core Committee begins with a short address given by the current office bearers. The address must highlight the major roles and responsibilities of holding that particular office. Additionally, it may also shed light upon the challenges faced during the past tenure, qualities expected in an ideal office bearer and initiatives that are expected to be taken up by the next office bearer.

6. The address is followed by a short window of nomination. Any eligible candidate can nominate himself/herself by a simple show of hand.

7. After the closing of the nomination window, all the candidates are given dedicated individual floor time. In this duration, they are expected to formally introduce themselves to the voters and state their reasons and/or motives behind contesting for this particular post of the SBG Core

Committee. In addition, they may also highlight previous work experience, past incidents, character qualities that make them a perfect fit to hold that particular office and initiatives that they plan to take up in the coming tenure, if elected.

8. After the completion of the floor time dedicated to the candidates, all other students who are present can ask the candidates questions with regard to their previous work experience, initiatives and opinions on matters that might come up for discussion during their tenure.

9. Once the above procedure has been completed for all four posts of the SBG

Core Committee, the voting members of the SBG will vote for all four posts.

The voting will take place via Secret Ballot.

10. The Evaluation of the result is to be conducted using the same method used in the General Elections, but in addition to ordinary preferences, each voter can cast a None of the Above (NOTA) vote for a particular post if he/she feels that none of the contesting candidate(s) should hold the post.

11. In the event that result is in the favor of NOTA (tie or majority votes in favor of NOTA), re-elections shall be conducted within 36 working hours of declaration of the result.

12. The re-election process will be the same as the core election process, including the provision of NOTA. Candidates from the previous election remain eligible to participate. If the result remains in favor of NOTA even in the re-election, the seat will no longer be subject to further elections, and the Dean(Students) will assume the responsibility for selecting a candidate.

### 4 Autumn Semester Elections for Masters and

Ph.D. Batches

The following guidelines are applicable only to the elections which take place in the autumn semester to elect first year MSc/MTech/Ph.D. representatives to the committees -

1. The number of seats open for elections are as specified in the seat matrix specified in the SBG Constitution.

2. Any SBG member that is elected through the autumn semester election process shall be entitled to the same voting rights, and have equal say as any other elected SBG member in future events.

3. The dates of the Elections shall be decided solely by the EC, with suggestions from the concerned committees.

4. The election process will mirror the process of the General Election.

5. The Code Of Conduct shall be in effect for students of the concerned batches from the day the election nominations are declared open, until the announcements of results.

6. The tenure of members elected through these elections shall be:

● For the SPC - 1.5 years

● For all other committees – 1 year

### 5 By-Elections

By-Elections are to be conducted elections in the event when the incumbent resigns, is impeached or becomes ineligible to continue in office for any reason.

The following guidelines are with regard to these situations only.

1. Under no circumstances can a by-election be conducted before the EC

receives a resignation letter under the hand of the incumbent or intimation from the SBG Core regarding the impeachment or ineligibility to continue to hold office.

2. The decision of whether or not elections must take place to fill the vacant seat is to be taken by the EC, with suggestion from the SBG Core.

3. In the case that by-elections are to be conducted, the announcement for the same should be sent at least 24 working hours before and not more than 48

hours before the by-election process commences.

4. The Election Process for the By-Election is identical to that of the election for the post of a SBG Core Committee Member.

5. A Quorum of 75% of the voting members of the SBG is required for by-elections to be conducted.

### 6 Voting System

### 6.1 Preferential Voting

The voting system that we follow is that of a SINGLE TRANSFERABLE VOTE

in which if the voter’s first choice gets eliminated, the vote gets transferred to the next competing candidate according to the voter's stated order of preference.

### 6.2 Evaluation Algorithm

Let the number of seats for a committee be n and the total number of candidates contesting for these n positions be x. Each voter will be allowed to give max (2, min (x-n-1, 6)) preferences, specifying the order of preference among the candidates voted for. Evaluation of results will be done as follows:

All the x candidates are under consideration at the beginning of the evaluation. The evaluation process is iterative in nature, and each iteration removes one candidate from consideration effectively reducing the number of candidates by one. This iterative loop continues till there are only n candidates remaining, who are then declared to be winners and go on to fill the n committee positions.

To decide the candidate which will be removed from consideration, a count of the most significant preference of votes is taken, the candidate receiving the least number of votes is the one who is removed from consideration. In the event that two candidates share the same number of votes, Tie-breakers are implemented as discussed in the next section.

Once a certain candidate is taken out of consideration, the subsequent preferences of his votes are redistributed among other candidates contesting in the process for the next iteration. This step helps represent everyone’s opinion in the selection process.

### 6.3 Tie-Breakers

In case of tie between two or more candidates:

1. Counting cross-preferences: Look at just the next preference of those tied candidates and distribute those votes among tied candidates only. The one with the least number of cross preference votes, gets eliminated.

2. After the cross preferential votes are taken into consideration, if the tie still persists, then

(a) If they are from different batches, then seniority shall be taken into con- sideration. The junior-most candidate gets eliminated. A student is considered senior to another on the basis of time spent at the institute.

(b) If all candidates are of equal seniority, the one with the lowest CPI gets eliminated.

(c) If the tie still persists after the above clause, then one of the candidates shall be eliminated using the process of random selection.

### 6.4 Voting Method

1. The voting for the General Elections will be carried out via the e-voting soft-ware. As an exception to this, in some cases, such as the HMC Elections where the number of votes per post are low, a paper-based secret ballot process may be used.

2. The voting for the Autumn Semester M.Sc. Elections will take place via e-voting only.

3. The voting for all other elections (SBG Core, By-Elections, etc.) will take place via Secret Ballot only, unless stated differently beforehand.

### 7 Code Of Conduct

This Code of Conduct is a collection of some expected behaviors and restrictions that must be followed by all candidates standing in the elections. Violation of these norms will attract punitive actions from the election officers ranging from cancellation of candidature to prohibition from participating in future elections.

1. No candidate shall indulge in any activity which may aggravate or create mutual hatred or cause tension between different castes and communities religious, linguistic, etc.

2. Criticism of other candidates, when made, shall be confined to their policies and programs. Candidates shall refrain from criticism of all aspects of private life, not connected with public activities. Criticism of other candidates based on unverified allegations or distortion must be avoided.

3. There shall be no appeal to caste, regional and communal feelings for securing votes. Lecture theatres, OAT or any other place on the college campus shall not be used as a forum for election propaganda. No rallies and public meetings shall be held. A proper platform for putting a candidate's uniqueness will be provided by the election commission.

4. All candidates must scrupulously avoid all activities which are “corrupt practices”, such as bribing of voters, intimidation of voters, impersonation of voters. Candidates are asked not to arrange any parties or treats as long as the code of conduct is in effect.

5. The right of every individual for peaceful and undisturbed personal-life shall be respected, however much the candidates may resent that individual’s opinions or activities.

6. No candidate shall permit himself/herself or his/her supporters to make use of any college building, compound wall etc., for suspending banners, pasting notices, writing slogans etc.

7. Candidates shall ensure that their supporters do not indulge in any kind of fights with the other candidates and their supporters.

8. Use of electronic mail/broadcasts for election propaganda is strictly prohibited. Use of any form of technology to cross question the voter regarding their preference of voting and indulging in any kind of vote barter is strictly prohibited.

9. No student activity, event, announcement, monetary distribution, thanksgiving ceremony or organized gathering of any nature shall be scheduled, conducted, or publicly notified while the Code of Conduct is in effect. Exceptions may be granted only in extraordinary circumstances, subject to prior approval of the Election Commission.

10. No person shall directly or indirectly compel, coerce, or exert undue pressure upon any student to file nomination, withdraw candidature, or refrain from filing nomination for any position against their free will.

11. Election or your candidature cannot be given as an excuse to miss classes, lectures or labs.

12. No candidate or voter shall indulge in any official campaigning during the 24 hours prior to the commencement of polling.

Note: EC can enforce the code of conduct (partially or as a whole) any time near the commencement of the election, with Dean (Students)’s approval. It should be communicated to the student community via official channels. Action against violation of code of conduct during partial enforcement of code of conduct is at the discretion of EC.

### 8 Referendums

1. A referendum shall be initiated by the EC if it receives a copy of a signed memorandum addressed to the SBG Convener with signatures of any of the following entities -

• ≥ half of the total strength of the SBG

• ≥ one-third of the total strength of the student community.

2. The EC shall conduct a referendum within 48 hours of receipt of the memorandum.

3. A referendum shall be considered valid if and only if more than two-third of the total student community cast their vote.

4. A referendum shall be considered as successful only if a simple majority of these votes is observed to be in favor of the motion.

5. In the case of Halls Of Residences, a referendum shall be initiated only if more than half of the total strength of the hostel asks for it through a signed memorandum addressed to the SBG Convener, with a copy sent to the EC

and the Warden of the respective HOR.

6. An HOR referendum shall be considered valid if and only if more than two-thirds of the total strength of the hostel cast their vote.

7. A HOR referendum shall be accepted as successful only if a simple majority of votes is observed in the favor of the motion.

### 9 Grievances Committee

1. The Grievances Committee shall consist of the Dean (Students) and EC.

(May include student representatives of the Gender Cell or DAC). It shall look into all the complaints regarding the conduct of the elections and take appropriate decisions. The decisions of this Committee shall be final.

2. In pursuit of its duties, the Grievance Committee may take action against violators of any aspect of the Code Of Conduct or the rulings of the

Grievance Committee.

3. All complaints regarding the election should be submitted to the Grievances

Committee (by writing to the EC) within 24 hours of the declaration of the results.

4. Any complaints with regard to the events taking place during the course of the General Elections, prior to the Polling Day, must be submitted as promptly as possible to the Grievance Committee. (by writing to the EC)

5. In the event that a complaint is to be submitted against the EC, it must be addressed to the Dean (Students) and a copy must be sent to the EC.

6. Members of the Grievance cell are prohibited from filing complaints.

7. The Grievance Committee may dismiss a complaint if –

● The complaint was not filed within the time frame prescribed above.

● The complaint fails to state a cause of action for which relief may be granted.

● If a complaint is not dismissed, then a hearing must be held. The

Grievance Committee shall inform, in writing, or via e-mail, the com-plaining party and all individuals or groups named in the complaint of the time and place of the hearing. The parties are not considered notified until they have received a copy of the complaint.

● In the event that the mail is against the EC, the Dean (Students) can dismiss the complaint upon his/her own discretion.

8. The EC shall be answerable to the SBG and the student community for actions taken by it regarding the complaints.

10 Amendments To the Constitution

1. Amendments to the constitution may be suggested by any student, committee or faculty, to the EC (via mail) with a copy addressed to the Dean

(Students).

2. The EC is obliged to consider every amendment suggested to it, but is not obliged to act upon each and every one of it.

3. The procedure for amendments to the constitution can occur at most once per semester.

4. In case of emergencies, any temporary change if required, can be made by the Dean (Students) in consultation with the EC. The temporary change holds till the next amendment meeting of the EC in which final decision over the issue can be taken.

5. Minor changes in the constitution can be done by the EC in consultation with the Dean (Students). The EC is responsible for announcing any such change(s) to the SBG in the upcoming General Meet and/or via email.

6. No amendments can be made in the period two-weeks prior to the opening of nominations for any election process.
