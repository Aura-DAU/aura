# Overview

Guidance page for parents of first-year students at Dhirubhai Ambani University (DAU, formerly DA-IICT), Gandhinagar, on the transition to university life. Page footer shows "Last Updated: 06-08-2026."

# Main Content

## 1. What to Expect
The first year is a big transition for students and families. From the moment students join, the university deals directly with them as adults — academically and administratively — not with their families. This is both a legal obligation and part of students' development into responsible citizens.

## 2. New Environment
Students fluctuate between excitement and apprehension as they adjust to campus life, increased academic workload, and being away from family. Anxiety about fitting in is common; teaching style, evaluation, faculty interaction, labs, and hostel living will all feel new.

## 3. Academic Expectations
Adjusting to DA-IICT's academic life is challenging mainly because success now requires self-initiative and self-discipline rather than external supervision. Support is available through professors, teaching assistants, and registrarial staff — but the onus to seek help is on the student.

**Advice for students:**
- Never miss a class.
- Buy at least one book per course (don't rely solely on e-books).
- Follow a daily routine of at least 4 hours of self-study.
- Eat properly and get sufficient calories on time.
- Stay regular in studies — students who slip in SPI/CPI in the first two semesters often struggle to recover confidence and catch up later.

## 4. Changes in Support Systems
Students' support systems shift — some rely on family, others build new networks with peers, professors, and advisors.

**Advice for parents:**
- Don't visit too frequently — it can distract from studies and social life.
- Don't bring students home every weekend, especially if within ~6 hours' drive — weekends are valuable study/project time.

## 5. Increased Freedom and Flexibility
Learning is largely self-directed; work isn't assigned daily/weekly, so students must track their own readings and deadlines — a shift from the structure of school.

## 6. New Independence, New Ideas
Exposure to diverse cultures, perspectives, and beliefs may lead students to question some of their own values. Families are encouraged to give them room to explore while trusting that core values and identity generally remain.
